Property taxes, flood coverage and summer energy use

Houston homebuyers often need to examine property taxes, homeowners insurance, community fees and exposure to flooding in addition to principal and interest. The total cost of ownership can differ sharply between properties that have similar listing prices.

Texas does not impose a personal state income tax, but that is not the same as having no housing-related taxes. Local property taxes and special district assessments can matter to a monthly housing budget. Verify the address-specific situation instead of applying a general statewide assumption.

Flood risk and flood insurance need separate investigation because homeowners coverage commonly does not replace flood coverage. For properties where such protection is relevant, ask insurers and the lender about requirements and costs. Enter those costs in a separate household budget if not covered by our basic mortgage input fields.

Long periods of warm weather can make cooling and utility costs important for renters and homeowners alike. Compare home size, efficiency and actual utility responsibilities before deciding what monthly payment feels comfortable.

Practical checklist for Houston

  • Check local property tax and district assessment information
  • Review flood exposure and separate flood insurance needs
  • Include air-conditioning and utility estimates
  • Ask whether the community charges HOA or other assessments
