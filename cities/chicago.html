Condo assessments, property taxes and the cost of four seasons

Chicago homebuyers may compare downtown condominium units, two-flats, townhomes and detached houses. Monthly association assessments for condos can be a meaningful part of the housing budget, particularly where buildings provide elevators, heat, common amenities or major shared maintenance.

Property tax bills are not uniform across Chicago neighborhoods or property types. Before committing to a purchase, review the property's actual tax history and any assessment information. Our tool asks for the annual tax amount instead of pretending a citywide percentage applies to all homes.

Winter heating and summer cooling can add to the monthly costs of owning or renting a home. Ask about the heating system, who is responsible for energy charges, and what the recent bills look like. A mortgage principal-and-interest payment alone will not account for seasonal household expenses.

Transit availability, parking expenses and building fees can change what is practical for a household. Renters can use the rent-budget tool below, then adjust the separate utility estimate for the specific unit and lease.

Practical checklist for Chicago

  • Consider condo assessments and shared building expenses
  • Check the actual property tax bill and reassessment situation
  • Allow for seasonal heating and cooling in your budget
  • Compare public transit access against car and parking costs
