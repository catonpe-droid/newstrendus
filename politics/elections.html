Voting and registration: use an official source

Election dates, ID requirements, eligibility, early-voting schedules, polling places and registration deadlines vary by state and locality and may change. For current information, find your state or local election office on USAGov. To locate your assigned polling place, consult USAGov's polling-place guidance. A news headline is not an official notice.

How to verify the 2026 midterm results

Election-night projections and final certified vote totals are different. News organizations may call a race before election officials finish counting eligible absentee, provisional and overseas ballots. An early lead can change as more ballots are counted. The U.S. Election Assistance Commission explains why those results remain unofficial until canvassing and certification.

  1. Choose the correct jurisdiction. Confirm the state, county, congressional district and office on the ballot. Results from one county do not describe the whole state.
  2. Find the official count. Use USAGov's state-election-office directory to reach the responsible election agency. Look for the agency's election results page and timestamp.
  3. Read the status label. A media race call, an unofficial election-night count, a canvass update and a certified result are not interchangeable. Check whether mail and provisional ballots are still being processed.
  4. Confirm final certification. The EAC's voter FAQs describe canvassing and certification; the rules and deadlines vary by state. For historical results and official federal reporting, use USAGov's election results guide.

What local politics includes

Local government can include county commissioners, municipalities, city councils, school boards and officials responsible for services that residents use. Ballot measures may also involve local policies, bond initiatives or rules subject to a direct vote. Look at the district and office involved; a story in one county does not automatically apply to the entire state.
