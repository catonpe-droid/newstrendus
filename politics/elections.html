How NewsTrendus covers elections

We automatically discover headlines and source links from the US edition of Google News RSS search. Results carry publication dates and publisher attribution. We do not copy the linked publisher's entire article, assign electoral projections or infer a voter's political views. Search ranking and headline prominence do not imply an editorial endorsement or scientific polling result.

Voting and registration: use an official source

Election dates, ID requirements, eligibility, early-voting schedules, polling places and registration deadlines vary by state and locality and may change. For current information, find your state or local election office on USAGov. To locate your assigned polling place, consult USAGov's polling-place guidance. A news headline is not an official notice.

What local politics includes

Local government can include county commissioners, municipalities, city councils, school boards and officials responsible for services that residents use. Ballot measures may also involve local policies, bond initiatives or rules subject to a direct vote. Look at the district and office involved; a story in one county does not automatically apply to the entire state.

How often headlines refresh

The underlying publishing workflow checks news sources approximately every 30 minutes when GitHub Actions runs on schedule. The system checks groups of states in rotation so all 50 states plus DC are searched over roughly three hours. The newsroom-style display retains recent source links for up to a week and does not claim comprehensive real-time election returns.
