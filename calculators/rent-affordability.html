How to think about rent affordability

A common starting exercise is to allocate a share of gross income to housing. This calculator uses a customizable percentage and then deducts the monthly debts and utilities you enter. It is a simple planning method, not an approved underwriting formula for every apartment.

Why costs vary by household and city

Your effective budget changes with commuting costs, medical expenses, childcare, savings goals, utility arrangements and local housing conditions. A landlord may apply a different income-screening rule and require up-front payments such as a security deposit or broker fees.

Budget before looking at listings

Test several percentages rather than treating any single ratio as universally safe. Leave room for moving costs and emergencies, and remember that rent increases and utility bills can change. The calculator uses gross income; your net-pay cash flow may require a more conservative target.

Common questions

Is 30% a legal housing limit?

No. It is a common planning benchmark, not a universal legal rule or guarantee of financial comfort.

Can I compare different cities?

Yes. Use the same income and expense assumptions for each scenario and adjust them for your real local costs.
