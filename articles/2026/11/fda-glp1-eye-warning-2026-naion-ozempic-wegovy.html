FDA GLP-1 Eye Warning: What Patients Should Know

The FDA has ordered new warnings for GLP-1 medications, including Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro and Zepbound, about a possible increased risk of a rare optic-nerve condition called NAION. The agency advises anyone with sudden vision changes to contact a health professional immediately; the notice does not mean every user will develop eye damage.

Quick answer: In its November 9, 2026 drug safety communication, the FDA required GLP-1 drugmakers to add a warning about possible non-arteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy (NAION). The condition is rare but can cause sudden, painless vision loss. Contact a clinician right away for new symptoms and discuss medication decisions with your prescriber.

Which GLP-1 drugs are covered by the FDA warning?

The agency says its labeling requirement applies to all GLP-1 receptor agonist medicines. Its published table names Ozempic, Wegovy and Rybelsus (semaglutide); Mounjaro and Zepbound (tirzepatide, a dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist); Trulicity (dulaglutide); Victoza and Saxenda (liraglutide); and other products. This is a class-wide warning, not a recall or a statement that every medication has the same measured risk. Patients should use the FDA's full drug list to identify their product rather than assume a brand is excluded.

What are NAION symptoms, and what should patients do?

The FDA describes sudden, painless loss of vision in one eye, sometimes noticed upon waking. Other signs can include blind spots, tunnel vision, changes in color perception or blurred central vision. Contact a health professional immediately if these symptoms appear. The FDA advises people who have experienced NAION to discuss with their prescriber whether to continue or stop their GLP-1 medicine. Do not independently change treatment based only on a headline: the benefits and risks depend on the individual condition and medical history.

How large is the risk? What the FDA data do and do not prove

In an FDA analysis of 126 randomized clinical trials, the NAION incidence was 6.4 cases per 100,000 person-years among GLP-1 users versus 3.8 per 100,000 person-years in comparator groups. A person-year combines the number of people studied and the time they were followed; these rates are not a prediction that a specific patient has a 6.4% risk. The analysis found 15 probable cases overall, so estimates remain imprecise.

In a separate FDA real-world analysis among adults with type 2 diabetes, the GLP-1 comparison with SGLT-2 inhibitors did not show a statistically significant increased risk, while a comparison with DPP-4 inhibitors did. These different findings are a reason to describe the evidence as a potential association, not settled proof that GLP-1 treatment causes NAION. Diabetes, hypertension and other conditions can themselves be risk factors.

Why is the warning being added now?

The FDA says it reviewed clinical trials, postmarketing reports, observational studies and animal data after earlier research raised questions about semaglutide. The agency did not identify a clear biological mechanism in its animal review. Its decision is to require warnings so patients and clinicians can recognize symptoms and weigh treatment decisions with better information. Reuters independently reported the labeling action on November 9, including the manufacturers' responses; those responses do not supersede the FDA's clinical guidance.

What should U.S. patients check next?

Review the official FDA MedWatch alert, note any new visual symptoms and contact your prescribing clinician for questions about your personal risk. Patients can report suspected adverse events through the FDA's MedWatch system. For more context, browse our Health coverage and Latest News Articles. The FDA has announced a labeling change; the exact timing of updated packaging and prescribing documents may vary by product.
