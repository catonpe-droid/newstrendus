Chase Bank Outage: What Happened to Zelle Payments

A software problem at JPMorgan Chase slowed some transactions Monday, November 16. Reports about other banks and Zelle did not establish that every bank or the Zelle network itself was down.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · November 16, 2026 · Source-linked desk reporting · Representative photograph

This is an independently written explainer based on attributed reporting, not a live bank-status feed or first-hand technical investigation. AI-assisted drafting may be used. See our editorial and corrections policy.

Representative licensed photograph of paperwork and a phone, not an image of Chase systems or affected transactions. Photographer: Leeloo The First / Pexels.

Quick answer: Chase acknowledged a technical problem on November 16, 2026, that caused some transactions to take longer than usual. The bank said customers retained access to their accounts and funds. Zelle's operator said its network was operational, although payments involving participating banks could be delayed. A later complete-resolution time has not been independently verified here.

Was Chase down on November 16?

Customers reported problems with Chase online banking and payments on Monday. The Associated Press reported that JPMorgan Chase attributed the disruption to a temporary software bug in hardware it operates. Chase described the issue as technical, rather than a cyberattack, and said it was working to restore normal service. That does not establish that every Chase service or account was inaccessible.

Reuters reported that Downdetector registered complaints involving Chase and several other major U.S. banks. User-submitted outage reports are useful signals of disruption, but they are not a verified measure of affected accounts or proof that all named institutions had the same technical failure.

Did Zelle go down too?

Early Warning Services, the operator of Zelle, said its network remained operational, according to AP. Some Zelle payments can nevertheless be delayed when a participating bank has trouble processing transactions. U.S. Bank separately warned of possible delays for transfers involving other financial institutions. The distinction matters: a transfer delay involving Zelle does not automatically mean the Zelle network itself failed.

Chase's problem could also affect transfers involving Chase and another institution. Bank of America and Citigroup said their banking services were not affected, AP reported. Avoid assuming a nationwide banking-system outage from simultaneous reports on an outage-tracking website.

What should customers do about a pending transfer?

First check the transaction history and status in the bank's official app or website. If a payment is marked pending, avoid repeatedly sending the same amount without confirming its status; duplicate submissions may create separate transfers. Keep a record of the time, amount, recipient and any confirmation number, then contact the sending bank using the phone number on its card or official website if the payment is urgent or unexpectedly delayed.

If someone claims they can reverse a transfer or fix a banking outage in exchange for a verification code, password or upfront payment, do not provide it. Use official bank support rather than links in unsolicited texts. A status report published on Monday does not guarantee that a specific customer's transfer is complete on Tuesday; only the financial institution can confirm that transaction.

Was this a cyberattack or a problem at every bank?

Chase described a software problem and said there was no evidence of a cyberattack, according to AP's November 16 account. Reuters reported that other banks said they were not experiencing significant issues. These statements do not independently establish a root cause for every reported payment delay. There is also no verified basis in the cited reports for linking this incident to separate recent technology or airline disruptions.

When will all services be back to normal?

Chase said it was working on the issue, but the sources reviewed for this article do not confirm a definitive restoration timestamp for every affected service or customer. Check official bank channels for updated status before relying on a transaction deadline.
