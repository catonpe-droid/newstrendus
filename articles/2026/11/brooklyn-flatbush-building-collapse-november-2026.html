Brooklyn Building Collapse: Flatbush Rescue and Road Closures

A three-story building collapsed beside an excavation site in Brooklyn's Flatbush neighborhood on November 11. At least one person died, 10 others were injured, and rescuers searched the debris into the evening. The cause has not been officially established.

Quick answer: A three-story building at 1321 Flatbush Avenue in Brooklyn collapsed shortly after 4 p.m. ET on Wednesday, November 11, 2026, beside an active construction excavation. The FDNY reported at least one death and 10 injured people, including four in critical condition, according to WABC's local updates and Associated Press reporting. These figures were preliminary as crews continued searching.

Where did the Brooklyn building collapse happen?

The collapse occurred in Flatbush near the intersection of Flatbush and Bedford avenues. Local station WABC identified the affected building as 1321 Flatbush Avenue and the neighboring construction site as 2625 Bedford Avenue. This location matters for residents checking street access, nearby businesses and building-safety inspections. The NYPD asked people to avoid the area as emergency crews worked; road restrictions may change, so check NYC Emergency Management and local traffic alerts rather than assuming a street is open.

What is confirmed about the casualties and rescues?

By late Wednesday, the FDNY's reported toll was at least one person dead and 10 injured, with four taken to hospitals in critical condition and six sustaining minor injuries, according to WABC. Reuters reported that rescue teams pulled survivors alive from the rubble and continued searching for others. The figures are a snapshot of a developing rescue operation, not a final casualty count. We are not publishing unconfirmed names or identifying survivors from footage.

Firefighters searched the collapsed structure with rescue dogs, tools and aerial support. AP reported that crews worked around a large excavation pit filled with fallen building material. Nearby structural damage required inspection before residents could safely return.

Did construction cause the collapse?

That has not been established by the official findings cited here. The building fell beside an active demolition or excavation site, and debris landed in the adjoining pit. Proximity alone does not prove that the construction work caused the failure. New York City's Department of Buildings was investigating the scene, according to local reporting. Claims about a contractor, permit violation or specific structural defect should be treated as unconfirmed until investigators publish evidence.

What should Flatbush residents and commuters do?

People in the area should follow police barricades and emergency instructions, avoid entering damaged buildings or construction zones, and allow rescue vehicles access. For an immediate danger, call 911. For nonemergency city services or building complaints, use NYC 311. Anyone displaced should follow city and building-management instructions rather than relying on social media claims that the site is safe.

What happens next?

Emergency crews must finish search and recovery operations before investigators can fully assess the structural failure. Building inspectors will also evaluate nearby properties. The precise cause, final casualty count, and timeline for lifting restrictions remained unresolved in the sources reviewed early November 12.
