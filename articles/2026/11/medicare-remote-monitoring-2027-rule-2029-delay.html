Medicare Remote Monitoring Rule: 2027 Changes, 2029 Delay

CMS has finalized Medicare remote monitoring changes for 2027, but the controversial staffing restriction is postponed to 2029. Here is what actually takes effect and when.

Quick answer: The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) released its final 2027 Medicare Physician Fee Schedule rule on November 4, 2026. Some remote physiologic monitoring (RPM) and remote therapeutic monitoring (RTM) requirements begin January 1, 2027. A restriction on Medicare payment for monitoring performed by outside contracted clinical staff will not take effect until January 1, 2029.

What changed in the 2027 Medicare remote monitoring rule?

CMS's official November 4 fact sheet identifies three monitoring policies finalized for 2027: RTM is limited to established patients, practitioners billing RPM or RTM must furnish a separately reportable initiating visit at the start of monitoring, and CMS is updating how these services are valued. The agency also finalized a fourth policy about the employment status of monitoring staff, but deferred its effective date by two years.

This distinction is especially important for practices planning contracts, staffing and Medicare billing in 2027. The CMS-1848-F regulation page lists November 4 as the public display date and November 9 as the scheduled Federal Register publication date. The rule is final, not a proposed change awaiting a new comment period.

2027 versus 2029: which requirements start when?

The delayed requirement is not canceled. CMS says it postponed implementation to give practices additional time to adapt. That is different from saying outsourced monitoring will be permanently reimbursable.

Why did CMS delay the outsourced-staff restriction?

CMS said it considered public comments and moved the staffing restriction to 2029. Under the policy, Medicare payment for RPM or RTM would be limited to services performed by clinical staff employed by the billing practice rather than contracted outside clinical staff. The delay preserves a transition period for providers that currently rely on third-party monitoring operations.

Independent coverage from STAT on November 6 described industry efforts to contest the eventual change. Fierce Healthcare's November 4 analysis also highlighted the delayed vendor restrictions alongside wider physician-payment changes. Industry reaction is not a substitute for the actual CMS text.

Does this mean patients lose home monitoring in 2027?

No blanket loss of Medicare remote monitoring coverage was announced in the final-rule summary. Instead, the 2027 requirements change conditions under which providers may bill for specific monitoring services. Whether a particular patient's device, service or clinical visit is covered depends on the applicable Medicare rules and the patient's situation. Patients should not stop prescribed monitoring because of a reimbursement headline.

People using home blood-pressure, heart-related or therapeutic monitoring should ask their care team whether any new visit or billing step applies to their program. The rule does not establish a universal patient copayment or a new retail price for monitoring equipment.

What should clinics and monitoring vendors do before January 2027?

  1. Review the official CMS final-rule fact sheet and regulation, rather than relying on summaries of the earlier proposal.
  2. Check whether the patient qualifies as established for RTM and document the required initiating visit for new monitoring services.
  3. Review coding and payment valuation changes with billing staff or the practice's Medicare Administrative Contractor.
  4. Identify which monitoring functions use contracted clinical staff, and prepare for the separate January 2029 employment restriction without treating the delay as a repeal.
  5. Tell patients about any actual changes to their care or billing only after verifying their specific coverage.

These are operational considerations, not individualized billing or medical advice. CMS may issue implementation guidance; practices should watch official updates rather than assume every detail can be inferred from a news summary.

Did CMS replace RPM and RTM codes with four new G-codes?

No. CMS sought comments on replacing or bundling the existing monitoring codes into four new HCPCS G-codes, but the November 4 fact sheet says the agency may consider those comments for future rulemaking. That potential future approach should not be presented as a finalized 2027 billing requirement.

What happens next?

The final rule is scheduled for Federal Register publication on November 9, 2026. Its main 2027 monitoring changes begin January 1, 2027, while the clinical-staff employment provision is set for January 1, 2029.
