October 2026 CPI: Grocery, Gas and Insurance Prices

October's U.S. inflation report shows food, energy and insurance costs rising, even as used-car prices declined. Here is what the official numbers mean for household budgets.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · November 10, 2026 · Source-linked analysis

This is a source-linked desk explainer, not first-hand reporting. Research and drafting may use AI-assisted tools. Read our editorial and corrections policy.

Representative licensed photograph of an office conversation, not a photograph of the October CPI release or survey participants. Photographer: Edmond Dantès / Pexels.

Quick answer: The Bureau of Labor Statistics' November 10 report says the Consumer Price Index rose 0.4% in October 2026, after 0.6% in September. Grocery prices rose 0.4%, gasoline 1.0%, motor vehicle insurance 0.7%, and used cars fell 1.2%. Most year-over-year CPI comparisons are unavailable because the agency did not collect October 2025 survey data.

What was the October 2026 inflation rate?

The headline CPI-U increased 0.4% on a seasonally adjusted monthly basis. Core CPI, excluding food and energy, increased 0.3% for the third consecutive month. Both readings were 0.1 percentage point above the forecasts reported by Reuters. Monthly inflation eased from September's 0.6%, but prices still rose overall. A smaller monthly increase does not mean the general price level fell.

There is no official all-items October 2026 annual CPI percentage. The BLS explains that a 2025 lapse in appropriations prevented October 2025 price collection for most series. Do not mistake September's 3.7% annual rate for October's, or treat a private estimate as an official reading.

Shelter accounted for more than 30% of the overall monthly CPI increase. The food index rose 0.5%, including a 0.9% rise in beef prices and a 1.0% rise in snacks. Electricity rose 0.9%, while utility piped gas service declined 0.8%. These differences show why households with different spending patterns may experience inflation differently.

Does a 0.7% insurance increase mean my premium will rise?

No. The BLS motor-vehicle-insurance index rose 0.7% in October, but an individual auto insurance renewal depends on the insurer, state, driver, vehicle, coverage and underwriting. The CPI number is a broad measure of price movement, not a notice that every policyholder owes 0.7% more. Consumers comparing renewals should check the actual premium, deductible, limits and exclusions rather than using the CPI change as a quote.

Why is the annual inflation number missing?

The October 2025 government funding lapse disrupted the comparison month. The BLS says it cannot publish 12-month changes for indexes without that October 2025 baseline. Some component series, such as gasoline, still have annual readings where the agency has comparable data; the gasoline index was up 37.4% from a year earlier. That exception should not be used to infer a national all-items annual rate.

What does the CPI report mean for interest rates?

The hotter-than-expected figures added to market discussion of a December Federal Reserve rate increase, according to AP's November 10 coverage and Reuters. The CPI release itself does not set interest rates, mortgage rates or credit-card APRs. Decisions depend on further economic information and the Federal Open Market Committee's judgment; a market probability is not a guarantee.

For context on inflation expectations versus measured prices, see our existing New York Fed household expectations report and Richmond Fed commentary on rates. The surveys and speeches are different from today's official CPI measurement.

When is the next CPI report?

The BLS lists the November 2026 CPI report for December 10, 2026, at 8:30 a.m. Eastern. Readers should check the official CPI page for the latest release, methodological notes and any corrections. A household can use October's category breakdown to identify areas to watch, but the data cannot predict a particular store price, insurance renewal or loan offer.
