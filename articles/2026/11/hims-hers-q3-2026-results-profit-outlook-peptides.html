Hims & Hers Q3 2026: Revenue Rises, Profit Outlook Falls

The telehealth company's third-quarter sales grew rapidly, but higher costs and a narrower annual forecast complicate the picture for customers and investors.

Quick answer: Hims & Hers reported $890.4 million in revenue for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, up 49% year over year, but posted a $33 million net loss. The company narrowed its full-year sales forecast to $3.13 billion–$3.15 billion and reduced adjusted EBITDA guidance to $257 million–$277 million, according to its November 9 SEC-filed results.

What did Hims & Hers report for Q3 2026?

The results show a business growing in sales and subscribers while spending more to support its product mix and international expansion. Revenue rose 49% compared with the third quarter of 2025. Subscriber count increased 13% to nearly 2.8 million, but the company reported a net loss rather than the $15.8 million profit recorded a year earlier.

Gross margin declined to 57% from 74% in the year-earlier quarter. This is important because sales growth alone does not show how much the company retains after direct costs. The company also reported adjusted EBITDA of $82.4 million, up from $78.4 million; adjusted EBITDA is a non-GAAP metric and is not the same as net income.

The narrowed revenue range should not be confused with an upgrade to profit expectations: the company reduced its adjusted EBITDA outlook. The difference matters to anyone evaluating the sustainability of rapid expansion. The forecasts are forward-looking statements and may change.

What does the planned peptide offering mean?

Reuters reported that the company plans to offer certain peptide treatments, including NAD+, sermorelin and glutathione, in November, with other products potentially manufactured later. Crucially, regulatory enforcement discretion for some compounded substances does not mean FDA approval of a treatment for safety or effectiveness. Availability and legality can depend on the specific substance, preparation and circumstances.

Readers should not interpret a product launch as evidence of proven medical benefit. People considering a treatment should consult a qualified clinician about evidence, risks and alternatives, and check the FDA's current status for the exact product. The company's expectation that peptide sales will be immaterial in 2026 is not a forecast of proven medical outcomes.

What should U.S. customers and investors watch next?

Customers should distinguish FDA-approved branded medicines from compounded products and ask which exact product they would receive, who prescribes it and what monitoring is offered. Investors should watch the fourth-quarter results, the mix of U.S. and international revenue, subscriber retention and gross margins—not only headline revenue growth.
