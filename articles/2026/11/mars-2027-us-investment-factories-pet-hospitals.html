Mars $1.4B US Investment: Factories and Pet Hospitals

Mars says its largest-ever single-year U.S. investment commitment will cover manufacturing and operations in 2027. The company named four veterinary hospital projects, but did not itemize the full budget.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · November 10, 2026 · Source-linked analysis

This is a source-linked desk explainer, not first-hand reporting. Research and drafting may use AI-assisted tools. Read our editorial and corrections policy.

Representative licensed candy photograph; not an image of a Mars manufacturing site or investment announcement. Photographer: Terrance Barksdale / Pexels.

Quick answer: On November 10, 2026, Mars announced an estimated $1.4 billion investment in its U.S. manufacturing and operations during 2027. The company specifically identified more than $34 million for four veterinary hospitals in Maryland, Texas and North Carolina, with up to 195 jobs tied to those hospitals—not a companywide jobs forecast. Read the original Mars announcement.

Where will Mars invest $1.4 billion in 2027?

The McLean, Virginia-based food, snacking and pet-care company describes the $1.4 billion as its largest planned U.S. investment in a single year. The commitment spans manufacturing and operations, including veterinary services; it is not solely a candy-factory construction budget. Mars says it has invested more than $9 billion in domestic operations since 2017, and that 94% of the products it sells in the U.S. are made domestically.

The November announcement does not provide a complete state-by-state allocation of the 2027 funds. It also does not say how many total new manufacturing jobs the plan will create. Readers should distinguish the named veterinary openings from older factory projects cited as examples of ongoing expansion.

Which new Mars pet hospitals and jobs are confirmed?

Mars says more than $34 million of the 2027 commitment will open four facilities early next year. The company estimates these projects could create up to 195 jobs combined:

What about the Utah, Texas and Ohio factories?

Mars highlighted three recent investments as evidence of its U.S. strategy. These are background projects, not a disclosed breakdown of the entire 2027 spending commitment:

  • Salt Lake City, Utah: $60 million for two Trü Frü manufacturing lines inside an existing Nature’s Bakery facility. Mars says the lines will have capacity for 17 million frozen Trü Frü products per year.
  • Waco, Texas: $165 million invested since 2024 at the site that makes Snickers, Starburst and Skittles, with work focused on efficiency and operations.
  • Columbus, Ohio: $49 million at a wet pet-food facility to bring production of Temptations Lickable Treats to the United States, with projected capacity exceeding 400 million pouches annually.

These historical amounts should not simply be added to the new 2027 budget. The company has not provided sufficient detail to establish whether or how individual past projects overlap with the newly announced commitment.

Will the investment lower candy prices or create jobs nearby?

No price reduction, national hiring total or consumer savings estimate was announced. Investment in capacity and supply-chain resilience may affect future operations, but it does not establish a specific retail price change. Mars says it employs about 80,000 people in the United States across its operations, which include more than 2,000 veterinary clinics and hospitals.

Independent Reuters reporting places the announcement against slower packaged-food demand and changing consumer preferences. That broader industry context does not change what Mars has—and has not—committed to specific locations.

What happens next?

The first identifiable milestones are the four hospital openings expected in early 2027. Additional factory locations, exact construction schedules and any broader hiring commitments would require further company announcements.
