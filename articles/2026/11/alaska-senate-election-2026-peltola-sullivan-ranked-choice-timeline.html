Alaska Senate Election 2026: Why Peltola vs. Sullivan Is Still Undecided

Mary Peltola led Dan Sullivan by 626 votes in a Nov. 4 count. Here is Alaska’s mail-ballot timetable, ranked-choice rules and what happens next.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · November 5, 2026 · Source-linked reporting · Representative photograph

Representative licensed photograph, not an image of the actual news event. Photo: John De Leon / Pexels (licensed photography).

Quick answer: Mary Peltola led Dan Sullivan by 626 votes in a Nov. 4 count. Here is Alaska’s mail-ballot timetable, ranked-choice rules and what happens next.

What do the latest reported vote counts show?

A November 4 election-reporting snapshot showed Mary Peltola with 102,701 first-choice votes and Republican incumbent Dan S. Sullivan with 102,075, a lead of only 626 votes. Those figures are not a live counter, a prediction of the ultimate winner or a certified result. Consult the Alaska Division of Elections for the most current official totals.

With a margin this narrow, additional ballots and ranked-choice preferences can matter. Neither leading candidate had established an outright majority in the initial report.

Why does counting take longer in Alaska?

Alaska's election procedures allow some eligible mail ballots to arrive after Election Day, subject to postmark and receipt rules. Alaska Public Media reports deadlines of up to ten days for some domestic mailed ballots and fifteen days for certain overseas ballots. Election workers also verify eligibility and process outstanding votes.

As a result, a near-tie on election night is not the same as a final tally. Counting and certification are official administrative processes that continue after the first television projections.

How does ranked-choice voting affect the outcome?

In Alaska's ranked-choice general election, voters can rank candidates in preference order. If nobody receives a majority of first-choice votes, the last-place candidate is eliminated and applicable ballots transfer to another valid ranked choice. Rounds continue according to the state's tabulation rules until a winner emerges.

The expected November 18 ranked-choice tabulation is therefore important if the initial count produces no majority. The date is a published election-process milestone, not a guarantee about the ultimate winner.

Why are there two candidates named Sullivan?

Reporting on this race noted that the ballot contained incumbent Senator Dan S. Sullivan and another candidate, Daniel J. Sullivan Jr. Similar names drew political accusations and legal disputes. Those reports do not establish that fraud occurred or that any given voter mistakenly selected a candidate. Assertions about voter intent should not be treated as facts without evidence.

When should voters expect a final result?

The next meaningful milestones are the arrival and acceptance of outstanding lawful ballots, the official ranked-choice tabulation if required, and state certification. The authoritative source is the Alaska Division of Elections. NewsTrendus will avoid calling a winner based solely on an early lead.
