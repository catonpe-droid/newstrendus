C.A.M.P. Ice Axe Recall: Models, Batches and Repair

A federal safety recall covers specialized ice-climbing axes with hardware that may come loose. Here is the exact model-and-batch checklist and the manufacturer's free repair process.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · November 12, 2026 · Source-linked desk reporting · Representative photograph

Quick answer: C.A.M.P. USA recalled about 2,230 mountain ice axes on November 5, 2026 because a screw may loosen and allow the handle or pick to detach during climbing. Stop using affected gear and arrange a free repair through C.A.M.P.'s official recall portal. Federal recall number: 27-070.

Which C.A.M.P. ice axe models and batches are recalled?

The recall includes four climbing-axe models plus a separately sold nut-and-bolt accessory kit. The Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) lists the following identifiers. The batch number is on the side of the shaft, where the brand name CAMP is printed in white.

Do not assume every C.A.M.P. axe is affected. Check the exact model and batch against the federal photographs and contact the company if the marking is unclear. The separate model 3641 accessory kit is included in the notice.

Why is the bolt problem serious?

The screw can loosen in use, potentially allowing the handle or pick to detach. On steep ice or mixed terrain, a sudden loss of a tool component could lead to a fall and serious injury or death. The recall announcement reported no incidents or injuries; that is not evidence an affected tool is safe to climb with. A climber should stop using the recalled equipment immediately rather than trying an improvised tightening procedure.

How to get the free C.A.M.P. ice axe repair

Owners should visit camp-usa.com/x-bolt-recall-102026 and register the model and batch number. C.A.M.P. offers two options: a free replacement hardware kit with repair instructions for the owner, or repair performed by the company. For company service, C.A.M.P. supplies prepaid shipping and packaging for returning the affected ice axe. Follow the manufacturer's instructions rather than substituting hardware from a local store.

For help, call 877-421-2267 on weekdays, 8 a.m.–5 p.m. Mountain Time, or email warranties@camp-usa.com. These are the contact details listed in the CPSC recall, not a third-party claims service. Keep the repair confirmation with your gear records.

Where were the recalled tools sold?

The CPSC says the equipment was sold at REI stores nationwide and online through Camp-usa.com, Backcountry.com, Amazon.com and REI.com between November 2025 and September 2026. Listed prices ranged from about $30 to $330, reflecting different products including the accessory hardware. The recalled products were manufactured in Italy and imported by C.A.M.P. USA Inc. of Golden, Colorado.

What should a climber check before the next trip?

Inspect the shaft marking and batch, compare with the official notice, and remove affected gear from use pending the authorized fix. If your climbing partner or guide service owns the tools, share the recall details with them. A past purchase from REI or another listed retailer alone does not prove a particular tool is recalled; model and batch identification matters.
