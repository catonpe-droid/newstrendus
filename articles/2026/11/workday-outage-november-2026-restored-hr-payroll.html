Workday Outage: Service Restored After November 13 Disruption

Workday's November 13 disruption interrupted HR and payroll-related access at U.S. organizations. Reports on November 14 say the provider restored service, but employees may still need to reconcile missed transactions with their own employers.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · November 14, 2026 · Source-linked desk reporting · Representative photograph

This is a source-linked explainer, not first-hand incident reporting. Research and drafting may use AI-assisted tools. See our editorial and corrections policy.

Representative licensed office photograph, not a photo of the Workday incident. Photo: Alan Quirván / Pexels.

Quick answer: Workday experienced a widespread service disruption on Friday, November 13, 2026. Reporting published November 14 says service was restored by approximately 11 a.m. Eastern Saturday. If your employer's timekeeping, benefits or payroll workflows were affected, check its own HR notices and confirm that any missed submissions were recorded.

Is Workday back up after the November 13 outage?

According to a November 14 report citing Workday, the provider restored service by about 11 a.m. Eastern. The company attributed the incident to disruption affecting two data centers. That provider-wide recovery report does not establish that every organization's downstream integrations or queued employee transactions were immediately reconciled. An employee who still sees an error should contact their organization's Workday administrator rather than assuming a new global outage.

What happened on Friday, and which functions were affected?

The disruption became widely visible Friday afternoon and evening. Brown University's public IT incident history documented Workday unavailability on November 13 and identified time reporting, open enrollment, expense reports, hiring and time-off management among functions its community could not use. Brown's notice said the vendor had encountered a configuration error while reconnecting systems during routine maintenance. That description is specific to the institution's incident update and should not be treated as an independently confirmed final root-cause report for every customer.

The incident affected access to business processes, but an unavailable application is not proof that employee records were deleted, salaries were missed or personal information was exposed. Such claims require separate evidence. Brown's status communications specifically addressed the privacy of its information; readers should rely on their own employer's communications for their circumstances.

What should employees do about timesheets and payroll?

First, try the employer-provided Workday login rather than an unfamiliar link circulating on social media. If a time entry, leave request or expense claim was interrupted, compare the transaction against the system after restoration. Preserve your own contemporaneous record of hours and submission confirmations and ask HR or payroll when a missing entry should be re-entered. Avoid duplicate submissions without checking the employer's instructions: some delayed transactions may sync later.

Brown's outage instructions told employees who normally enter time directly in Workday to keep separate records during the outage; workers using designated physical time clocks could continue using them, with synchronization expected after service resumed. These were Brown-specific operational instructions, not a universal policy for every Workday customer. Paycheck timing, benefit deadlines and approval procedures vary by employer.

How can HR teams check recovery safely?

Administrators should review their own Workday tenant and vendor notices, confirm whether integrations and scheduled jobs have caught up, and communicate clear deadlines for employees to correct missing entries. Prioritize timekeeping and payroll cutoffs, open-enrollment deadlines and expense approvals. A service-restored announcement is an important milestone, but it does not substitute for checking records in each organization's environment.

What is confirmed and what remains unknown?

The verified chronology is a November 13 Workday disruption, documented impacts at institutions such as Brown, and a reported November 14 restoration. The precise scope of delayed payroll transactions across all customers, the number of employees affected, and a comprehensive technical postmortem were not independently established for this report. Readers should avoid claims of a cyberattack or data breach without authoritative evidence.
