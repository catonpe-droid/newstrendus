Waymo School Bus Investigation: What NHTSA Is Reviewing

Federal safety regulators are taking another look at how Waymo's driverless vehicles respond to stopped school buses after a previous software recall. The new review is an investigation, not a finding that every vehicle is unsafe.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · November 11, 2026 · Source-linked desk reporting · Representative photograph

This independent explainer is based on linked regulatory records and news reporting, not firsthand observation. AI-assisted drafting may be used; see our editorial and corrections policy.

Representative licensed photograph of a vehicle interior; it does not show a Waymo robotaxi, school bus, or the events under investigation. Photo: Erik Mclean / Pexels.

Quick answer: The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration has opened a new review of Waymo automated-driving systems following reports of vehicles passing school buses displaying stop signals. Reuters reported the investigation November 9, 2026, saying it covers about 2,895 vehicles and examines whether a prior software remedy was sufficient. The investigation is ongoing; it does not establish a final defect determination.

Why is NHTSA reviewing Waymo again?

The issue is specific: a driverless vehicle must properly respond when a school bus is stopped to load or unload children with red warning lights or its stop arm activated. A robotaxi proceeding before those signals are withdrawn can create a serious safety risk even if there is no collision. Reuters reports that school districts in Austin and Atlanta documented additional apparent violations after a software fix. The review is intended to examine the effectiveness of that fix and subsequent behavior, not to adjudicate every reported event in this article.

Waymo says it has made substantial improvements to school-bus behavior and is cooperating with regulators. That company response is distinct from a regulator's independent conclusion. The NHTSA safety-issues database is the authoritative place to check the latest investigation and recall status.

What did the December 2025 recall address?

In its December 10, 2025 Part 573 recall report, Waymo identified a software condition in its fifth-generation automated driving system. Under certain circumstances, vehicles stopped or stopping for a school bus could proceed again while red lights were still flashing or the stop arm remained extended. The report listed 3,067 potentially involved systems and said the affected vehicles had received updated software. This was a software recall of Waymo-operated vehicles, not a recall requiring private car owners to visit a dealership.

The approximately 2,895 vehicles cited in Reuters' November 2026 report refer to the newer regulatory review, not the original 3,067-system recall population. Those numbers should not be added together or treated as counts of crashes, injuries, or confirmed violations.

Does this mean Waymo service has been suspended?

No nationwide suspension is established by the cited investigation report. Opening a recall query does not itself mean regulators have ordered all Waymo vehicles off public roads. A subsequent recall, software change, operational restriction, or closure without further action would require separate official confirmation. Readers should avoid interpreting a safety investigation as a completed legal judgment.

What should parents and road users know?

School-bus stop signals exist to protect children crossing or entering the roadway. Drivers and automated vehicles alike must comply with applicable state laws. Anyone witnessing a potential safety problem should prioritize immediate safety and provide factual details to appropriate local authorities. NHTSA also accepts vehicle safety complaints through its official reporting channel; a report is not proof of a defect.

Waymo owns and operates the affected driverless fleet, so ordinary motorists should not assume their personal vehicle has a Waymo recall. For unrelated privately owned vehicles, the NHTSA VIN recall lookup can check open safety recalls. For broader reporting on car-buyer protections, see our FTC auto pricing explainer; it concerns a separate consumer issue.

What happens next?

Regulators may seek further records, evaluate reported incidents and assess whether the earlier remedy adequately addressed the behavior. No deadline for a final NHTSA decision was confirmed in the sources reviewed. Future outcomes should be checked against the agency's published investigation and recall records rather than assumed from a headline.
