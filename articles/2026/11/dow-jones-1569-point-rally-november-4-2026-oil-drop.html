Dow Jumps 1,569 Points on November 4: What Drove the US Stock Market Rally?

The Dow closed up 1,569.40 points on Nov. 4, 2026. See S&P 500 and Nasdaq closes, falling oil prices and why investors reacted after the midterms.

Quick answer: The Dow closed up 1,569.40 points on Nov. 4, 2026. See S&P 500 and Nasdaq closes, falling oil prices and why investors reacted after the midterms.

How big was the rally?

The Dow Jones Industrial Average climbed 1,569.40 points on Wednesday, November 4, 2026, closing at 53,356.12, according to the Associated Press closing-market summary. The S&P 500 rose approximately 1.6% to 7,688.62 and the Nasdaq Composite gained about 1.4% to 25,736.51. The Russell 2000 index of smaller companies also gained roughly 1.6% to 2,856.95.

These are November 4 closing values, not live November 5 quotations. Intraday moves and subsequent trading can differ materially.

How did the midterm elections affect market sentiment?

The rally followed U.S. midterm elections in which Democrats captured majorities in Congress. Analysts and traders weighed how the new political balance might affect legislation, oversight and government policy. A change in expectations can move share prices, but it is not possible to attribute every point of the market rise to a single political event.

Corporate earnings, positioning, interest-rate expectations and other news were also moving markets. Headlines that say the election alone caused the entire surge would oversimplify the trading session.

Why did falling oil prices matter?

Crude oil prices fell sharply in Wednesday trading. Lower oil costs can reduce projected expenses for airlines, transportation companies and other energy-intensive businesses, and can eventually influence gasoline prices and inflation expectations. But the effect on consumers is not immediate or uniform.

Refining costs, taxes, distribution and local conditions influence retail gasoline prices. Meanwhile, falling crude may weigh on the revenues of energy producers even as some non-energy sectors benefit.

Does one strong session signal a lasting bull market?

No single day establishes a durable trend. Equity prices are sensitive to changing expectations, and a large upward move can be followed by volatility or reversals. Investors should avoid treating historical closes as fresh buy signals or assuming the movement will repeat.

The next indicators to monitor include crude-oil prices, Treasury yields, inflation data, corporate results and specific legislative proposals after the election.

What sources establish these figures?

The figures cited above are from published November 4 closing-market reports. They are historical market data and may not match prices shown by a live financial app on a later trading day.
