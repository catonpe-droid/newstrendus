IRS FIRE Deadline Nov. 9: What Filers Must Check

The November 9 cutoff applies to changes to existing FIRE transmitter-code applications—not to every Form 1099 filing. Here is the IRS transition timetable and what businesses should verify.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · November 8, 2026 · Source-linked IRS filing guidance · Representative photograph

This desk explainer is based on published IRS documents and independent professional reporting, not a first-hand test of IRS systems. AI-assisted drafting may be used. Read our editorial and corrections policy.

Representative licensed photograph of tax paperwork, not a photo of the FIRE or IRIS systems. Photographer: Leeloo The First / Pexels.

Quick answer: The IRS says November 9, 2026 is the final day to change existing Information Returns (IR) applications for FIRE Transmitter Control Codes (TCCs). FIRE itself has a separate final submission deadline of November 19 at 3 p.m. Eastern. Businesses and tax preparers using FIRE must move to the Information Returns Intake System (IRIS) for the 2027 filing season.

What exactly expires on November 9?

The deadline concerns edits to existing FIRE IR applications for TCCs, not the submission of all information returns. According to the IRS TCC application instructions, existing applications become read-only after November 9 and remain available for historical reference. The agency is already no longer accepting new FIRE TCC applications.

That distinction matters for payroll teams, accounting practices and third-party transmitters: changing an existing FIRE application is a different task from obtaining an IRIS TCC. A FIRE code does not automatically provide IRIS access. The National Association of Tax Professionals independently explains the credential transition.

The dates come from IRS release IR-2026-99 and the agency's FIRE retirement page. These are system transition deadlines; they do not, by themselves, establish or extend a particular taxpayer's Form 1099 due date.

Who needs to act, and does this affect all taxpayers?

The immediate audience is organizations and service providers that still use FIRE to electronically transmit information returns. This includes some payroll operations, accounting firms and business information-return filers. It is not a new deadline for an individual simply filing a personal Form 1040. If a business outsources information returns, its service provider should confirm which system and credentials will be used for the 2027 season.

The PayrollOrg professional briefing separately confirms the November deadlines and notes that IRIS TCC applications can take substantial processing time. The IRS 2026 General Instructions for Certain Information Returns says typical TCC processing takes within 45 business days, although timing varies. Applying now does not guarantee approval by a specific date.

IRIS portal versus application-to-application: which fits?

The IRS describes two IRIS channels. Its Taxpayer Portal is a free browser-based option that can handle up to 100 returns at a time through manual entry or CSV upload. Application-to-Application (A2A) supports software-based, higher-volume electronic submissions. A firm should evaluate volume, its software's supported format, its authorization and whether a third-party transmitter will file on its behalf. Neither option makes an old FIRE TCC interchangeable with an IRIS TCC.

The IRS provides the current application route and technical resources through its IRIS information hub. Avoid treating a vendor's marketing promise as confirmation of an IRS approval or a filing receipt.

Five checks before the FIRE shutdown

  1. Determine whether your organization, accountant or filing vendor still sends information returns through FIRE.
  2. If an existing FIRE IR application needs correction, check the official IRS instructions before the November 9 cutoff.
  3. Confirm an IRIS TCC application has been submitted where needed; do not assume the FIRE credential transfers.
  4. Test your intended IRIS workflow, including data export, recipient details and any software/vendor integration, using current IRS specifications.
  5. Retain acknowledgments for any remaining FIRE transmissions before November 19 at 3 p.m. ET and prepare an IRIS process for 2027 filings and corrections.

These are practical workflow checks, not individualized tax advice. The IRS has not announced that its transition waives ordinary filing penalties or guarantees an extension. Verify obligations for your forms and business circumstances.

What is different from the IRS Tax Pro Account payment update?

This FIRE-to-IRIS change concerns electronic submission of information returns, not the separate November Tax Pro Account expansion for business-client payments. Readers tracking other tax policy changes can also browse Business coverage and Latest News Articles. The systems solve different problems, so a payment-account authorization is not a substitute for information-return transmission credentials.
