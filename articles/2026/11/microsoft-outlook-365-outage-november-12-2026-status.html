Microsoft Outlook Outage: What Happened on November 12

A major U.S. workday interruption hit Outlook email and other Microsoft 365 services. Here's what was confirmed, what Microsoft said it was doing, and how to distinguish service-wide trouble from an account problem.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · November 12, 2026 · Source-linked desk reporting · Representative photograph

Quick answer: On Thursday, November 12, 2026, Outlook, Exchange Online and other Microsoft 365 services experienced widespread access and connectivity problems in the United States. Microsoft acknowledged Exchange Online mailbox connection errors in the Americas and described elevated load on part of its infrastructure. It was redistributing traffic and taking additional mitigation steps. The reporting available at publication did not establish a universal restoration time.

Was Outlook down on November 12?

Yes, many users reported being unable to load Outlook inboxes or send and receive messages on November 12. The Associated Press reported that Downdetector received nearly 130,000 Outlook problem reports shortly after 1 p.m. Eastern Time. These are user-submitted reports, not a count of affected Microsoft accounts, so they should not be described as the number of people actually impacted.

Which Microsoft 365 services were affected?

The disruption was particularly visible in Outlook and Exchange Online mailbox access. Reports also involved Teams, Copilot and broader Microsoft 365 services. Reuters reported more than 97,000 Outlook reports, more than 38,000 Microsoft 365 reports and approximately 4,500 Teams reports at 2:07 p.m. ET. The reporting totals differ because they reflect different snapshots and service categories; adding them together would not produce a reliable unique-user total.

What did Microsoft say caused the interruption?

Microsoft acknowledged connectivity errors affecting some Exchange Online infrastructure handling access requests. It said elevated load on part of its systems was likely contributing to the errors. Reuters reported that the company was pausing deployments, redirecting traffic away from affected infrastructure and restarting servers as part of mitigation. That is an account of the company's operational response, not a confirmed final root-cause finding. A separate Teams problem in North America had earlier been described as restored, but that statement did not mean every Microsoft 365 service was fully recovered.

How can U.S. users check whether Outlook is working again?

For business and school accounts, ask the organization's Microsoft 365 administrator to consult the official Microsoft 365 admin center and its Service health dashboard for tenant-specific advisories and incident updates. Microsoft's public service status page may provide broader context, though it does not replace tenant-specific incident details. Avoid treating a social-media screenshot or a falling Downdetector graph as proof that every affected mailbox has recovered.

If Outlook fails in both browser and desktop or mobile clients, that may be consistent with a service-side incident, but local account, device or network problems can also cause similar symptoms. Preserve unsent work and use an approved alternative communication channel for urgent business messages. Repeatedly changing passwords, deleting profiles or disabling security protections is not a verified outage remedy.

What happens next, and what is still uncertain?

At the time of this November 12 report, Microsoft had described mitigation work but had not provided a confirmed all-users restoration time in the cited coverage. The scale of actual affected accounts, final root cause and any later complete recovery announcement require separate official verification. Readers should check the current Microsoft service advisory rather than assume this article is a live status feed.
