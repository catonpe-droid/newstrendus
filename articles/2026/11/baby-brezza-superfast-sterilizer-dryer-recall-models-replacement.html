Baby Brezza Recall: Check Models and Get a Free Replacement

A November 5 federal recall affects three Baby Brezza sterilizer dryer models. Here is how to check the bottom label and claim a free replacement.

Quick answer: The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission announced on November 5, 2026 that approximately 29,000 Baby Brezza SuperFast Sterilizer Dryers in the United States are being recalled because they can overheat. The affected model numbers are BRZ0083, BRZ0154 and BRZ0156. Stop using an affected appliance and follow Baby Brezza's official process to receive a free replacement, including shipping.

Which Baby Brezza sterilizer dryer models are recalled?

The CPSC identifies three model numbers: BRZ0083, BRZ0154 and BRZ0156. These countertop appliances sterilize and dry baby bottles, breast pump parts and accessories. The recall concerns these specified SuperFast Sterilizer Dryers, not every Baby Brezza product or every bottle sterilizer on the market.

Find the model number on the product label on the bottom of the unit. Before moving the appliance to inspect it, unplug it and let it cool. Compare the printed number with the official CPSC recall notice and product photographs. Do not rely on the appearance of a generic photograph.

Why were the SuperFast Sterilizer Dryers recalled?

According to the CPSC, the recalled units can overheat while plugged in and operating. The appliance's surface may become hot enough to pose a burn hazard when handled. The company reported 32 incidents involving overheating or melting plastic housing. No injuries had been reported in the November 5 notice. The absence of reported injuries does not mean the affected appliances are safe to continue using.

Parents separately covered the recall and its immediate safety implications for households that sterilize feeding equipment.

How do I request a free Baby Brezza replacement?

  1. Stop using the recalled dryer immediately. Switch it off, unplug it and allow it to cool.
  2. Check the bottom label for one of the three affected model numbers.
  3. Go to Baby Brezza's official product safety recall form and register your unit.
  4. Follow the manufacturer's instructions for documenting the model label, obtaining a return authorization number and disabling the appliance. The CPSC says the process includes cutting the power cord and submitting photographs. Do not cut a cord while an appliance is plugged in; follow the official directions and seek manufacturer assistance if uncertain.
  5. Send the required model-label, disabled-appliance and authorization photographs to recall@babybrezza.com as instructed, then dispose of the old appliance according to local rules.

The remedy is a free replacement sterilizer dryer with shipping included, not a cash refund. Follow the official recall form for the current process; the company may clarify documentation steps.

Where were the recalled units sold, and what did they cost?

The CPSC says the dryers were sold at TJ Maxx and Marshalls stores nationwide and online at babybrezza.com, Target.com and Babylist.com from January 2024 through October 2026. The reported retail price range was $160–$180. Those are historical prices cited in the recall, not current replacement prices or a reimbursement amount.

About 4,500 additional units were sold in Canada, according to the federal notice. U.S. owners should use the U.S. recall instructions; Canadian consumers should confirm the applicable local procedure.

How can I contact Baby Brezza about the recall?

The manufacturer lists 888-527-9824 for recall assistance, Monday through Friday from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. Eastern Time, and recall@babybrezza.com. The CPSC recall number is 27-072. If you cannot read the model label, contact the manufacturer rather than attempting to run the appliance to identify it.

What should parents do while waiting for a replacement?

Do not continue using a matching recalled unit while waiting for a replacement. For infant feeding equipment, follow the cleaning and sanitizing directions provided by the bottle or pump manufacturer and applicable public-health guidance; this recall notice does not prescribe a universal substitute appliance. Keep the disconnected recalled unit away from children until the manufacturer-directed disposal steps are complete.
