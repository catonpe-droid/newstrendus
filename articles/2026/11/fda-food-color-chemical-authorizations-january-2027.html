FDA Food Color Chemical Rules: What Changes January 11

The FDA has ended specific authorizations for chemicals once permitted in produce-marking inks and the manufacturing of three color additives. The January 2027 effective date does not mean all foods containing annatto, paprika or turmeric are being recalled.

Quick answer: The U.S. Food and Drug Administration announced November 9 that it is revoking authorization for methylene chloride in inks used to mark fruits and vegetables, and for three chemicals in production of annatto, paprika and turmeric color additives. The changes take effect January 11, 2027. The FDA says these uses have been discontinued or abandoned; its announcement does not order consumers to discard those foods.

Which chemical uses are losing FDA authorization?

Two separate regulatory actions address different uses. The first removes permission to use methylene chloride as a diluent in ink used to mark fruits and vegetables. The second removes authorizations for methylene chloride, ethylene dichloride and trichloroethylene in manufacturing annatto extract, paprika oleoresin and turmeric oleoresin. The latter three are color additives; the decision targets particular manufacturing substances and processes, not the underlying spices themselves.

These details come directly from the FDA's November 9 constituent update. The agency describes two separate petitions, not a blanket ban on every product that contains a natural color.

Why did the FDA make these changes?

For produce-marking ink, a petition from several public-health and environmental groups cited the federal Delaney Clause, which applies to certain ingested color additives found to cause cancer in humans or animals. The FDA states methylene chloride has caused cancer in laboratory animals. The agency's action on the other manufacturing uses followed a petition by the International Association of Color Manufacturers showing that industry had abandoned them. Those are different legal and factual bases and should not be conflated.

In independent reporting, Food & Wine describes advocates' concern about how regulators treat chemicals used during production versus ingredients classified as color additives. That debate is context; it does not establish that an individual grocery item is contaminated.

Do shoppers need to throw away turmeric, paprika or annatto?

No product-specific recall is announced in this FDA action. The regulator says the listed uses were already discontinued or abandoned, and that the three chemicals have no remaining authorized uses as color additives or in the production of color additives. Consumers should not interpret a manufacturing authorization change as evidence that every package of paprika or turmeric on shelves is unsafe. If a separate food recall is issued, it should identify the affected product and lots through an official notice.

For product-specific warnings, use the FDA recalls database. Our separate cinnamon recall identification guide concerns a different food-safety issue and must not be confused with this rule.

When does the new rule take effect, and what remains unresolved?

The FDA lists January 11, 2027 as the effective date. This article does not establish a new labeling requirement for consumers or a specific retailer refund process. The agency's announcement is limited to the named authorized uses. Separate questions about chemicals in decaffeination or other food manufacturing are not resolved by these two actions.
