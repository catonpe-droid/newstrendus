Magnetopia Magnetic Blocks Recall: Models and Refund

A U.S. recall covers 15 Magnetopia magnetic cube models sold in sets of 60 to 200. The packaging model code—not the color of the blocks—is the most useful way to check an order.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · November 9, 2026 · Federal recall announced November 5

Source-linked consumer-service reporting, not firsthand product testing. AI-assisted drafting may be used; see our editorial and corrections policy.

Representative licensed photograph of ordinary wooden building blocks, not recalled Magnetopia magnetic cubes or an incident photograph. Photographer: Ksenia Chernaya / Pexels.

Quick answer: The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission's November 5, 2026 notice recalls about 7,600 Magnetopia magnetic building cube and block sets. Stop using affected sets immediately and contact Magnetopia for a full refund; the company provides a prepaid return label. The notice lists no reported incidents or injuries.

Which Magnetopia building blocks are recalled?

Sets of 60, 100, 150 and 200 magnetic cubes are covered when their model numbers match the federal notice. The cubes are sold in themed colors and landscape designs, so a picture or color alone cannot confirm a match. Find the model number on the back of the original product packaging. Why did the federal agency announce this recall?

The CPSC says magnets can become loose if a cube breaks or opens. If swallowed, multiple high-powered magnets—or a magnet and another metal object—can attract inside the digestive tract, potentially causing blockage, twisting, perforation, blood poisoning or death. No incidents or injuries had been reported in this recall notice; that does not eliminate the underlying hazard. Keep affected sets out of children's reach rather than trying to repair a damaged cube.

How do buyers get the full refund?

  1. Stop use immediately. Put the cubes somewhere children cannot access them, including loose or broken pieces.
  2. Check the package model number. Compare it against the table and official CPSC photographs. An online order confirmation may help establish when and where the set was purchased.
  3. Contact Magnetopia. The CPSC lists toyrecall78945@outlook.com and the recall information link in its official recall record.
  4. Request the prepaid return label. The announced remedy is a full refund after return of recalled cubes using the prepaid label provided by the company. Follow the recall team's return directions rather than mailing loose magnets without instructions.

The notice does not specify a universal refund processing deadline or require consumers to pay for return postage. It does not announce a replacement product or a repair kit. If the packaging is missing, contact the recall team and ask what evidence they need before deciding the set is unaffected.

Where and when were the magnetic cubes sold?

The CPSC identifies online sales at Magnetopia.com from April 2025 through October 2026, priced approximately $20 to $40. The importer is HBT LTD, doing business as Magnetopia, of Hong Kong; the products were manufactured in China. The official recall identifier is 27-076. These purchase details help buyers find records but do not replace model-number verification.

What if a child may have swallowed a magnet?

Do not wait for symptoms or assume the magnet will pass harmlessly. Seek urgent professional medical assessment if ingestion is suspected, and tell the clinician the toy may contain high-powered magnets.
