NYC Marathon Tracker 2026: How to Follow Runners, Start Times and Official Results

The 2026 NYC Marathon has winners: Andreas Almgren and Sheila Chepkirui. Here are verified elite results and how to find an individual runner’s official time.

Quick answer: To follow a runner in the November 1, 2026 New York City Marathon, use the free official New York Road Runners app. Its runner tracking and leaderboards provide live, unofficial progress; confirmed finishing results are posted separately at NYRR Results after the race.

NEW YORK, November 1, 2026 — Searches for an NYC marathon tracker are rising as the five-borough race takes place on Sunday. The race organizer, New York Road Runners (NYRR), says its official mobile app offers live runner tracking, leaderboards and professional-race coverage. Unlike an athlete's actual GPS broadcast, race tracking is based on recorded checkpoints and projected progress; do not treat a moving marker as a continuously verified street-level position. NYRR's race guide explicitly describes the live information as unofficial.

November 1 results update: Sweden’s Andreas Almgren won the men’s race in 2:06:01 in his marathon debut, while Kenya’s Sheila Chepkirui won the women’s race in 2:24:36. American Jessica McClain finished second among women in 2:24:42. See the race results and the distinction between live tracking and official records below.

2026 NYC Marathon winners and elite results

NEW YORK, November 1 — Almgren finished 13 seconds ahead of Italy’s Yemaneberhan Crippa (2:06:14); Lesotho’s Kamohelo Mofolo was third (2:06:26). Chepkirui finished six seconds ahead of American Jessica McClain (2:24:42), with Kenya’s Hellen Obiri third (2:24:43). The elite finishing times were independently reported by Reuters, Associated Press and NBC Sports. For searchable participant records and any later corrections, consult the official NYRR results database.

McClain’s runner-up finish is particularly relevant for U.S. readers: she passed Obiri in the closing stages to secure an American women’s podium place. In the wheelchair divisions, Marcel Hug won the men’s race and Catherine Debrunner the women’s race, as reported by the Associated Press. Race-day media times should be treated as reported results until reconciled against NYRR’s official database.

How to track a runner in the 2026 NYC Marathon

Open the official NYRR marathon app page and choose the App Store or Google Play download link. In the app, navigate to the 2026 TCS New York City Marathon and its runner-tracking feature. Search for the person you are following using the name or identifying information supported by the current app, then follow their recorded progress and any available alerts. The organizer confirms the app provides key-moment updates and race leaderboards. If two participants have similar names, verify the runner's bib or other details with them before assuming a match.

Runner tracking can lag, especially around crowded areas or between timing checkpoints. A predicted finish time is an estimate, not a promise of where the runner is at a particular second. NYRR advises families to agree on a meeting location outside the runners-only finish area because cellular networks may be saturated near Central Park. For someone you intend to meet, use both the runner's expected pace and official post-finish instructions rather than relying on a single app dot.

  • 8:02 a.m. — women's professional wheelchair division.

These are scheduled starts, not proof that an individual crossed the starting mat at that exact moment. The five wave times come from NYRR's official start guide, which cautions that schedules can change. Professional start times are also listed in LetsRun's October 31 race-day guide.

Where to find official results, and what is provisional?

NYRR distinguishes live, unofficial runner tracking during the event from official results posted after it concludes. Use results.nyrr.org for the official finishing records once published. A leaderboard or unofficial split shown while the race is in progress should not be reported as a certified final result. The elite race winners and reported times are summarized above with independent source attribution; NYRR remains the authoritative source for final searchable participant records.

For spectators comparing runners in different waves, note that a finish-clock time and a runner's net race time are not necessarily the same. NYRR states that a participant's net time starts when they cross the starting line. A late-wave participant may have a faster net result than someone who reaches the finish earlier on the wall clock.

How to watch the NYC Marathon live in the United States

NYRR's October 26 broadcast announcement lists ESPN2 for national television coverage and ABC7/WABC-TV for local New York-area coverage. The official NYRR app also carries uninterrupted feeds of the four professional divisions. Availability may depend on device, provider and region; consult the broadcaster's current schedule before subscribing to a service solely for this event.

The 26.2-mile course connects Staten Island, Brooklyn, Queens, the Bronx and Manhattan before ending in Central Park. This year's edition marks 50 years since the marathon adopted its five-borough format in 1976, according to NYRR. The milestone is about the five-borough course, not the first-ever New York City Marathon, which began in 1970.

What if the tracker stops updating?

First check whether your runner's assigned wave has started. Then refresh the app or wait for the next checkpoint; missing a fresh split does not, by itself, mean the runner has stopped. If the app is unavailable, the official marathon site links to organizer resources and coverage. After the race, use the official results database rather than an unsourced social-media screenshot. If you are meeting a participant, NYRR recommends allowing roughly 15–25 minutes after the expected finish to reach the family-reunion area, and choosing a meeting spot in advance.
