Sharper Image Fire Pit Recall: Refund and Models

A federal recall covers two Sharper Image-branded tabletop fire pits after serious burn injuries. Here is how to identify the units, document them and apply for the full refund.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · November 12, 2026 · Source-linked desk reporting · Representative photograph

This article summarizes official recall documentation, not first-hand product testing. Research and drafting may use AI-assisted tools. See our editorial and corrections policy.

Representative licensed photograph of an unrelated decorative outdoor fire pit, not the recalled Sharper Image products. Photo: Gary Saamanen / Pexels.

Quick answer: Allstar Marketing Group recalled about 265,000 Sharper Image tabletop fire pits on November 12, 2026, after reports of burning fuel escaping and causing severe injuries. Stop using the affected fire pits immediately. The remedy is a full refund through the official recall registration portal. CPSC recall 27-082 gives the identifying details.

Which Sharper Image fire pits are recalled?

The recall includes two outdoor tabletop products sold under the Sharper Image name: the Sharper Image Tabletop Fire Pit and the Sharper Image Tabletop Infinity Fire Pit. The first has a small cylindrical concrete body, an open burning chamber and a removable snuffing lid. The Infinity version has a steel base with tempered-glass side panels. The CPSC identifies a “77” logo on the front, a lot-code sticker at the center of the base, and a round black “CHINA” sticker bearing a five-digit code, the letters KS-CY or KS-FC, and a YYYY-MM-DD date code. Compare all markings and photographs with the official CPSC notice rather than relying on the product name alone.

Why did the tabletop fire pits get recalled?

The federal agency says ignited flammable liquid can escape from the units or cause a flame jet from a fuel container. Allstar received four reports of ignited fuel escaping during use, with four injuries that included third- and fourth-degree burns to multiple body parts. The agency describes this as a burn and fire hazard. Do not attempt a home repair, refill the unit for testing or keep using it while a refund request is pending.

How to request the full Sharper Image fire pit refund

According to the recall instructions, owners should stop using the product and register at firepitrecall.expertinquiry.com. The process requires marking the product “RECALLED – DO NOT USE” in permanent marker and providing three photographs following the instructions on the official recall page. After registration is confirmed, dispose of remaining fuel according to local hazardous-waste requirements, then discard the recalled fire pit safely as directed. Do not burn off leftover fuel as a shortcut. The CPSC describes the remedy as a full refund, not a repair or replacement.

For help, contact Allstar at 855-215-5032, Monday–Friday, 9 a.m.–5 p.m. Eastern Time, or email allstarmarketing6861@sedgwick.com. These contacts are listed by the CPSC; NewsTrendus does not process refunds or collect registration information.

Where and when were the recalled fire pits sold?

The CPSC lists sales from January 2023 through June 2026 at prices between $30 and $50. Retailers included Ace Hardware, Burlington, Home Depot, Lowe’s, Kohl’s, Marshalls, Nordstrom, Rite Aid, Target, TJ Maxx, Walgreens and Walmart, as well as other stores nationwide. Online sales included Amazon.com and HSN.com. A purchase at one of those retailers does not automatically establish that an item is included: inspect the design and identifying markings.

What should owners do next?

Keep children and visitors away from the unused product, photograph the identifying stickers, and follow the refund registration process. Do not sell or donate a recalled fire pit. Because the recall includes instructions for handling leftover flammable fuel, check local hazardous-waste guidance before disposal. The notice identifies Allstar Marketing Group LLC of Winston-Salem, North Carolina as the importer and China as the country of manufacture. If a product looks similar but has different markings, consult the CPSC images and contact the recall administrator for clarification.
