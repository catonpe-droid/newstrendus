BMW Recall: 720,796 Vehicles, Park-Outside Warning

A fresh U.S. recall calls for some BMW owners to park outside while a revised water-pump electrical connector remedy is developed.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · November 13, 2026 · Source-linked analysis

This is a source-linked desk explainer, not first-hand reporting. Research and drafting may use AI-assisted tools. Follow the cited source links and read our editorial and corrections policy.

Representative licensed photograph of a car interior; not a recalled BMW, the defective connector or a fire scene. Photo: Erik Mclean / Pexels.

Quick answer: BMW is recalling 720,796 U.S. vehicles because a water-pump electrical connector may short circuit and cause a fire, according to Reuters' November 13 report citing NHTSA. Owners of affected vehicles are advised to park outside, away from buildings and other cars, until repairs are completed. Check the exact vehicle using the official NHTSA VIN lookup or BMW's recall lookup.

Which BMW models are included in the November 2026 recall?

Reuters reports that the campaign includes various model years of the BMW 2 Series, 3 Series, 4 Series and 5 Series, along with the X1, X3, X4, X5 xDrive40e and Z4. The model-year ranges differ: examples include 2012–2016 3 and 5 Series, 2012–2015 X1, 2013–2017 X3 and 2016–2018 X5 xDrive40e. A model name alone is not enough to establish whether a particular car is affected; the 17-character vehicle identification number is the decisive check.

This newly reported campaign concerns vehicles previously recalled in 2024 for a related water-pump electrical connector issue. A BMW owner notice for the 2024 recall describes how liquid reaching the connector could produce a short circuit or fire. The new November 2026 action should not be confused with BMW's separate 2025 starter-relay recall, which involved different vehicles and a different component.

Why is NHTSA telling owners to park outside?

The safety concern is an improperly sealed water-pump electrical connector that can be exposed to moisture. According to Reuters, NHTSA and BMW have identified 10 potentially related fires after vehicles received the earlier recall repair. That figure is not a count of injuries or a prediction that every recalled car will catch fire.

The park-outside recommendation is a precaution while BMW develops another fix. Do not assume that a previous repair closes this new recall. Follow the latest manufacturer notice for your VIN, even if your car was serviced under the 2024 campaign.

How to check your VIN and arrange a free recall repair

  1. Locate your 17-character VIN on the lower driver's-side windshield, registration or insurance card.
  2. Enter it at NHTSA.gov/recalls or BMW's official recall page.
  3. Review the open campaign and any interim safety directions; save the recall number for reference.
  4. Contact an authorized BMW dealer to confirm whether a remedy is available and how to schedule the work. BMW says safety recall repairs are free at authorized centers.
  5. If the newly announced recall does not yet appear, check again: NHTSA warns that some recent campaigns take time to populate all affected VINs.

BMW's official customer-service number is 800-525-7417. NHTSA's Vehicle Safety Hotline is 888-327-4236. Avoid third-party sites that charge for a basic recall check.

Is the repair ready, and what happens next?

As of the November 13 report, BMW was still developing the additional remedy. The company had not established a confirmed repair-completion date in the reporting reviewed for this article. Owners should not infer that the previous water-pump repair resolves the new concern. Keep the vehicle outside and away from structures as directed while seeking official instructions.

If smoke appears from the engine compartment, do not continue driving. Move occupants to a safe location and call emergency services when appropriate; the earlier BMW recall notice also provides emergency and roadside-assistance guidance.

How this recall differs from other vehicle safety alerts

The specific issue here is fire risk at a water-pump connector, not the steering-assist software concern in our Honda recall explainer.
