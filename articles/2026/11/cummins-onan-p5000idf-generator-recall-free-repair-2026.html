Cummins Generator Recall: P5000iDF Free Repair Steps

A federal safety recall covers one Cummins dual-fuel portable generator model. Owners should compare the model and part number on the side label before arranging the no-cost repair.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · November 14, 2026 · Source-linked desk reporting · Representative photograph

This is a source-linked consumer explainer, not first-hand product testing. AI-assisted drafting may be used. See our editorial and corrections policy.

Representative licensed photograph of an unrelated portable generator; not the recalled Cummins unit or a reported incident. Photo: Abdulkadir muhammad sani / Pexels.

Quick answer: On November 12, 2026, the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission announced a recall of about 2,960 Cummins Onan P5000iDF EFI dual-fuel portable generators. An incorrectly installed valve can prevent fuel vapors from venting, creating excess pressure and a potential fire or burn hazard. Stop using an affected generator immediately and check the official CPSC recall 27-087 and Cummins campaign 7308 for a free repair.

Which Cummins generators are included?

The recalled product is the Cummins Onan P5000iDF EFI dual-fuel portable generator, sold in dark gray and designed to run on gasoline or propane. Output ranges from approximately 4,500 to 5,000 watts depending on the fuel. The side of the machine displays the Cummins C logo and “Onan P5000iDF EFI”; the model and part number are printed on a label on the side. Not every Cummins generator is covered. Check the precise part number using the company's official campaign lookup, rather than relying only on the product's appearance.

Why is the generator being recalled?

According to CPSC, an incorrectly installed valve can stop gasoline-tank vapors from venting properly. Pressure may build inside the tank, allowing fuel to discharge or the cap to eject unexpectedly. This can create a fire and burn hazard, even when a generator appears to operate normally. The company reported 11 incidents involving fuel discharge or cap ejection, including one person splashed in the face with fuel; CPSC says that person did not need medical attention. These are the incidents reported as of the November 12 announcement, not a prediction of future outcomes.

Where and when were affected generators sold?

CPSC lists Camping World, Factory Pure, Northern Tool, AP Electric and Cummins.com among sellers. Sales ran from May 2025 through August 2026, with listed prices between $1,640 and $1,930. The units were manufactured in Vietnam and distributed by Cummins Inc. of Columbus, Indiana. A receipt can help identify a purchase, but the machine's model and part-number label is essential for determining recall eligibility.

How to get the free Cummins generator repair

Stop operating the generator while checking whether it is affected. First, locate the side label and compare its model and part number with Cummins recall campaign 7308. If included, contact an authorized Cummins service location to schedule the free repair at the service location. The manufacturer directs owners to its QuickServe service locator. You can also contact Cummins at 800-286-6467. The published remedy is a repair, not a general cash refund or an owner-installed kit.

What should owners do next?

Keep the generator out of service until its recall status is confirmed and any required repair is completed. Do not loosen a fuel cap or attempt a valve repair yourself as a substitute for the official process. Review the CPSC notice and photographs for the identifying label. For other, separate November 2026 consumer-safety actions, see our QIOMALA pressure washer recall and Huffy e-trike recall; their refund and repair instructions do not apply to this generator.
