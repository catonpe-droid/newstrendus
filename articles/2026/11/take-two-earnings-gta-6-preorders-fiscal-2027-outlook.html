Take-Two Earnings: GTA 6 Preorders, Revenue and Outlook

Take-Two Interactive reported $1.60 billion in fiscal second-quarter net bookings on November 9, down 18% from a year earlier. The company still expects $8.0 billion to $8.2 billion in full-year bookings as Grand Theft Auto VI approaches its November 19 launch.

Quick answer: Take-Two's quarter was weaker on bookings, but its GAAP loss narrowed. Management kept its fiscal 2027 bookings target at $8.0–$8.2 billion and highlighted unusually strong GTA VI preorders. Those are separate indicators: current-quarter performance is reported history, while the annual target and game launch remain forward-looking.

What did Take-Two report in its November 9 earnings?

In the quarter ended September 30, 2026, Take-Two reported net bookings of $1.60 billion, compared with $1.96 billion a year earlier, an 18% decline. GAAP net revenue was $1.50 billion, down from $1.77 billion. Its GAAP net loss narrowed to $54.0 million (29 cents per share), from $133.9 million (73 cents per share) in the year-earlier quarter. These figures come directly from the company's November 9 fiscal second-quarter release.

The distinction between revenue and bookings matters. Take-Two defines net bookings as the net value of products and services sold digitally or sold in physically during a period, including certain licensing and in-game purchases. That operational metric is not identical to revenue recognized under accounting rules. A reader comparing headlines should check which measure is being quoted.

How much of Take-Two's business is repeat spending?

Recurrent consumer spending, which includes virtual currency, add-on content and in-game purchases, accounted for 81% of quarterly net bookings, according to Take-Two. That proportion shows how important established games and ongoing engagement are even before a major new release. The company said recurrent spending bookings decreased 9% year over year; a large share of the total does not mean that spending grew.

The quarter's principal contributors included NBA 2K, existing Grand Theft Auto titles, Toon Blast, Match Factory!, Empires & Puzzles and Borderlands. That portfolio context helps explain why investors examine both GTA VI launch expectations and the performance of current franchises.

What is the fiscal 2027 outlook, and what does GTA VI change?

Take-Two reiterated a full-year $8.0 billion to $8.2 billion net bookings forecast for the fiscal year ending March 31, 2027. Management said GTA VI preorders were reaching unprecedented levels, while Reuters' November 9 report noted that the company did not increase its bookings forecast before the game's launch. The forecast is management guidance, not money already earned.

GTA VI remains scheduled for November 19, 2026 in the company's earnings release. A launch on that date would fall in Take-Two's fiscal third quarter. Investors and players should distinguish an announced release date from confirmation that a launch has occurred. For the prior schedule and storage questions, see NewsTrendus' GTA VI release-date explainer and GTA VI storage and preload coverage; dated details there should be checked against the newest official notices.

What should investors and players watch next?

Three milestones matter more than speculation: the actual November 19 launch, subsequent disclosures about launch-period sales or bookings, and Take-Two's next reported quarterly results. The November 9 release does not establish final first-week sales, guaranteed preorder conversions or a future share price. The company's own financial release includes risk warnings about release timing, market acceptance, competition and operating conditions.

For US consumers, the practical distinction is straightforward: company financial guidance is not a change to a customer's purchase terms. Buyers should verify their platform's official storefront for product availability, storage requirements, refund rules and any edition-specific pricing instead of relying on market forecasts or viral rumors.
