Honda Recalls 635,808 US Vehicles: How to Check Your VIN

A newly reported power-steering software recall raises an immediate question for Honda owners: is my vehicle affected?

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · November 10, 2026 · Source-linked analysis

Quick answer: Honda is recalling 635,808 vehicles in the United States over an issue involving power-steering assist software, according to Reuters' November 10 report citing the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration. Owners can check for open recalls with their vehicle identification number (VIN) using NHTSA's official recall lookup. This report does not establish the affected model-year list or repair timetable.

What has been confirmed about Honda's steering recall?

The reported recall covers 635,808 U.S. vehicles and concerns software associated with steering assistance. That is the confirmed scope in the available November 10 reporting. The number describes potentially affected vehicles, not a count of crashes, injuries or confirmed steering failures. The available report does not provide a model-by-model breakdown, recall campaign number, owner-notification date or exact dealer remedy, so none is asserted here.

A steering-assist software issue should not be confused with Honda's earlier, separate steering-gearbox recalls. A previous campaign affecting some Civic, CR-V and HR-V vehicles involved mechanical gearbox components; it is not evidence that those models are included in this newly reported software recall.

How do I check whether my Honda is recalled?

  1. Find the 17-character VIN on the driver's side of the dashboard near the windshield, on the registration or on the insurance card.
  2. Open the official NHTSA VIN recall lookup and enter the VIN; do not use an unrelated third-party form that requests payment.
  3. Review any open recall result and its remedy status. Save the campaign number if one appears.
  4. Contact an authorized Honda dealer for confirmation and scheduling instructions, particularly if the newly announced recall is not yet reflected in the database.

NHTSA cautions that some newly announced recalls may not immediately show every affected VIN. Therefore a temporary “0 unrepaired recalls” result is not proof that a very recent campaign can never apply. Recheck and follow manufacturer notices.

Is the repair free, and should owners keep driving?

Under U.S. safety-recall rules, manufacturers generally must provide an appropriate remedy without charge for covered vehicles. However, the exact repair, software update process and availability for this particular campaign were not established in the initial report. Owners should not assume a repair date or perform their own steering software modification.

If the steering feels unusually heavy, a steering warning illuminates or vehicle control seems impaired, stop in a safe location and seek qualified assistance. This is general safety guidance, not a claim that every vehicle in the reported recall exhibits those symptoms. Follow any specific instructions from Honda or NHTSA once the official notice is available.

What information should Honda owners watch for next?

The next useful facts are the NHTSA campaign identifier, affected makes and model years, production dates, risk description, recall notification schedule and dealer remedy. Those details determine whether a specific car is included and what its owner should do. Check the official record rather than treating an unverified social-media model list as definitive.
