US Stocks Rally Nov. 9 as Oil Falls; Nvidia Lags

Wall Street rebounded on Monday after a punishing week for AI-related stocks. Falling crude prices helped broad market sentiment, but Nvidia did not join the rally.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · November 9, 2026 · Source-linked market analysis

This is a source-linked desk explainer, not firsthand trading-floor reporting. AI-assisted drafting may be used; review the cited reporting and our editorial and corrections policy.

Representative licensed photograph of financial paperwork, not a photo of the November 9 market session. Photographer: Leeloo The First / Pexels.

Quick answer: On November 9, 2026, the Dow Jones Industrial Average gained approximately 2.2%, the S&P 500 about 2.4%, and the Nasdaq Composite roughly 2.6%–2.7%. The rally followed sharp losses the prior week and coincided with a drop in oil prices. Nvidia fell despite gains in many other technology shares, according to AP and Reuters.

How did US stocks perform on November 9?

Major U.S. stock indexes finished sharply higher on Monday. Reuters reported gains of 2.21% for the Dow, 2.41% for the S&P 500 and 2.67% for the Nasdaq. AP rounded those moves to 2.2%, 2.4% and 2.6%, respectively. The difference is rounding, not conflicting accounts of separate trading sessions.

All 11 S&P 500 sectors advanced, according to Reuters. That breadth is important: the move was not simply a rebound in a handful of AI companies. Industrials and consumer discretionary stocks were among the strongest groups.

Why did falling oil prices help the stock market?

AP reported Brent crude declined 4.7% to $93.73 per barrel. Lower oil prices can ease near-term fuel-cost pressure on airlines, cruise operators and other energy-intensive businesses, although a one-day commodity move does not establish that consumer prices will fall. AP identified United Airlines and Carnival among companies benefiting from the change in sentiment.

The oil retreat also gave investors a reason to reconsider inflation risks after a volatile week. But the relationship is not automatic: oil markets can reverse quickly, and monetary-policy decisions depend on broader evidence than crude alone.

Why did Nvidia fall while other chip stocks rose?

Nvidia was an outlier. AP reported a 5.4% decline, while Reuters described a drop of roughly 6%, reflecting differences in timing or rounding in market coverage. Reporting linked the selloff to questions surrounding an investigation involving the company's chief executive. Those reports do not establish that Nvidia's products, customer contracts or systems were compromised; AP said the company had no reason to believe its security or trade secrets had been affected.

Other semiconductor names rebounded strongly. Reuters reported AMD gained about 13% and Broadcom nearly 9%, while the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index rose more than 6%. Investors should not treat a sector-wide bounce as evidence that every company faces the same risks.

Does the rally erase last week's AI selloff?

No. The previous Thursday's AP-documented decline was severe: the S&P 500 lost about 4.4% and the Nasdaq 5.5% in that session. AP said that even after Monday's rebound, the S&P 500 remained about 3.4% below its record high. Recovering some losses is not the same as returning to a previous peak.

For readers tracking the AI infrastructure debate, our existing report on used gas turbines and data-center power constraints explains a different underlying issue: physical capacity and equipment lead times. A market rally does not resolve those operational bottlenecks.

What should US investors watch next?

Investors are awaiting new inflation readings and corporate results. Reuters identified the October consumer-price report scheduled for Tuesday and the producer-price report expected Friday as near-term catalysts. Upcoming company earnings may offer more direct evidence about AI infrastructure demand and profitability than daily price swings.

The practical distinction is between three separate signals: market prices show what investors paid on a particular day; economic releases measure conditions such as inflation; and company filings and earnings show business performance. None alone predicts the next trading session.
