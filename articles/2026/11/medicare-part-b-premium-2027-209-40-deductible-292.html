2027 Medicare Part B Premium: $209.40 and Deductible Changes

The 2027 Medicare Part B premium rises to $209.40 and the deductible to $292. Compare 2026 costs, income surcharges and enrollment steps.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · November 18, 2026 · CMS-source analysis · Representative photograph

This is a source-linked desk explainer, not firsthand reporting. AI-assisted research and drafting may be used. Read our editorial and corrections policy.

Representative licensed photograph; not an image of the CMS announcement. Photo: Tima Miroshnichenko / Pexels.

Quick answer: Medicare's standard Part B premium will be $209.40 a month in 2027, up $6.50 from $202.90 in 2026. The annual Part B deductible rises $9 to $292, according to CMS's November 17 announcement. Higher-income enrollees may owe additional premiums.

How much does Medicare Part B cost in 2027?

The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) November 17 fact sheet sets the standard monthly premium at $209.40. Part B helps cover physician visits, outpatient hospital services, durable medical equipment and certain other medical services. CMS attributes the increases mainly to projected growth in health-care spending.

The annual premium comparison assumes enrollment for all 12 months at the standard rate; it excludes income-related adjustments, other coverage costs and any assistance programs.

Who pays more than the standard Part B premium?

Some beneficiaries pay an income-related monthly adjustment amount (IRMAA) in addition to the standard premium. The exact surcharge depends on modified adjusted gross income and filing status. CMS published the 2027 income brackets and amounts in its official tables. Do not assume the standard $209.40 rate is your total bill if you have received an IRMAA notice. Verify the income category and any appeal rights with Social Security.

What about Medicare Part A hospital costs?

CMS also set the 2027 Part A inpatient hospital deductible at $1,776, compared with $1,736 in 2026, a $40 increase. Approximately 99% of beneficiaries do not pay a monthly Part A premium because they meet the required work-history conditions. The hospital deductible is separate from Part B's annual deductible and may apply by benefit period, not as one annual charge.

Does the increase change Medicare Advantage or Part D prices?

No single Part B announcement establishes the full cost of every Medicare Advantage or prescription-drug plan. Private plan premiums, drug formularies, networks and out-of-pocket charges vary. CMS separately published its 2027 Medicare Advantage and Part D outlook. Compare the actual plans available in your ZIP code rather than relying on national averages.

What should beneficiaries do before December 7?

Medicare's annual open enrollment period runs from October 15 through December 7, 2026, for changes generally effective January 1, 2027. Review the Annual Notice of Change, check your prescriptions and clinicians against the next year's plan, and use Medicare's official Plan Compare tool. Open enrollment is not a requirement to accept the standard Part B premium change; eligibility and enrollment circumstances differ.

If your Part B premium is deducted from Social Security, review your benefit notice for your actual net payment rather than subtracting $6.50 from an estimated cost-of-living increase. Individual circumstances and income adjustments matter.

Why this matters for U.S. readers

For someone budgeting on the standard Part B rate, the premium change alone adds $78 over a full year. A beneficiary who meets the Part B deductible would also face a $9 higher deductible, but actual medical spending varies. Independent reporting by Quartz corroborates the CMS premium and deductible figures. This is a cost comparison, not personalized insurance or medical advice.
