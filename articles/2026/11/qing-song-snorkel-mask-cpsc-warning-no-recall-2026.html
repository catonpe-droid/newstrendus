Qing Song Snorkel Mask Warning: No Recall or Refund

The CPSC's November 12 warning covers certain discontinued Qing Song full-face snorkel masks sold online from January 2019 through August 2026. A distinctive shark-fin snorkel tube helps owners identify them.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · November 17, 2026 · Source-linked desk reporting · Representative photograph

This is a source-linked consumer safety explainer, not first-hand reporting. Research and drafting may use AI-assisted tools. See our editorial policy.

Representative licensed coastal photograph; it does not show the product or a reported incident. Photo: David Dragan / Pexels.

Quick answer: The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission urges owners of certain Qing Song full-face snorkel masks to stop using them immediately and dispose of them. This is a federal safety warning, not a manufacturer-agreed recall; the agency has not announced a refund or replacement program.

Which Qing Song snorkel masks are covered?

According to the CPSC warning dated November 12, 2026, affected masks were offered on Amazon.com and TikTok.com between January 2019 and August 2026. They have a full-face glass viewing screen, a breathing section covering the nose and mouth, and a snorkel tube rising from the top center. The tube has a distinctive shark-fin-shaped feature. The masks come in multiple colors.

The notice does not provide a definitive list of model or serial numbers. Owners should compare their mask with the agency's official photographs and description rather than assume a similar-looking mask is either included or safe. The warning is specific to certain Qing Song products; it does not establish that every full-face snorkel mask from every brand is subject to this notice.

What incidents prompted the warning?

The CPSC says it knows of eight incident reports involving acute breathing difficulties, dizziness or loss of consciousness while people used the masks; one person was hospitalized. The agency warns that these masks can contribute to labored breathing, excess fluid in the lungs and loss of consciousness, creating a drowning hazard. It also identifies increased carbon dioxide levels as a concern. These are reported incidents and regulatory safety findings, not a claim that eight deaths occurred.

Independent specialist outlet Divernet reported on November 16 on the warning and highlighted the difficulty of recognizing the shark-fin feature. Readers should use the CPSC's product images for identification, not the representative coastal photo above.

Why is this a warning rather than a recall?

The CPSC states that seller Shenzhen Songxin Silicone Products Co., Ltd. has not agreed to an acceptable recall. Therefore, the November 12 announcement is a government safety warning, not a company-operated recall with a confirmed remedy. The agency has not specified a refund, replacement or repair offer. Consumers should not assume a refund deadline or claim form exists.

What should US consumers do now?

Stop using the affected mask immediately. Check the brand, the shark-fin tube shape and the sales period against the official CPSC notice. The agency directs owners to dispose of the hazardous masks and not sell or give them away. That matters even if a mask has been stored unused since a past vacation.

If an owner experienced a safety incident, they can submit a product report through SaferProducts.gov. Someone experiencing breathing difficulty or loss of consciousness in the water needs immediate emergency assistance; this article is not a substitute for emergency medical care.

What is confirmed, and what remains unclear?

Confirmed: the November 12 warning, eight reported incidents, one hospitalization, the shark-fin identification feature, the online sales window and the seller's lack of agreement to an acceptable recall. Not established in the public notice: a precise total number of masks sold, a refund program or a model-by-model exemption list. The agency's warning should be consulted for later changes.
