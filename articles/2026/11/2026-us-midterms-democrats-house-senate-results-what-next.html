2026 US Midterms: Democrats Win House and Senate, but Several Races Remain Uncalled

Democrats won the House and Senate in the 2026 US midterms. What is confirmed, which races remain open and what changes when Congress convenes.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · November 5, 2026 · Source-linked reporting · Representative photograph

Quick answer: Democrats won the House and Senate in the 2026 US midterms. What is confirmed, which races remain open and what changes when Congress convenes.

What did the November 3 elections decide?

Democrats secured control of the U.S. House of Representatives and Senate in the November 3, 2026 midterm elections, according to election reporting by the Associated Press and Reuters. The outcome gives the party majorities in both chambers of the incoming Congress, while President Donald Trump remains in office. This is a congressional result, not a presidential election, and changing control of the legislature does not by itself enact any new law.

The immediate consequence is a change in who sets congressional calendars, chairs committees and decides which proposals receive hearings. Exactly how each chamber uses that authority will depend on lawmakers and the final membership of each caucus.

Why are several races still uncalled?

A projected winner and a certified election result are different. Reported leads can change as absentee, provisional and other legally eligible votes are processed. News organizations may call a race once the remaining vote cannot reasonably alter the outcome, but state election officials administer and certify the count.

Alaska's closely contested Senate race between Mary Peltola and incumbent Dan Sullivan remained unresolved in initial November 4 reports. A narrow margin is a reason to await further counting, not evidence of an irregularity.

What changes when Congress meets?

With majorities in both chambers, Democrats can organize committees, lead investigations, advance legislation and exercise oversight of the administration. Proposed legislation still must move through the required congressional process and, in most cases, reach the president for signature or face a veto. Depending on Senate rules, a majority alone might not be enough to move every measure.

For households and businesses following taxes, spending, federal programs and regulation, the useful next step is to track actual bills, hearings, votes and enacted policies rather than assume an election-night headline settles policy details.

When will the final numbers be known?

Certification deadlines and remaining-ballot procedures vary by state, so there is no universal hour at which every 2026 contest becomes final. Follow state election authorities for official canvasses and reputable vote trackers for projected outcomes. We will distinguish a newly called race from a certified result when reporting further updates.

The incoming Congress convenes in January 2027. Until then, the current Congress continues to serve under its existing membership and procedures.
