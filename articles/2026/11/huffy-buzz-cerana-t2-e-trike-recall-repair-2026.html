Huffy Buzz E-Trike Recall: Free Repair and $100 Reimbursement

Huffy recalls 286 Buzz Cerana T2 E-Trikes after wheel failures and injuries. Check model E4004 and learn how to request a free repair.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · November 16, 2026 (U.S. Eastern) · Independently written from credited sources · Representative editorial photograph

This is a source-linked desk explainer, not first-hand reporting. Research and drafting may use AI-assisted tools. Follow the cited source links and read our editorial and corrections policy.

Representative licensed photograph, not a photo of the reported event or actual subject. Photo: Team EVELO / Pexels (Pexels free-use license).

At a glance: On November 12, 2026, the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission announced that Huffy is recalling about 286 Buzz Bicycles Cerana T2 electric tricycles. Rear wheels may loosen or detach. Owners should stop riding affected trikes and contact Huffy for free replacement rear axle assemblies.

Which Buzz electric tricycles are recalled?

The CPSC recall notice 27-086 covers the Buzz Bicycles Cerana T2 E-Trike, model E4004. The affected pedal-assist tricycle is gray, has BUZZ logos on both sides, a 500-watt motor, a 48-volt battery and three 24-inch wheels. Look for the model-number label near the pedals, on the lower part of the frame downtube. Compare it with the agency's actual product images rather than the representative photograph accompanying this article.

What failed, and were riders injured?

According to the federal notice, the rear wheels can loosen or separate from the rear frame, creating a fall hazard. Huffy reported 12 incidents involving loose or detached rear wheels and three injuries, including shoulder and wrist sprains, leg bruises and abrasions. These are the figures reported at the recall announcement; they should not be interpreted as an estimate of all incidents.

How to obtain the free axle repair

Stop using an affected E4004 tricycle immediately. Contact Huffy at the official Buzz recall page, email service@huffy.com or call 855-430-6240, Monday through Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Eastern. Huffy will provide free replacement rear axle assemblies directly to consumers or to a third party performing the repair. The company says it will reimburse up to $100 for third-party repair charges. Confirm reimbursement documentation and repair arrangements with Huffy before paying a mechanic. The recall does not announce a blanket cash refund for the purchase price.

Where and when were these trikes sold?

CPSC says the recalled tricycles were sold at independent bicycle shops across the United States and online at BuzzBicycles.com between November 2024 and July 2026, for approximately $1,400 to $2,500. A purchase outside that period is not, by itself, proof that a trike is safe or recalled; check the model label and ask Huffy if uncertain. The importer is Huffy Corporation of Miamisburg, Ohio, and the agency lists China as the manufacturing country.

What should owners do next?

Record the model label, keep purchase or service documentation where available, and arrange the recall remedy before resuming use. Do not attempt to determine axle safety merely by a quick visual check or a short test ride; the official remedy is replacement of the rear axle assembly. For any changes to the remedy, consult CPSC's dated recall notice and the manufacturer's recall page.

Related consumer safety coverage

Other recent CPSC actions cover different hazards and products, including the Cummins generator repair recall, the Kakgold toy refund recall, and the Boshengya rug fire-hazard recall. Each has separate eligibility and remedy instructions.
