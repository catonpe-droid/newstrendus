2027 Opportunity Zones Map: 6,576 Tracts Designated

The new federal Opportunity Zone map is final, but the 2027 designations are not effective yet. Here is what investors, developers and local residents can verify before January 1.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · November 9, 2026 · IRS/Treasury source review · Representative photograph

Quick answer: The Treasury Department and IRS announced on November 5 that 6,576 census tracts have been designated as Qualified Opportunity Zones for the next ten-year cycle. 2,594 are classified as rural. The new designations take effect January 1, 2027; a tract's appearance on the list does not mean every project or investor automatically receives a tax benefit.

What changed in the 2027 Opportunity Zones map?

The IRS announcement IR-2026-132 confirms that all 6,576 nominated tracts across the 50 states, District of Columbia and territories were designated. The agency identifies 2,594 of those as rural, where additional investment incentives may apply under the 2025 tax law. This is a finalized designation list, rather than the much larger preliminary pool of eligible tracts published in April.

The American Bankers Association's November 5 report independently confirms the announcement and explains that governors, and the D.C. mayor, nominated qualifying areas. The final selection is geographically specific: a city's name alone is not enough to establish that an address qualifies.

When do the new zones start, and how long do they last?

The new designations begin January 1, 2027 and remain designated for ten years. That is a designation period, not a promise of a ten-year investment return. Treasury and IRS established a process for selecting new tracts every decade. The April IRS nomination guidance describes the selection criteria and nomination framework.

How to check whether an address is in a 2027 Opportunity Zone

  1. Start with the official IRS release, which links to the full designation list and map.
  2. Identify the precise census tract for the property, rather than relying on ZIP code or city boundaries.
  3. Match the tract to the final 2027 designation list, not an earlier eligible or nominated list.
  4. Check the rural classification in the official data before evaluating any rural-specific treatment.
  5. Confirm the applicable investment, fund and tax-year rules with qualified tax counsel before committing funds.

The designation itself does not grant a homeowner a refund, change property-tax rates or guarantee financing. An investment must satisfy separate federal tax rules to qualify for favorable treatment.

Why 6,576 is different from 25,332

The IRS identified 25,332 eligible tracts in April, including 8,334 rural tracts. Those were potential candidates, not the final map. After the nomination process and additional eligible-tract nominations, the final designation count was 6,576, including 2,594 rural tracts. Confusing eligibility with final designation could lead readers to the wrong address or tax conclusion.

For context, HousingWire's November 6 coverage discusses the housing-development implications while distinguishing the new map from actual investment outcomes. Treasury says the original program has attracted more than $110 billion in private investment since its 2017 creation; that historical total is not a forecast for the newly designated tracts.

What should local businesses and investors do next?

Businesses considering projects can verify census-tract boundaries, determine whether a Qualified Opportunity Fund structure is relevant, and model financing and tax risks separately. Community members can use the official map to understand whether a neighborhood is designated, but designation alone does not establish that new jobs or housing will arrive. Future investment announcements should be checked individually.
