Costco Whipped Cream Recall: Check Lot Codes in 12 States

One batch of Kirkland Signature Real Heavy Whipped Cream was voluntarily recalled after Costco shoppers reported a chemical taste or smell. A precise can-date and timestamp check identifies the affected product.

Quick answer: Costco and manufacturer Alamance Foods issued a voluntary notice dated November 7, 2026, for a specific batch of Kirkland Signature Real Heavy Whipped Cream sold in 12 U.S. states. Look for item 1777000, a best-if-used-by date of April 2, 2027, and a printed can timestamp from 00:35 through 02:45. Do not use an affected can; return it to Costco for a full refund. The Costco recall notices page is the primary source.

How to check whether your whipped cream is recalled

The affected product is the 15-ounce (425-gram) red-and-white can of Kirkland Signature Real Heavy Whipped Cream, Costco item #1777000 and supplier item code AC50. The recall notice describes an eight-count case of three-packs. Match the best-if-used-by date and the printed time on the can, rather than relying on the brand or package appearance alone.

The notice concerns one batch, not every Kirkland whipped cream can. A matching date without a timestamp in the specified range does not by itself establish that the can is part of this recall. Consult the retailer's posted notice if markings are unclear.

Which 12 states sold the recalled batch?

The reported warehouse distribution covers Alabama, Florida, Georgia, Iowa, Illinois, Indiana, Missouri, Mississippi, North Carolina, South Carolina, Tennessee and Wisconsin. The affected sale window was October 11 through October 31, 2026. A buyer who carried the product across state lines should still check the can identifiers; the list describes reported retail distribution, not where a product might be consumed.

Why was the whipped cream recalled?

Alamance Foods said one batch may contain a small amount of a cleaning chemical after customers described an unusual chemical taste and smell. The company did not identify the specific chemical in the publicly reported notice. At the time of the November 7 announcement, it said no serious adverse effects requiring medical care had been reported. That is a statement about reports received at that time, not proof that consumption is safe.

Costco's recall page lists the product notice. Independent reports from Good Housekeeping and the San Francisco Chronicle corroborate the item, date range and voluntary-recall instructions. The absence of an FDA posting in early coverage should not be taken as evidence that Costco's notice is invalid.

What should Costco members do now?

Stop using the affected whipped cream. Do not taste it to test whether it is contaminated. Bring the recalled product to your local Costco for the full refund described in the member notice. For questions about the supplier's notice, contact customer.care@alamancefood.com. If you consumed the product and have health concerns, contact a qualified health professional; this article cannot diagnose an exposure.

For consumers planning Thanksgiving desserts, the practical step is to check cans purchased in October before using them. Where can you verify updates?

Use Costco's official product recall notices for the original member notice and any revisions. The product is also identifiable by the Costco product listing for item 1777000, although a product listing alone does not determine whether a particular can is recalled.
