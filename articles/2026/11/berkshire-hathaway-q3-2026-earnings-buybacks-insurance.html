Berkshire Q3 2026: $9.6B Buybacks, Lower Operating Profit

Berkshire Hathaway bought back $9.6 billion of its shares in the third quarter, even as operating earnings fell nearly 6% from a year earlier. Its insurance businesses weakened while railroad and energy earnings improved.

Quick answer: In its November 7 earnings release, Berkshire Hathaway reported $12.705 billion in third-quarter 2026 operating earnings, down from $13.485 billion in the same period of 2025. The company repurchased approximately $9.6 billion in shares during the quarter. Insurance underwriting was the main operating drag; BNSF railroad and Berkshire Hathaway Energy posted gains.

Why did operating profit fall while buybacks increased?

Operating earnings declined about 5.8% year over year, with insurance underwriting down about 43%. That weakness outweighed improved profits in the railroad, energy, and manufacturing/service/retail operations. The buybacks are a separate capital-allocation decision: repurchasing stock does not mean every operating business is growing. Reuters independently reported the earnings decline and the $9.6 billion repurchase total.

According to the company's release, repurchases for the first nine months of 2026 totaled about $14.4 billion. That implies roughly $4.8 billion was bought in the first half, before the much larger third-quarter activity. Buybacks reduce shares outstanding, but their eventual benefit to continuing shareholders depends on the price paid and future business performance.

What happened at Geico, BNSF and Berkshire Hathaway Energy?

Insurance underwriting profit fell from $2.369 billion to $1.352 billion. Associated Press reporting identifies a particularly sharp decline at Geico amid higher claims costs and advertising spending. These costs can change as the insurer seeks customers, but a single quarter does not establish the future profitability of the strategy.

BNSF's after-tax earnings rose from $1.449 billion to $1.729 billion, an increase of about 19%. Berkshire Hathaway Energy rose from $1.489 billion to $1.612 billion, approximately 8%. Those increases show why Berkshire's diversified operating portfolio matters: the quarter's outcome was not uniform across its businesses.

Why are net earnings different from operating earnings?

Berkshire reported net earnings of $22.112 billion, down from $30.796 billion a year earlier. Unlike operating earnings, net earnings include investment gains and losses, including changes in the market value of shares Berkshire still holds. The company's own release cautions against interpreting short-term investment fluctuations as a straightforward measure of underlying operating performance.

For readers comparing headlines, the $22.112 billion net-earnings figure and the $12.705 billion operating-earnings figure describe different accounting measures; neither should be substituted for the other. The company asks investors to read its complete third-quarter filing rather than rely on the short press release alone.

What should Berkshire shareholders watch next?

The key questions are whether insurance underwriting recovers, whether buybacks continue at elevated levels, and how capital is allocated between share repurchases, business acquisitions and public stocks. A repurchase announcement is not a promise of future repurchases, and the company has not guaranteed an earnings rebound. Readers should distinguish reported quarter-end figures from later estimates and market commentary.
