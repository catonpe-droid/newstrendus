US Consumer Sentiment Rises to 51.0 in November: What Changed

The University of Michigan's preliminary survey improved in November, yet consumers remain troubled by the cost of living. Here are the figures, the important caveats and the next release to watch.

Quick answer: Preliminary US consumer sentiment increased to 51.0 in November 2026 from 48.0 in October, according to the University of Michigan's November 6 release. Inflation expectations eased, but the improvement does not mean Americans consider prices affordable.

What did the November 2026 consumer sentiment survey show?

The University of Michigan Surveys of Consumers reported a 6.3% monthly rise in its headline sentiment index. The index was unchanged from November 2025, when it also stood at 51.0. That distinction matters: a monthly rebound is not evidence that households feel better than a year ago.

The preliminary reading was released on Friday, November 6. It captures responses gathered before the November 3 US midterm election, according to Wall Street Journal coverage. It should not be presented as a post-election verdict.

The first three measures come from the university's published index table; inflation-expectation figures were also reported by Reuters on November 6. A survey index is not an inflation rate, a wage measure or a forecast of retail-sales growth.

Why did sentiment rise if the cost of living remains painful?

The improvement was uneven. Reuters reported stronger sentiment among independent voters and lower- and middle-wealth households. By contrast, the measure declined about 5% among respondents holding the largest stock portfolios after turbulent trading. That split helps explain how the national average can improve even when some groups feel less secure.

What does the AI bubble concern mean for US households?

Survey commentary cited by Reuters highlighted increasing anxiety about a possible artificial-intelligence investment bubble. Such concern may be particularly salient to people whose savings and retirement accounts are exposed to volatile technology shares. The survey does not establish that an AI bubble exists or predict whether one will burst.

For readers, the useful distinction is between three separate questions: how consumers feel today, how they expect prices to change, and whether they are worried about investments. These indicators can move in different directions.

When is the final November consumer sentiment report?

The university is scheduled to publish its final November reading on Friday, November 20, 2026. That update may revise the preliminary figures and include later interviews. Readers should check the official survey website for the final values rather than assuming 51.0 will remain unchanged.
