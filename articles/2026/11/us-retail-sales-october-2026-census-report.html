US Retail Sales October 2026: Why 0.2% Growth Is Uncertain

The October retail-sales estimate shows a modest rise in spending, but its statistical uncertainty matters more than the headline alone.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · November 17, 2026 · Source-linked desk reporting · Representative photograph

Source-linked analysis of preliminary government estimates, not firsthand survey collection or financial advice. See our editorial and corrections policy.

Representative licensed photograph of wrapped gifts; not a photograph of the Census survey or a particular retailer. Photographer: Rahul Pandit / Pexels.

Quick answer: The U.S. Census Bureau reported on November 17 that October 2026 retail and food-services sales totaled an estimated $748.2 billion, up 0.2% from September. The agency says the 90% confidence interval includes zero, so the month-to-month gain is not statistically distinguishable from no change.

How much did US retail sales change in October 2026?

The official October advance release puts seasonally adjusted retail and food-services sales at $748.2 billion. September's revised estimate was $746.6 billion, up 0.8% from August. October sales were 6.8% higher than in October 2025, according to the same release. These are dollar-sales estimates, not inflation-adjusted measures of the quantity of goods purchased.

Why does the Census Bureau flag the 0.2% increase?

The reported October monthly increase is 0.2% with a margin of error of plus or minus 0.4 percentage points. Because that interval includes zero, the statistical evidence does not establish that sales actually increased from September. That caveat is part of the government's release, not an interpretation that the agency confirmed a decline. The estimates may also be revised as more survey information arrives.

The advance survey is based on a subsample of retail firms and provides a timely indicator rather than a final tally. Comparing nominal spending with a household's purchasing power requires separate price data; higher receipts can reflect higher prices as well as more purchases.

Did sales improve outside autos and gasoline?

The Census chart shows October sales excluding autos rose 0.6% month over month, while sales excluding both autos and gasoline rose 0.5%. Those are different measures from the all-category 0.2% headline. The agency cautions that comparisons across industries have not been tested for statistical significance. The figures suggest why readers should avoid describing every retail category as equally weak.

What does the report mean for US shoppers and businesses?

For consumers, the release does not forecast holiday discounts, individual store prices or personal inflation. For retailers and small businesses, it offers an early national demand signal but cannot substitute for local sales, inventory and customer data. Reuters' November 17 report also highlighted slower headline growth and pressure on discretionary purchases. The government's uncertainty note is especially important before drawing conclusions about the health of consumer spending.

For seasonal shopping context, see our existing Google Holiday 100 buying guide. It covers shopping-interest trends rather than official spending statistics. Readers can also explore the NewsTrendus Business section for related economic coverage.

When is the next retail sales report?

The Census Bureau schedules its November 2026 advance retail sales release for December 16, 2026, at 8:30 a.m. Eastern, according to its official release calendar. The October estimate may be revised in subsequent reports. Until then, the defensible conclusion is that October's estimated growth was modest and statistically uncertain, not that the government proved consumer spending stalled.
