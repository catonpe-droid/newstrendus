Vevor BMX Bike Recall: Models and Free Chain Guard Fix

A federal safety recall covers certain Vevor BMX bicycles sold in the United States. Here is how to identify the affected bikes and obtain the free chain-guard repair.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · November 12, 2026 · Source-linked desk reporting · Representative photograph

Independent source-linked consumer safety explainer, not first-hand product testing. Research and drafting may use AI-assisted tools. See our editorial and corrections policy.

Representative licensed photograph of a BMX bicycle, not a recalled Vevor product. Photo: Tima Miroshnichenko / Pexels.

Quick answer: The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission announced a November 5, 2026 recall of about 1,950 Vevor BMX bicycles because they lack a chain guard required by federal bicycle safety rules. Owners of affected 20-inch or 24-inch models should stop riding and contact Vevor for a free chain-guard repair kit. See the official CPSC notice.

Which Vevor BMX bicycles are recalled?

The recall applies to Vevor-branded 20-inch and 24-inch BMX bicycles, model numbers SS24001 and SS24002. The affected bikes are black and carry the Vevor logo on the downtube. According to the CPSC, model numbers are printed on the product packaging; owners who no longer have the box should contact Vevor rather than assume their bicycle is excluded based on appearance alone.

The bicycles were sold at Vevor stores and on Vevor.com from October 2024 through April 2026, for approximately $152–$188. The CPSC lists roughly 1,950 units in the U.S. recall. The sales dates are an identification clue, not a substitute for checking the model.

What is the safety problem?

Federal regulators say the bicycles lack a required chain guard. Without that guard, loose clothing can become caught in the chain drive, creating an entanglement and crash risk. The notice also identifies noncompliance with federal labeling requirements. These are regulatory findings, not a claim that every recalled bicycle has caused an accident.

The CPSC notice states that no incidents or injuries had been reported when the recall was announced. That does not remove the potential hazard; owners should follow the corrective instructions rather than waiting for a problem while riding.

How to get the free Vevor BMX repair kit

First, stop using an affected bicycle. Check the model number on its original packaging, if available. Then use Vevor's official recall instructions or contact the company at recalling@vevor.com. The CPSC says Sanven Technology will provide chain guards, installation tutorials and after-sales support without charge.

Do not purchase a replacement guard from an unverified seller on the assumption that it satisfies this recall. Follow the manufacturer's specific repair procedure, and confirm that the corrective work has been completed before riding again. If a bicycle was bought secondhand, the safety issue still deserves attention; contact the company with the available model details.

Is there a refund or a reported injury?

The stated remedy is a free repair kit, not a general cash refund. The CPSC lists no reported incidents or injuries. The recall identifier is 27-073, despite its November 2026 announcement date; CPSC recall numbering follows the agency's fiscal-year convention. The agency identifies Sanven Technology Ltd. of Rancho Cucamonga, California, as the importer.

What should parents and riders check next?

Check the bike's size and model, keep the bicycle out of use until repaired, and use the official notice to confirm the remedy. Families buying used bicycles should ask sellers whether the repair was completed and request documentation.
