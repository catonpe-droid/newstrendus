Miami Beats UNC 38-35: The Fourth-Down TD and Fumble That Saved No. 1

No. 1 Miami stayed unbeaten, but the Hurricanes needed a fourth-down touchdown and one late defensive takeaway to escape North Carolina on Halloween night.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · November 1, 2026 · Independently checked against official team reports · Representative photograph

Representative licensed photograph of American football players in Baltimore; not the Miami–UNC game or its participants. Photo: Styves Exantus / Pexels (Pexels free-use license).

At a glance: No. 1 Miami defeated North Carolina 38-35 on October 31, 2026, at Kenan Stadium in Chapel Hill. Mark Fletcher Jr.'s fourth-down rushing touchdown put Miami ahead for good; a strip-sack on the next UNC possession helped secure the result.

Miami beat North Carolina 38-35 on Saturday, October 31, to remain undefeated. The final score is confirmed by both Miami Athletics and UNC Athletics. The result moved the Hurricanes to 8-0 overall and 6-0 in Atlantic Coast Conference play; the Tar Heels fell to 4-4 and 2-3.

What was the Miami vs. UNC final score?

Miami won 38-35. The Hurricanes scored 7, 10, 7 and 14 points by quarter; North Carolina scored 0, 14, 7 and 14. Miami led only 17-14 at halftime. UNC took a 28-24 lead early in the fourth quarter before the visitors answered with two touchdowns. A final North Carolina score cut the margin to three points, but the Tar Heels could not regain possession.

Why did fourth-and-one change the outcome?

UNC led 28-24 after Demon June finished a 75-yard drive with a one-yard rushing touchdown. Miami responded with its own 75-yard march. On fourth-and-one at the UNC two-yard line, running back Mark Fletcher Jr. scored over right tackle. Instead of facing a possible turnover on downs or settling for a field goal, Miami took a 31-28 lead. That decision and execution shifted the pressure back to North Carolina.

The UNC game account lists the fourth-down score and the preceding drive; Miami's official recap independently describes Fletcher's decisive run. It was his third touchdown of the evening, counting two runs and a 31-yard receiving score.

What happened on the decisive North Carolina fumble?

With 5:33 remaining, Miami defensive lineman Marquise Lightfoot hit UNC quarterback Billy Edwards Jr. from behind and forced a fumble. Justin Scott recovered at the North Carolina 37-yard line. Six plays later, Darian Mensah threw a nine-yard touchdown pass to Malachi Toney, stretching the Hurricanes' advantage to 38-28 with 2:33 left.

The takeaway was especially consequential because the teams otherwise avoided turnovers. UNC still responded: Edwards threw a 15-yard touchdown pass to Trech Kekahuna to make it 38-35 with 1:38 left. Miami ultimately secured the ensuing onside-kick sequence and ran out the clock. Associated Press reporting carried by CBS Sports also describes the late turnover and onside-kick drama.

How did the quarterbacks and offenses compare?

Mensah completed 22 of 29 passes for 274 yards and three touchdowns, while Edwards completed 28 of 34 for 341 yards and four touchdowns. Miami finished with 416 total yards to UNC's 407, according to the official UNC statistics. The Tar Heels actually produced more passing yards and first downs (27 to 25). Miami, however, gained 142 rushing yards against UNC's 66 and won the turnover battle 1-0.

Those numbers explain why the contest remained close despite the teams' different records. UNC's passing game sustained long possessions and repeatedly answered Miami scores. Miami's ability to convert a high-stakes fourth down and capitalize on the only takeaway ultimately mattered more than its narrow advantage in total yardage. This is an interpretation of the verified play-by-play, not a claim that a single statistic determines every game.

What does the result mean, and who plays next?

Miami preserved its unbeaten season and remained in a strong ACC position, though the narrow win exposed defensive questions before a scheduled trip to Notre Dame. North Carolina's near-upset did not change the standings: the Tar Heels entered November at 4-4 and next visit UConn on November 7, with UNC listing a noon kickoff. Rankings, kickoff details and postseason projections can change; no unconfirmed playoff outcome is assumed here.
