California High Surf Advisory: Beaches and End Times

The National Weather Service issued high surf advisories across stretches of the California coast on November 16, with some Northern California breaking waves forecast at 20–25 feet and occasional larger sets. The exact advisory expiration depends on the beach.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · November 16, 2026 · National Weather Service advisory analysis · Representative photograph

This is a source-linked public safety guide, not eyewitness reporting. Research and drafting may use AI-assisted tools. Read our editorial and corrections policy.

Quick answer: The National Weather Service warned of hazardous breaking surf and strong rip currents along multiple California coastal zones on Monday, November 16. San Francisco and Monterey-area advisories were scheduled to end at 4 a.m. PST Tuesday, November 17; several Southern California advisories were scheduled through Tuesday afternoon or early Wednesday. Conditions and alerts can change, so check your specific beach before traveling.

Which California beaches are under high surf advisories?

The NWS San Francisco coastal hazard bulletin, issued at 4:51 a.m. PST November 16, covers San Francisco, the coastal North Bay including Point Reyes, the San Francisco Peninsula coast, Northern Monterey Bay, Southern Monterey Bay and Big Sur. It forecasts breaking waves of 20–25 feet, with occasional sets of 25–35 feet at favored breakpoints, through 4 a.m. PST Tuesday. These are forecast wave ranges for the named coastal zones, not measurements at every beach.

The NWS Los Angeles/Oxnard bulletin, issued at 4:14 a.m. PST November 16, covers San Luis Obispo County beaches and Santa Barbara County's central coast, with surf increasing toward 15–18 feet and local sets near 20 feet. The same bulletin lists Ventura County beaches at 8–12 feet and Malibu/Los Angeles County beaches at 7–9 feet. Those high surf advisories were scheduled through 3 p.m. PST Tuesday, November 17.

When does the advisory end in San Diego and Orange County?

The NWS San Diego bulletin carried in the regional advisory summary, issued at 12:47 a.m. PST Monday, forecasts breaking waves of 6–9 feet with sets to 11 feet along west-facing beaches in Orange and San Diego counties. That advisory was scheduled to end at 4 a.m. PST Wednesday, November 18. Strong rip currents are a major concern even where wave heights are lower than in Northern California.

High surf advisory versus coastal flood advisory

A high surf advisory primarily warns that large breaking waves and associated currents can endanger people at the shoreline. A coastal flood advisory concerns possible inundation of low-lying coastal areas. The Los Angeles/Oxnard office also listed a coastal flood advisory for San Luis Obispo County beaches and the Santa Barbara County central coast through 9 p.m. PST Monday, November 16, with minor flooding possible. These alerts are not interchangeable, and their boundaries and expiration times differ.

What should beachgoers do?

The NWS says powerful waves can knock people off rocks, jetties and beaches and drag them into turbulent water. Stay out of the water where advised; avoid rocky shorelines, exposed jetties and wave-watching close to the surf. Do not assume that a lull means the next set will be small. Check local lifeguard guidance and current NWS alerts before approaching the coast. This is especially important for families, tourists, inexperienced swimmers and anyone planning to photograph the waves.

Boaters should consult harbor and Coast Guard restrictions separately: a coastal surf advisory does not itself specify whether a particular harbor is closed. For a visit later in the week, use an updated forecast rather than Monday's expiration time.

What happens next?

In its Monday morning bulletin, the Los Angeles/Oxnard office said some advisories might need extension and another larger surf episode was possible Thursday into Friday. That is a forecast, not a confirmed extension. Consult the issuing NWS office for the latest alert status.
