Royal Caribbean Q3 2026 Earnings: $6.57 Adjusted EPS, Higher Outlook and Fuel Risks

The cruise operator raised its 2026 profit outlook after stronger bookings and favorable cost timing, even as fuel prices remain a watchpoint.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · October 27, 2026 · Independently written from credited sources · Representative editorial photograph

Representative licensed photograph, not the reported event or people. Photo: Mike Bird / Pexels (Pexels free-use license).

At a glance: Royal Caribbean reported $5.6 billion in Q3 2026 revenue, $6.57 adjusted EPS and higher full-year guidance. What bookings, costs and fuel mean.

MIAMI, Oct. 27, 2026 — Royal Caribbean Group reported third-quarter revenue of $5.6 billion, up 9% from a year earlier, and adjusted earnings of $6.57 per diluted share. The company raised its full-year adjusted EPS forecast to $17.85–$17.95. Those figures come from the company's October 27 earnings release, not from an estimate of future share-price performance.

What did Royal Caribbean report for the third quarter?

The company reported GAAP net income of approximately $1.7 billion, or $6.45 a share, while adjusted net income was about $1.8 billion. Adjusted earnings exclude certain items and are a non-GAAP measure; they should not be mistaken for the statutory per-share result. Adjusted EBITDA was $2.5 billion. The business served approximately 2.7 million guests, a 9% increase, while available capacity expanded 8%.

Reported load factor was 112%, a cruise-industry measure that can exceed 100% because cabins may hold more than two passengers. It does not mean ships were 12% beyond a safety limit. This distinction matters when comparing cruise utilization with airline or hotel occupancy.

Why did earnings beat the company's expectations?

Management pointed to better-than-expected close-in demand, particularly in Europe and the Caribbean, alongside the timing of operating expenses. Net yields increased 0.9% as reported and 0.6% at constant currency. Net cruise costs excluding fuel per available passenger cruise day fell 1.9% as reported. The company explicitly cautioned that some costs shifted into the fourth quarter rather than disappearing permanently.

This is why the headline earnings beat should be read with the cost outlook: a quarter can look stronger when expenses are recognized later. Royal Caribbean's fourth-quarter adjusted EPS forecast of $3.48–$3.58 provides a nearer-term reference point for assessing the raised full-year projection.

How strong are cruise bookings for 2027?

Royal Caribbean said booking volumes increased in the mid-teens across major channels since its previous earnings call, with bookings for 2027 sailings rising in the high teens. Management described 2027 bookings as ahead of historical levels at higher rates. These are company-reported trends, not a guarantee that demand will remain equally strong throughout next year.

For U.S. travelers, higher booking demand may mean fewer attractive cabins or less flexibility on some popular sailings, but it does not establish that every cruise fare is rising. Total vacation costs still depend on departure port, season, itinerary, onboard purchases and cancellation terms.

What could higher fuel prices change?

The company identified fuel and currency movements as partial offsets to the quarter's better performance. Its fourth-quarter guidance assumed approximately $379 million in fuel expense, with 58% of expected fuel consumption hedged. Hedging can reduce exposure to short-term price swings, but it does not eliminate all fuel-cost risk.

The release also outlined a planned investment in Sandals and Beaches Resorts that remains subject to closing conditions and regulatory approvals. That prospective expansion should be treated separately from completed third-quarter operating results.

What should investors and travelers watch next?

Investors can compare actual fourth-quarter margins, net yields and fuel expense with management's guidance, while checking the company's non-GAAP reconciliations before relying on adjusted EPS alone. Travelers should compare total trip prices and cancellation policies rather than assuming a strong corporate earnings report translates directly into cheaper or more expensive individual vacations.

The earnings report supports a conclusion of resilient demand at the reporting date. It does not establish future stock returns or guarantee a particular fare. This article is informational and is not personalized investment advice.
