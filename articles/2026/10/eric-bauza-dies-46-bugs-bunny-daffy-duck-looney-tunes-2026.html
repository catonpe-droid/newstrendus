Eric Bauza Dies at 46: Looney Tunes Voice Actor Behind Bugs Bunny and Daffy Duck

The family of award-winning voice performer Eric Bauza confirmed his death, prompting tributes to the actor whose work introduced classic cartoon voices to new audiences.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · October 26, 2026 · Independently written from credited sources · Representative editorial photograph

Representative editorial photograph, not the reported event or people. Photo: Kaboompics / Pexels (Pexels free-use license).

Quick answer: Eric Bauza, the Emmy-winning performer who voiced Bugs Bunny, Daffy Duck and other Looney Tunes characters, has died at age 46, his family said on October 25.

LOS ANGELES, Oct. 26, 2026 — Eric Bauza, the voice actor known for performing Bugs Bunny, Daffy Duck and other Looney Tunes characters, has died at age 46. His family announced his death in a message shared Sunday, October 25, prompting tributes from fans and people across the animation industry.

What has been confirmed about Eric Bauza's death?

Bauza's family said in a public statement that the Emmy-winning actor had died suddenly and indicated that further details would follow. News outlets including ABC7 Los Angeles and The Guardian independently reported the announcement. This article focuses on confirmed information rather than rumors circulating alongside searches for the actor's name.

Later reporting by The Guardian, citing the Los Angeles County medical examiner, identified hypertensive cardiovascular disease as the cause of death. Readers should distinguish that reported medical finding from the family's initial statement, which did not specify a cause. The family has not requested speculation about private circumstances.

Which Looney Tunes characters did Eric Bauza voice?

Bauza became one of the modern voices associated with Bugs Bunny, Daffy Duck, Porky Pig and Tweety. His work continued a tradition of character performances established by earlier voice actors, including Mel Blanc, while adapting the timing and delivery for contemporary animation.

He also voiced characters in projects beyond Looney Tunes, including Teen Titans Go!, Muppet Babies and The Adventures of Puss in Boots. His broad body of work reflects the range required of voice performers, who may switch among personalities, ages and vocal styles within the same production.

Eric Bauza's Emmy awards and career

Bauza received three Children's and Family Emmy Awards for voice-performance work, according to reporting on his career. Recognition included roles in Looney Tunes Cartoons, Bugs Bunny Builders and Teen Titans Go!. The awards were an acknowledgment of a craft often heard by millions of viewers without the performer appearing on screen.

Born in the Toronto area in 1979, Bauza built a career spanning more than two decades. His work on recent animated productions included Coyote vs. Acme, which received renewed attention following the announcement of his death.

How the animation industry is remembering him

Warner Bros. television leadership praised Bauza's distinctive voice talent and his contribution to the enduring Looney Tunes legacy, according to The Guardian. Colleagues have emphasized both his technical skill and the affection he brought to characters whose identities depend so heavily on sound.

For viewers, the loss is not just the passing of an actor but the loss of a familiar creative presence behind characters seen across generations. His recorded performances remain part of the work through which audiences will encounter him.
