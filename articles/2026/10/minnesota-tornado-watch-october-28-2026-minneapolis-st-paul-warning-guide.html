Minnesota Tornado Watch October 28: Twin Cities Storm Risk and What Residents Should Check

The late-October severe-weather threat prompted a tornado watch; a watch means conditions are favorable, not that a tornado has been confirmed.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · October 28, 2026 · Independently written from credited sources · Representative editorial photograph

Representative licensed photograph, not the reported event, product or person. Photo: ClickerHappy / Pexels (Pexels free-use license).

At a glance: A tornado watch covered Minneapolis–St. Paul and parts of Minnesota and Wisconsin on October 28, 2026. Understand watch vs warning and safety steps.

MINNEAPOLIS, October 28, 2026 — A tornado watch was issued for the Twin Cities and portions of Minnesota and Wisconsin as thunderstorms moved through the region Wednesday. The unusual late-October setup brought concern about damaging winds, hail and isolated tornadoes. Residents should use the latest National Weather Service alerts for their exact location, because conditions and warning boundaries can change quickly.

Where was the Minnesota tornado watch in effect?

Local reporting from the Minnesota Star Tribune described a watch covering Minneapolis–St. Paul and broad areas of Minnesota and Wisconsin, with a stated expiration of 9 p.m. Central Daylight Time on October 28. The National Weather Service Twin Cities forecast page displayed a tornado watch among its advisories during the afternoon.

That expiration time is the announced watch window, not a guarantee that the danger will continue until then or end at exactly that time. Severe-thunderstorm and tornado warnings can be issued, updated or canceled for much smaller areas. Check the NWS Twin Cities office or local emergency alerts before making decisions.

What is the difference between a tornado watch and warning?

A tornado watch means atmospheric conditions favor the possible development of tornadoes across a broad area. It is a prompt to prepare, monitor updates and identify a safe shelter. A tornado warning means a tornado has been indicated by radar or observed and immediate protective action is needed in the warned area.

A watch does not confirm a tornado touched down. Nor does the appearance of a watch alone establish property damage, injuries or a particular tornado track. Those claims require separate official storm reports and later verification.

What weather hazards did forecasters identify?

Minnesota Public Radio's October 28 weather analysis highlighted a period of heightened severe-weather potential from late afternoon into the early evening, particularly around the Twin Cities and east toward Wisconsin. Forecasters described a risk of damaging hail, strong straight-line wind and an isolated tornado.

Unusually warm and humid conditions for late October helped create the environment. Weather risk is not uniform across a metropolitan area: some neighborhoods can see heavy rain or lightning while others experience little severe weather. Forecast probabilities are not predictions that a specific home will be hit.

What should Minneapolis and St. Paul residents do during a warning?

If a tornado warning is issued for your location, go immediately to a basement or a small interior room on the lowest level of a sturdy building, away from windows. Put as many walls as possible between yourself and the outside, protect your head and avoid large open-span rooms. Do not shelter under a highway overpass.

Keep Wireless Emergency Alerts enabled, charge your phone and have a second way to receive warnings, such as NOAA Weather Radio. If you are in a mobile home, identify a sturdier nearby shelter before severe weather arrives. Never drive toward a storm to photograph it.

What happens after the October 28 storms?

Forecasters expected a cooler air mass to arrive Thursday, October 29, with temperatures closer to seasonal norms. Any verified tornado tracks, hail reports or wind damage will need to be checked against subsequent NWS storm summaries rather than inferred from the original watch.

Safety note: This report records the October 28 watch and contemporaneous forecast. It is not a real-time emergency warning service. For immediate risk, follow current NWS alerts and local authorities.
