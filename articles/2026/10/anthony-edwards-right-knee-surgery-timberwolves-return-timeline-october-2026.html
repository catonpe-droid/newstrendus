Anthony Edwards Knee Surgery Report: Timberwolves Return Timeline and What Is Known

The Timberwolves guard's right knee is the subject of reports about an arthroscopic procedure. The difference between an expected absence and a confirmed return date matters.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · October 28, 2026 · Independently written from credited sources · Representative editorial photograph

Representative licensed photograph, not the reported event or people. Photo: cottonbro studio / Pexels (Pexels free-use license).

At a glance: Anthony Edwards is expected to need a right-knee arthroscopic procedure, AP reports. Why a two-month recovery is an estimate and what Minnesota faces without him.

MINNEAPOLIS, Oct. 28, 2026 — Minnesota Timberwolves guard Anthony Edwards is expected to undergo an arthroscopic procedure on his right knee and miss several weeks, according to an Associated Press report published October 27. ESPN's Shams Charania reported a possible absence of up to two months. Neither projection should be mistaken for a firm, team-announced return date.

What happened to Anthony Edwards' knee?

AP reported that a person familiar with the situation said Edwards needs an arthroscopic procedure to address a right-knee problem. The source spoke anonymously because the Timberwolves had not publicly announced the operation at the time of that report. AP said a more precise timetable would depend on what doctors find and how recovery progresses.

The injury report had already listed Edwards with right-knee concerns. He had experienced recurring discomfort, but the exact underlying condition and scope of any planned procedure were not detailed in the sourced public information. It would be premature to assign a specific medical diagnosis or guarantee that surgery has already been completed.

Is Edwards out for two months?

NBC Sports, citing ESPN, described a potential absence of up to two months. AP used the broader formulation 'several weeks' and emphasized that the timeline would be clearer after the procedure. These reports are not necessarily contradictory: one is an estimated outer window and the other avoids specifying a date before medical assessment.

If the recovery lasted roughly eight weeks, Edwards might not return until late December. That is a calendar illustration, not a medical prediction. The team, not a search snippet or speculative schedule, will determine when he is cleared for full basketball activity.

Why the timing matters for Minnesota

Edwards had opened the 2026-27 season with 133 points in four games, an average of 33.3 per contest, according to AP. He scored 47 in Minnesota's 127-125 overtime loss at Cleveland on October 26. The Timberwolves were 3-1 after their first four games. Losing the team's primary scorer just as the season begins could alter shot distribution, late-game playmaking and defensive matchups.

Other Minnesota players will need to share the offensive workload, but projecting exact replacement minutes without a coach's confirmed rotation would be speculation. A shorter absence would create a different problem from a two-month recovery; both possibilities remain open until the club provides more detail.

Is this the same injury Edwards had in the playoffs?

No. The latest reports concern his right knee. In April 2026, the NBA reported a left-knee hyperextension and bone bruise during the playoffs. Those are separate episodes and should not be collapsed into one injury. Prior reports of right-knee discomfort are background, not proof of the current medical diagnosis.

That distinction is important because old injury headlines and clips can circulate alongside new coverage, creating the false impression that a current medical announcement is months old or that the affected knee has changed.

What should fans check for the next update?

Look for a dated Timberwolves medical update, a team injury report and attributable comments from coaches or physicians. The important milestones are confirmation of the procedure, the nature of any restrictions, progression to on-court work and clearance for contact. A player appearing at practice does not necessarily mean he is ready for a game.

For now, the verified news is that credible outlets report a forthcoming right-knee procedure and a potentially substantial absence. An exact comeback date, a confirmed diagnosis and a post-surgery assessment were not established by the sources available for this October 28 report.
