Israel Election 2026: Exit Polls Show Deadlock, Not a Final Winner

Competing exit polls put both main blocs short of a Knesset majority. Final certified results and coalition negotiations are still ahead.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · October 28, 2026 · Independently written from credited sources · Representative editorial photograph

Representative licensed photograph, not the reported event or people. Photo: Jeffry Surianto / Pexels (Pexels free-use license).

At a glance: Israel's Oct. 27 election exit polls show no clear majority for Netanyahu or Eisenkot. How the 61-seat threshold, counting and coalition talks work.

Who won Israel's October 27 election?

No final winner had been established in the early reporting reviewed after polls closed in Israel on Tuesday, October 27, 2026. Reuters reported that two major television exit polls put opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot's bloc narrowly ahead of Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's camp, while a third projected the two blocs level. Exit polls are estimates, not certified votes.

The distinction is important for U.S. readers seeing search results labeled 'Israel election results.' A leading party, a leading bloc and the person who ultimately forms a government can be three different answers. Counting, party negotiations and the president's consultation process all affect the final outcome.

Why is the number 61 so important?

Israel's Knesset has 120 seats, so a governing coalition generally needs support from at least 61 lawmakers. Reuters reported that none of the major early projections gave either principal bloc that threshold. One television projection placed the two blocs at 51 seats each, illustrating the scale of the gap between an election-night lead and a governing majority.

Smaller parties can therefore have substantial leverage in negotiations. Arab parties were projected to gain seats outside the two largest blocs, but their possible role in government remained politically contested. Those projections should not be treated as final seat allocations.

What do turnout and vote counting tell us?

Reuters reported a 77.7% turnout figure attributed to Israel's Central Elections Committee, the highest in more than two decades. High turnout indicates broad participation; it does not, by itself, identify which coalition will be able to govern. Differences between exit polls may narrow or widen as actual ballots are counted.

Official results can take time, especially when close margins and smaller parties affect seat distribution. Readers should avoid posts that transform early television estimates into a definitive announcement of a new prime minister. The election's outcome must be distinguished from the later process of building a government.

What happens after the ballots are counted?

Once official results are available, President Isaac Herzog is expected to consult party representatives and task a lawmaker with attempting to assemble a coalition. A party with the largest single seat total does not automatically receive the premiership; the relevant question is which candidate can command parliamentary support.

Coalition negotiations can extend well beyond election night. During that period, a caretaker government may continue in office. Reporting about a possible unity government or an agreement with smaller parties remains provisional until participants announce and complete the relevant steps.

Why does the election matter to the United States?

Israel is a major U.S. security and diplomatic partner, and its next government could influence regional diplomacy, defense cooperation and negotiations involving Gaza and neighboring states. Those are policy implications to monitor, not predictions that any particular exit-poll scenario will immediately change U.S. policy.

As of the early October 28 reporting in Israel, the supported conclusion is a close election without a projected 61-seat majority for either main bloc. This article will need an update when certified results or a government-formation mandate becomes available.
