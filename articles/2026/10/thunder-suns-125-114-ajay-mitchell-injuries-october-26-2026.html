Thunder Beat Suns 125–114: Ajay Mitchell Scores 30 as Injuries Test OKC

Oklahoma City's unbeaten start continued, but injuries to key rotation players remain the bigger concern beyond the final score.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · October 27, 2026 · Independently written from credited sources · Representative editorial photograph

Representative licensed photograph, not the reported event, brand or people. Photo: KoolShooters / Pexels (Pexels free-use license).

At a glance: The Thunder beat the Suns 125–114 to improve to 4–0. Ajay Mitchell scored 30, Shai Gilgeous-Alexander added 26 and Oklahoma City played through significant absences.

Thunder vs. Suns final score: Oklahoma City improves to 4–0

OKLAHOMA CITY, Oct. 27, 2026 — The Oklahoma City Thunder defeated the Phoenix Suns 125–114 on Monday, October 26, remaining unbeaten through four games of the new NBA season. Ajay Mitchell scored 30 points and Shai Gilgeous-Alexander added 26, according to Reuters' October 27 report. The NBA's official matchup page identifies the October 26 game at Paycom Center. The final result is independently reflected in the Reuters NBA roundup.

How Ajay Mitchell helped cover the missing offense

Mitchell's 30-point performance provided scoring that Oklahoma City needed with multiple rotation players unavailable. Gilgeous-Alexander's 26 points gave the team another reliable source of offense. Isaiah Hartenstein contributed 15 points and 13 rebounds, while Aday Mara recorded his first career double-double, according to the Reuters game report. The production was spread across several contributors rather than resting entirely on one high-volume scorer.

Jalen Williams and Cason Wallace injury updates

Reuters reported that Jalen Williams suffered a fractured fibula and a dislocated left ankle and that Cason Wallace tore his labrum in the previous game. Both were expected to require surgery and miss extended time. Those are significant reported injuries; a precise return date was not established in the cited reports. Oklahoma City was also without Chet Holmgren for this matchup, and Alex Caruso left during the first half with an ankle problem. Any updated prognosis should come from the team or medical reporting, not fan speculation.

Where the Thunder seized control

Oklahoma City used a 22–6 second-quarter run to build separation, according to Reuters, and a 12–2 burst early in the fourth helped protect the lead. The team reached 125 despite the absences. The 11-point final margin is only one measure of the game: the scoring runs and ability to find offense from replacement contributors better explain why the Thunder stayed ahead.

Why Phoenix could not match the scoring

Jalen Green led the Suns with 20 points and Dillon Brooks added 19. Devin Booker, who had totaled 66 points over Phoenix's first two games, was limited to three points on 1-for-7 shooting, according to the Reuters recap. Phoenix still reached 114, but its offensive output was not enough to overcome Oklahoma City's key stretches. One poor shooting night should not be interpreted as evidence of a lasting decline.

What the 4–0 start means for Oklahoma City

Four wins are valuable, especially amid roster disruption, but the injuries complicate any long-range projection. Oklahoma City will need consistent bench minutes, careful workload management and new contributions while injured players recover. The next verified team injury reports will matter more than unsupported guesses about recovery dates.
