Hershey Halloween Candy Prices 2026: Why Sales Rise as Shoppers Buy Less

The candy maker's October earnings show why higher dollar sales do not necessarily mean Americans are buying more sweets.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · October 31, 2026 · Company filing and retail-survey analysis · Representative photograph

Representative licensed photograph of assorted Halloween sweets, not a Hershey earnings announcement or a 2026 store shelf. Photo: Terrance Barksdale / Pexels (Pexels free-use license).

At a glance: Hershey reported $3.28 billion in third-quarter sales on October 29, up 3% from a year earlier, even as company-wide organic sales volume/mix fell by roughly 10 percentage points. The National Retail Federation separately forecasts $13.5 billion in total U.S. Halloween spending, but that projection is not a final sales tally.

On Halloween weekend, the clearest signal from America's candy aisle is a split between dollars spent and the amount purchased. In its October 29 third-quarter earnings release, Hershey reported consolidated net sales of $3,275.5 million, up 3% year over year. Yet its organic, constant-currency sales rose just 0.8%: approximately 11 points of higher prices were offset by around 10 points of lower volume/mix. These are company-wide quarterly measures, not a calculation of Halloween-only purchases.

Did Hershey sell more candy, or charge more?

Hershey's North America Confectionery segment recorded $2.66 billion in quarterly net sales, a 1.6% increase. The company's investor disclosure says the segment benefited from roughly 13 points of net price realization while volume declined approximately 11 points. In plain English, higher selling prices outweighed fewer units or a less favorable product mix. The company cautions that quarterly shipment timing also influences the comparison.

This is why a higher revenue headline does not prove stronger household demand. Revenue reflects the price multiplied by quantities and mix; the latter can weaken even when the total rises. Hershey's reported net income was $580.8 million, but profit also reflects factors including hedging, costs and tariff-related effects—not just the number of candy bags bought.

What does the Halloween 2026 spending forecast actually say?

The National Retail Federation's 2026 survey estimates Americans intend to spend $13.5 billion across Halloween categories, versus $13.1 billion in 2025. It projects $4.1 billion for candy specifically and says 96% of shoppers plan to buy sweets. The survey of 7,889 U.S. consumers was conducted September 1–9, before the holiday. These are intentions and estimates, not verified October 31 receipts.

The apparent tension is important: aggregate holiday spending can set a nominal-dollar record while shoppers reduce quantities, select smaller packages or switch stores. The NRF says 31% of surveyed consumers would comparison-shop if prices exceeded expectations, and 26% would look for coupons or sales. That is evidence of budget sensitivity, not proof that every retailer or every candy brand is losing customers.

Why are candy shoppers feeling the squeeze?

Independent Reuters reporting on October 29 described Hershey's concern about household pressure from living costs, interest rates and gasoline. The company lowered its full-year organic net-sales-growth expectation to approximately 3%, even as it improved its adjusted earnings-growth outlook. These are separate measures; the stronger earnings outlook should not be mistaken for a forecast of stronger candy demand.

Hershey's results also show that the business is broader than Halloween chocolate. The company has salty-snack operations and acquisitions that affect its consolidated revenue. Comparing its entire third quarter directly with the NRF's candy-only holiday forecast would therefore be misleading.

What can U.S. shoppers do before buying last-minute candy?

For a practical price comparison, divide the shelf price by ounces or pieces rather than assuming a larger-looking bag is better value. Check whether a discount requires a loyalty account, and compare unit prices across packages and stores. A promotion may save money, but the company did not announce a nationwide Halloween candy price cut in its earnings statement.

Consumers should also distinguish an early-season survey from actual store availability. The NRF found that 49% of consumers expected to start Halloween shopping by September or earlier, so local stock and last-minute deals can vary widely. No nationwide October 31 price or guaranteed discount can be inferred from the company results.

What happens next for Hershey and candy prices?

Hershey's updated 2026 outlook calls for approximately 5% reported net sales growth and roughly 3% organic sales growth, according to the October 29 release. Those are management forecasts, subject to demand, costs and other changes. The company's next financial reporting will provide a clearer view of holiday-season sales, while the NRF's $13.5 billion figure should remain labeled a forecast until post-holiday data are available.
