Indiana Beats Michigan 13–6 in OT: First Big House Win Since 1967

No. 6 Indiana ended a nearly six-decade wait for a victory at Michigan Stadium with a defense-led 13–6 overtime win.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · October 25, 2026 · Independently written from credited reporting · Representative editorial photograph

Representative editorial photograph, not an image of the reported event or people. Photo: Wendy Wei / Pexels (free-use license).

Quick answer: Josh Hoover's overtime touchdown and a relentless Indiana defense secured the Hoosiers' first win in Ann Arbor since 1967.

ANN ARBOR, Michigan — No. 6 Indiana defeated Michigan 13–6 in overtime on Saturday, October 24, 2026. The result was significant beyond the scoreboard: Indiana's athletics department confirmed that it was the Hoosiers' first win at Michigan Stadium since 1967.

Indiana vs Michigan: How the overtime winner unfolded

The teams entered overtime tied at 6–6 after a game dominated by defenses and field goals. Indiana quarterback Josh Hoover completed a 21-yard pass to tight end Brock Schott, setting up his own four-yard touchdown run. The extra point made it 13–6. Michigan had a chance to respond but failed to complete its fourth-down play, ending the game.

Hoover's late touchdown stood out because the Indiana passing attack had struggled for much of the afternoon. Indiana's official report credits him with 81 passing yards on eight completions from 18 attempts and one interception. The final possession was therefore a timely change of pace rather than the continuation of an explosive offensive performance.

Indiana's defense controlled the result

Indiana registered four sacks and 10 tackles for loss while holding Michigan to 224 total yards, according to the Hoosiers' official recap. Defensive pressure repeatedly interrupted Michigan drives, including possessions that ended in missed field goals. Linebacker Rolijah Hardy recorded two sacks; defensive lineman Tobi Osunsanmi added two more.

Turnovers also mattered. Indiana recovered an early Michigan fumble and later intercepted a pass. Those takeaways helped set up Nico Radicic field goals, giving the visitors a 6–0 halftime lead. Michigan kicked field goals in the third and fourth quarters but never reached the end zone.

Why the historic win matters

The Hoosiers improved to 7–1 overall and 4–1 in Big Ten play, while Michigan fell to 4–3 and 1–3 in the conference. Indiana was coming off a loss to Ohio State, so the ability to win an ugly road game offered a different kind of test from a high-scoring performance.

For Michigan, the immediate concern is finishing drives. Its offense had opportunities to turn close field position into touchdowns but settled for two field goals. For Indiana, the defense provided enough margin for one effective overtime series to decide the contest. Michigan's official schedule listed a trip to Rutgers on October 31; Indiana's next game was against Minnesota.
