Hurricanes Beat Bruins 2-1: Aho's Third-Period Goal Ends Boston Streak

Carolina outshot Boston 30-15 and protected a one-goal lead through two late penalties.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · October 28, 2026 · Independently written from credited sources · Representative editorial photograph

Representative licensed photograph, not the reported event or people. Photo: Tima Miroshnichenko / Pexels (Pexels free-use license).

At a glance: Carolina beat Boston 2-1 on Oct. 27, 2026. Sebastian Aho scored the winner, Pyotr Kochetkov won his eighth straight start, and the Bruins' streak ended.

Hurricanes vs. Bruins: final score

The Carolina Hurricanes defeated the Boston Bruins 2-1 at Lenovo Center in Raleigh on Tuesday, October 27, 2026. Sebastian Aho supplied the decisive goal at 10:19 of the third period, and Carolina held on through a tense closing stretch. The result ended Boston's eight-game point streak while extending Carolina's own point streak to four.

The score was close, but the shot totals tell another part of the story: Carolina put 30 shots on goal to Boston's 15. Hurricanes goaltender Pyotr Kochetkov stopped 14 of 15, earning his eighth consecutive win to begin the season. Boston's Jeremy Swayman made 28 saves and kept his team in contention despite the disparity in chances.

How the goals unfolded

Boston struck first on a power play only 1:31 into the opening period. Pavel Zacha finished a passing sequence involving David Pastrnak and Elias Lindholm to put the Bruins ahead 1-0. That start offered Boston an opportunity to dictate the game, but the Hurricanes gradually generated more of the play.

Jackson Blake tied it with 52 seconds remaining in the first period after Nikolaj Ehlers won the puck below the goal line. The second period produced no scoring, leaving both teams to settle the game in the third. Aho's go-ahead finish came on a rush, with William Carrier and Andrei Svechnikov involved in the buildup. The NHL's official recap provides the scoring sequence and team comments.

Why Aho's goal held up

Taking a lead with nearly half a period remaining is not the same as protecting it. Carolina still had to survive two minor penalties in the final 4:41, which gave Boston opportunities to create pressure with the extra skater. The Hurricanes' penalty-killing group prevented the equalizer.

That late defense mattered because a 2-1 game can turn on a single rebound or broken assignment. Carolina's earlier shot advantage helped explain its control, but it was the disciplined finish that converted control into two standings points. Kochetkov's save total was modest because Boston did not put many pucks on target, not because the result was automatic.

What the numbers say about each team

The Hurricanes improved to 10-2-2; the Bruins dropped to 9-3-1. Boston had earned at least a point in each of its previous eight games, going 7-0-1 over that stretch. The loss ended that run, but it does not erase the team's strong start.

Swayman's 28 saves illustrate the work Boston's goalie had to do. Conversely, Carolina's 30-15 shot edge suggests a larger gap in sustained offensive-zone pressure than the one-goal margin alone conveys. These are game-level observations rather than claims about how either club will perform for the remainder of the season.

What to watch next

Boston's response will hinge on whether it can generate more shots at even strength while maintaining its early-game special-teams execution. Carolina will try to preserve the balance of offensive pressure and late-game defending that decided this matchup. The independent box score corroborates the final result and goal sequence.
