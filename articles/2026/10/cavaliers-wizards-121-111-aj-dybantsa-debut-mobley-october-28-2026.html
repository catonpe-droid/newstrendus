Cavaliers Beat Wizards 121–111: AJ Dybantsa Scores 19 in NBA Debut

The No. 1 draft pick finally made his NBA debut, but Cleveland's second-half response and Evan Mobley's outside shooting decided the result.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · October 29, 2026 · Independently written from credited sources · Representative editorial photograph

At a glance: Cleveland rallied past Washington 121–111 on October 28, 2026. AJ Dybantsa scored 19 in his NBA debut, while Evan Mobley led the Cavaliers with 24.

Cavaliers vs. Wizards score and debut facts

October 29, 2026 — The Cleveland Cavaliers defeated the Washington Wizards 121–111 on Wednesday, October 28, in a game notable for AJ Dybantsa's NBA debut. The rookie scored 19 points on 8-for-12 shooting after missing Washington's first four games with a sprained ankle. NBA.com confirmed that the No. 1 overall pick started and recorded two rebounds and two assists.

Dybantsa's first-quarter nine points offered an immediate glimpse of his scoring ability. Yet the official game recap shows why the outcome cannot be reduced to a rookie's debut: Cleveland erased a double-digit deficit and closed more effectively, while Washington struggled to match the Cavaliers' late scoring.

How did Cleveland rally from behind?

Washington scored 42 points in the first quarter and led 69–59 at halftime. The Wizards reached a 13-point advantage early in the third quarter, but Cleveland matched their 23 points in that period while cutting the gap, then won the fourth 29–19. The final 10-point margin therefore hides a game that Washington controlled for long stretches.

The Associated Press account published on NBA.com says Cleveland trailed 105–104 before scoring nine consecutive points. Peyton Watson's late layup, set up by James Harden, pushed the margin to 117–107 with 2:24 remaining. Cleveland's late run mattered more than any single highlight: it combined defensive stops with efficient half-court possessions.

Who led the Cavaliers?

Evan Mobley scored 24 points and grabbed 12 rebounds, making five three-pointers in a career-best long-range performance, according to the official Cavaliers box score and AP recap. Watson added 22 points and six rebounds; Donovan Mitchell scored 19. That distribution gave Cleveland options even as the Wizards focused on its leading scorers.

Washington had other contributors besides Dybantsa. Anthony Davis and Alex Sarr scored 18 apiece, and Bilal Coulibaly added 17. Kyshawn George recorded 11 assists. The Wizards also held an advantage in points in the paint, but Cleveland's timely outside shooting and fourth-quarter execution proved more decisive than the raw interior-scoring total.

Why does Dybantsa's debut matter?

His 19 points were the most by a Wizards rookie in his first NBA game since Tom Gugliotta scored 20 in November 1992, NBA.com reported. The milestone should be read with care: one efficient debut is a positive sign, but it is not a full measure of how a player will handle a long professional season.

For Washington, the next questions involve how Dybantsa fits beside established starters and how his workload develops after the ankle issue that delayed his first appearance. For Cleveland, the victory extended its winning streak to three and highlighted the value of multiple late-game scoring threats. Fans following the rookie can use the league's official box score to separate confirmed statistics from speculative projections.
