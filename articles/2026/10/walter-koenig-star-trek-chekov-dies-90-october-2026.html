Walter Koenig Dies at 90: Star Trek's Chekov Remembered by George Takei

The actor's representative confirmed his death at age 90. Former Star Trek colleague George Takei joined fans in remembering his decades of work.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · October 26, 2026 · Independently written from credited sources · Representative editorial photograph

Representative editorial photograph, not the reported event or people. Photo: A. S. / Pexels (Pexels free-use license).

Quick answer: Walter Koenig, known for playing Pavel Chekov in the original Star Trek series and its films, died at age 90. His representative and multiple news organizations confirmed the news on October 26.

LOS ANGELES, Oct. 26, 2026 — Walter Koenig, the actor who portrayed Pavel Chekov in the original Star Trek television series, has died at 90. His representative confirmed the death in statements reported Monday, and former co-star George Takei shared a public tribute. Koenig's death prompted renewed interest in the role that made him a recognizable figure in science-fiction television.

What has been confirmed about Walter Koenig's death?

Koenig died at his home in the North Hollywood area of Los Angeles, according to reports citing his talent representative. His daughter and niece were with him. Reports differ on whether the date should be described as Sunday, October 25, or Monday, October 26; the family's and representative's confirmation was publicly reported on October 26. Rather than repeating unverified social-media claims, this account relies on the representative's statement and reporting by established news organizations.

Reuters and the Associated Press described Koenig's long association with the original Star Trek cast. Reports citing his representative described natural causes or old age. No speculation about private medical details is necessary to understand the public news.

Why did Chekov matter to the original Star Trek?

Koenig joined Star Trek in its second season in 1967 as Ensign Pavel Chekov, the young Russian navigator aboard the USS Enterprise. The series, which aired during the Cold War, imagined an international crew working together on a shared mission. That premise gave the Chekov character a significance beyond his humorous exchanges and distinctive accent.

He appeared in 36 episodes of the original television run and later reprised the role across seven Star Trek films. Those appearances helped connect the original series to generations of movie audiences long after the television show's first broadcast ended in 1969.

What did George Takei say about his longtime colleague?

George Takei, who played Hikaru Sulu, remembered Koenig as more than the character fans knew. In a public tribute reported by the Associated Press and Entertainment Weekly, Takei emphasized their friendship and Koenig's work as a writer and teacher. The two performers remained associated with the franchise through appearances, conventions and reunions.

Koenig's representative also highlighted the actor's warmth toward fans. His convention appearances were part of the enduring culture surrounding the original series, where cast members often spoke directly with people who discovered the show decades after it first aired.

Walter Koenig's career beyond Star Trek

Koenig had a substantial career outside the Enterprise. Science-fiction viewers also knew him as Alfred Bester on Babylon 5, a character very different from the earnest Chekov. He worked as a screenwriter, author and occasional director, extending his creative career well beyond his best-known on-screen role.

His later appearances and writing reflected the difficulty and opportunity of building a career around an iconic television character. Although many headlines identify him simply as Chekov, colleagues and longtime viewers have pointed to the wider body of work he produced over several decades.
