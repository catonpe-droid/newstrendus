Anthropic IPO 2026: ANT Stock Ticker, SEC Filing and Date

Anthropic has filed for an IPO, but shares are not trading yet. Here's what the SEC filing confirms about the ANT ticker, potential timing and risks.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · October 26, 2026 · Independently written from credited sources · Representative editorial photograph

Quick answer (updated October 30): Anthropic publicly filed Form S-1 on October 26, 2026 and applied for Nasdaq ticker ANT. Its IPO offering price and confirmed first trading date are not stated in the preliminary prospectus. Read the original SEC filing.

UNITED STATES, Oct. 26, 2026 — Anthropic, the company behind the Claude AI assistant, published a registration statement for a proposed initial public offering with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission. The preliminary filing identifies ANT as the intended Nasdaq trading symbol, but it does not mean that shares are available to trade today.

Anthropic IPO date and ANT stock: what is confirmed?

The company's official announcement says it filed an S-1 registration statement with the SEC. The filing is preliminary, and the offering remains subject to regulatory review and market conditions. Neither the final share price nor the number of shares to be sold has been determined in the public material available at publication.

That distinction matters for searches such as “Anthropic stock price” or “ANT IPO date.” A proposed ticker is not a live quote. Investors should not confuse an S-1 filing with an effective registration statement or the start of public trading.

Anthropic revenue growth versus the reported net loss

According to the SEC filing, Anthropic reported approximately $16.3 billion in revenue for the first six months of 2026, compared with about $1.2 billion in the year-earlier period. The same document reported a net loss of roughly $80.7 billion. Those numbers require careful reading together rather than treating the net loss as ordinary operating cash burn.

Reporting on the filing indicates that most of the headline loss was tied to a noncash fair-value remeasurement of convertible financing instruments. Its operating loss for the half-year was far smaller, about $693 million. Prospective investors should distinguish accounting valuation effects, operating expenses and actual cash commitments.

Why AI infrastructure and governance are central

Training and serving advanced AI models requires substantial computing capacity. Anthropic's filing describes extensive long-term infrastructure commitments, making access to chips, electricity and data-center capacity important to the company's economics. A rapid rise in customer demand does not eliminate the financial risk of supporting that demand.

The document also describes an unusual governance structure involving the company's founders and a trust with significant board influence. Readers assessing the proposed shares should review voting rights and the risk factors rather than assuming that all public shareholders will have the same influence over corporate decisions.

What to watch before Anthropic stock can trade

The next concrete milestones are amendments to the prospectus, a proposed price range, a final offering price and an official listing date. Those details can change. As of this October 30 update, readers should not confuse a proposed stock ticker with an actively trading share. The SEC filing index is the primary source for any amendments. Anthropic IPO frequently asked questions

Is ANT stock available to buy now?

The October 26 preliminary filing does not establish that ANT shares are already trading. Check Nasdaq and the SEC for a confirmed listing before treating a quoted price as public-market data.

When will Anthropic start trading?

No official first trading day or final IPO price was provided in the preliminary S-1. A possible market window discussed in news coverage is not a confirmed listing date.

What ticker did Anthropic request?

The company applied to list its Class A common stock on the Nasdaq Global Select Market with the proposed symbol ANT, according to its SEC filing.
