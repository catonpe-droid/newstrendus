Anthropic IPO: $70–$78 Price Range, ANT Nasdaq Listing

Anthropic's Nov. 17 SEC amendment lists a proposed $70–$78 IPO range for 825 million shares and confirms Nasdaq approval for ticker ANT. A price range and listing approval do not mean shares are already trading.

Quick answer (updated November 18): The November 17 SEC S-1/A amendment estimates an IPO price of $70–$78 per share for 825 million Class A shares and states that Nasdaq has approved listing under ANT. These are proposed offering terms, not a live ANT stock quote or a final IPO price.

UNITED STATES, Oct. 26, 2026 — Anthropic, the company behind the Claude AI assistant, published a registration statement for a proposed initial public offering with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission. The preliminary filing identifies ANT as the intended Nasdaq trading symbol, but it does not mean that shares are available to trade today.

November 17 filing: the IPO terms in brief

  • Estimated price range: $70–$78 for each Class A share, subject to final pricing.
  • Shares in the offering: 825 million Class A shares, according to the amended SEC prospectus.
  • Nasdaq ticker: ANT; the November 17 filing says the exchange approved the listing.
  • What is not a live quote: An indicative IPO range. Confirm the registration status, final price and first trading session through official channels.

These details come directly from the SEC's November 17 S-1/A. Pricing and trading status can change after publication.

Anthropic IPO date and ANT stock: what is confirmed?

The company's October 26 filing announcement explained its original S-1. An amended SEC prospectus dated November 17 now states an estimated $70.00–$78.00 offer price for 825 million Class A shares and Nasdaq approval for ANT. The prospectus is still marked subject to completion; an estimated range is not the final price.

That distinction matters for searches such as “Anthropic stock price” or “ANT IPO date.” A proposed ticker is not a live quote. Investors should not confuse an S-1 filing with an effective registration statement or the start of public trading.

Anthropic revenue growth versus the reported net loss

According to the SEC filing, Anthropic reported approximately $16.3 billion in revenue for the first six months of 2026, compared with about $1.2 billion in the year-earlier period. The same document reported a net loss of roughly $80.7 billion. Those numbers require careful reading together rather than treating the net loss as ordinary operating cash burn.

Reporting on the filing indicates that most of the headline loss was tied to a noncash fair-value remeasurement of convertible financing instruments. Its operating loss for the half-year was far smaller, about $693 million. Prospective investors should distinguish accounting valuation effects, operating expenses and actual cash commitments.

Why AI infrastructure and governance are central

Training and serving advanced AI models requires substantial computing capacity. Anthropic's filing describes extensive long-term infrastructure commitments, making access to chips, electricity and data-center capacity important to the company's economics. A rapid rise in customer demand does not eliminate the financial risk of supporting that demand.

The document also describes an unusual governance structure involving the company's founders and a trust with significant board influence. Readers assessing the proposed shares should review voting rights and the risk factors rather than assuming that all public shareholders will have the same influence over corporate decisions.

What to watch before Anthropic stock can trade

The November 17 S-1/A already gives an indicative $70–$78 range and confirms Nasdaq listing approval for the ticker ANT. The final offer price, effectiveness of the registration statement and actual start of public trading still require confirmation. See the latest cited SEC amendment, the company's official IPO information and Nasdaq market notices rather than relying on an unverified social-media stock quote. Anthropic IPO frequently asked questions

Is ANT stock available to buy now?

The SEC's November 17 preliminary prospectus states there has not yet been a public trading market for Class A shares. Listing approval is not the same as the first market trade; verify the official Nasdaq listing status before attempting to buy.

When will Anthropic start trading?

The company has a planned IPO process, but the November 17 preliminary prospectus does not itself establish the first executed trade. Check the final prospectus, Nasdaq notices and company investor information for the confirmed first trading date.

What ticker did Anthropic request?

The November 17 SEC amendment states Nasdaq has approved listing of the Class A common stock under ANT. Approval does not establish that public trading has begun.
