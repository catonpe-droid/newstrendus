Texas Beats Ole Miss 34–27: Final Score, Comeback and Key Stats

The Longhorns protected their home field after a dramatic second-half push by Ole Miss. Here are the score, key performances and what the result means.

Quick answer: The Longhorns protected their home field after a dramatic second-half push by Ole Miss. Here are the score, key performances and what the result means.

AUSTIN, Texas — Texas defeated Ole Miss 34–27 on Saturday, October 24, after building a commanding first-half lead and surviving a furious Rebels comeback. The Longhorns led 24–3 at halftime, but the visitors recovered to level the contest at 24 early in the fourth quarter. Texas responded with the points needed to finish on top at Darrell K Royal-Texas Memorial Stadium.

Texas vs Ole Miss: How the game changed

Texas controlled much of the opening half, scoring 10 points in the first quarter and another 14 before the break. Ole Miss managed only a field goal during that stretch. The second half was far less comfortable for the home crowd: the Rebels produced 14 unanswered third-quarter points, then kept the pressure on as the fourth quarter began.

The final quarter delivered 10 points for each side, but that symmetry does not capture the momentum swings. Ole Miss had done the difficult work of erasing a large deficit, only for Texas to find a way back in front and protect its advantage. The official game summaries from both athletic departments confirm the final 34–27 score.

Arch Manning, Raleek Brown and the defining numbers

Texas quarterback Arch Manning threw for 212 yards, while Raleek Brown gave the Longhorns 157 rushing yards. Receiver Ryan Wingo posted 111 receiving yards. Those production totals show how Texas balanced its attack between the passing game and the ground.

For Ole Miss, Trinidad Chambliss passed for 250 yards and two touchdowns. Deuce Alexander had 87 receiving yards. The Rebels defense also made game-changing plays: Jayln Crawford recorded two interceptions as Ole Miss forced three turnovers. That defensive resistance helped turn what looked like a one-sided halftime game into a tense finish.

Why this SEC result matters

Both schools emerged from the contest with 5–2 overall records, according to their official game summaries. Texas improved to 2–2 in Southeastern Conference play, while Ole Miss stood at 3–2. In a competitive SEC schedule, this result matters beyond one Saturday because head-to-head games shape teams' conference outlooks and the conversation around the postseason.

For Texas, the encouraging point was its response after allowing the comeback. For Ole Miss, the lesson was how expensive an early 21-point deficit can become, even with impressive second-half adjustments. The comeback deserves recognition, but it did not change the outcome.
