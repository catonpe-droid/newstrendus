Brewers Enter World Series Game 3 Up 2–0 on Rays After Comeback Win

The Milwaukee Brewers traveled to St. Petersburg with a 2–0 lead over the Tampa Bay Rays in the 2026 World Series, following two close home victories and a dramatic Game 2 comeback.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · October 26, 2026 · Independently written from credited sources · Representative editorial photograph

At a glance: Milwaukee won Game 1 by 3–2 and Game 2 by 5–4. Game 3 was scheduled for October 26 at Tropicana Field; this report does not assert an unverified Game 3 final.

World Series Game 3: Milwaukee's 2–0 advantage

ST. PETERSBURG, Fla., Oct. 26, 2026 — The Brewers arrived at Tropicana Field needing two more wins to capture the championship, while Tampa Bay was looking for its first victory of the series. Milwaukee took Game 1 on October 23 by 3–2 and Game 2 on October 24 by 5–4. The official MLB postseason schedule listed Game 3 for Monday, October 26, in Florida.

At the time this article was prepared, the third game's final result was not independently verified. Readers seeking the latest inning-by-inning score should use MLB's live scoreboard rather than treating the 2–0 series lead as a claim about the Game 3 outcome.

How the Brewers escaped Game 2

Milwaukee trailed 4–0 early in Saturday's Game 2 before rallying to win 5–4. The Rays struck first in the opening inning, including a two-run homer by Liam Hicks. Milwaukee began cutting into the deficit immediately, then used Sal Frelick's sixth-inning solo home run to tie the game at four.

Cooper Pratt, whose early error had contributed to Tampa Bay's first-inning damage, delivered the go-ahead RBI single in the seventh. That turnaround made Pratt a central figure in the comeback. Associated Press reporting and Reuters' game account describe the rally.

Why Milwaukee's bullpen matters

After the difficult opening inning in Game 2, the Brewers' relievers held Tampa Bay scoreless for the remainder of the game. Aaron Ashby finished the contest by striking out Yandy Díaz, preserving the one-run lead. That ability to protect a slim advantage can shape how managers deploy pitchers in later games.

It is also a warning against overreading the series margin. The Rays were within one run in both games, and Game 2 began with Tampa Bay ahead by four. A 2–0 lead is important, but it is not the same as a dominant run differential.

What Tampa Bay must do at home

Moving the series to St. Petersburg gives the Rays a change of venue and an opportunity to avoid a three-game deficit. They have already demonstrated the ability to score early against Milwaukee. The question is whether Tampa Bay can maintain pressure after the opening innings rather than allowing the Brewers to work back into games.

Milwaukee, meanwhile, can draw confidence from two different types of wins: a tight opener and a four-run comeback. The World Series game log confirms the first two results. Future games and pitching plans should be checked against official MLB updates.
