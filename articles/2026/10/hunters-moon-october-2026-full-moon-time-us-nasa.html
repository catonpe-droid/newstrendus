Hunter's Moon October 2026: Full Moon Time in the US and What to Watch Next

The full Moon peaked just after midnight in the eastern United States, while West Coast viewers saw the exact moment on Sunday evening.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · October 26, 2026 · Independently written from credited sources · Representative editorial photograph

Quick answer: The 2026 Hunter's Moon reached its full phase at approximately 04:12 UTC on October 26, or 12:12 a.m. Eastern time. It remains striking on nearby evenings.

UNITED STATES, Oct. 26, 2026 — October's Hunter's Moon reached its full phase at about 12:12 a.m. EDT Monday, October 26, according to NASA's sky-event calendar and astronomy guides. That moment fell late Sunday evening for viewers in western US time zones, but the Moon will look nearly full on the nights around the event.

What time was the Hunter's Moon full in each US time zone?

The astronomical full Moon occurs at one instant worldwide. NASA's Sky Events Calendar lists October 26 at approximately 04:12 UTC. That corresponds to 12:12 a.m. Eastern Daylight Time on October 26, 11:12 p.m. Central on October 25, 10:12 p.m. Mountain on October 25 and 9:12 p.m. Pacific on October 25. Some astronomical listings round the instant to 04:11 UTC, a one-minute difference that does not change when people should look up.

The Moon does not suddenly brighten or dim at the exact full-phase minute. To the naked eye, it appears round for a period before and after the peak. Moonrise and moonset are local events, so viewers should check their city's horizon times rather than assume the full-phase timestamp is also moonrise.

Why is October's full Moon called the Hunter's Moon?

The Hunter's Moon is the traditional name for the full Moon that follows the Harvest Moon, which is the full Moon closest to the September equinox. The naming tradition is associated with the autumn season and the historically useful light available after harvest. It is a seasonal label, not a separate kind of Moon or an unusual astronomical mechanism.

Different cultures have their own names and stories for autumn full Moons. The scientific explanation for the phase is the same everywhere: Earth is positioned so that the Moon's sunlit hemisphere faces us almost directly.

Why does the full Moon look orange and unusually large?

A Moon near the horizon can appear yellow or orange because its reflected sunlight travels through more of Earth's atmosphere, which scatters shorter blue wavelengths. It can also seem larger when compared with buildings, trees or the horizon. Astronomers call that apparent size effect the Moon illusion; it is not evidence that the Moon has suddenly moved much closer.

For a good view, choose a place with an unobstructed eastern horizon around moonrise, and allow time for the sky to darken. No telescope is needed to enjoy the sight. Binoculars can reveal broad lunar features, though high-contrast crater shadows are often easier to see at other phases.

What is the next skywatching event after the Hunter's Moon?

NASA's October 2026 skywatching guide points to the Moon passing near the Pleiades star cluster on the nights of October 27–28. The cluster is also called the Seven Sisters. The Moon's glare can make faint stars harder to see, but a clear eastern sky and binoculars may help reveal the grouping.

The event is a line-of-sight appearance, not a physical encounter: the Moon is much closer to Earth than the distant stars. Conditions vary with clouds, city lights and the observer's location.

Is the October 2026 Hunter's Moon a supermoon?

Several astronomy guides distinguish this October full Moon from a supermoon. The popular term has no single official astronomical threshold, so headlines using it should be checked carefully. The reliably confirmed fact is the October 26 full-phase time, not a dramatic change in the Moon's physical size.
