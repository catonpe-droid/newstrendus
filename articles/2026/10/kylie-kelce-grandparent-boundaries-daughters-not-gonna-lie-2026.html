Kylie Kelce Explains Grandparent Boundaries and Why Her Daughters' Privacy Matters

The podcast host's comments were about specific family choices, not a public feud with grandparents. Her broader message focused on consent, safety and giving children room to grow up privately.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · October 27, 2026 · Independently written from credited sources · Representative editorial photograph

At a glance: Kylie Kelce described asking relatives not to kiss her young daughter's face, head or hands and said both sides of the family respected the request. She has also explained why she avoids posting identifiable photos of her children.

What Kylie Kelce said about grandparents and boundaries

PHILADELPHIA, Oct. 27, 2026 — Kylie Kelce has shared a straightforward example of how she and husband Jason Kelce handled family boundaries after becoming parents: asking grandparents and other relatives not to kiss their baby's face, head or hands. In a bonus discussion associated with her Not Gonna Lie podcast, she said the family members involved respected the request. Her remarks, described in People's October 27 reporting, do not support speculation about a dispute between the couple and their parents.

Why the rules were stricter when their first daughter was born

Kelce recalled taking additional precautions when their oldest daughter, Wyatt, was an infant during the COVID-19 period. Travel and close contact were more sensitive decisions then. That context matters: her examples describe the family's experience at a particular time, not a universal medical rule for every household. Families with newborns should follow their pediatrician's advice rather than treating a celebrity podcast as medical guidance.

Did the grandparents push back?

According to Kelce's account, both sets of grandparents accepted the boundaries. She expressed appreciation for relatives who understood that parents set the terms for their own children. The distinction is important because social-media discussion can turn an ordinary parenting preference into a story about conflict. There is no verified evidence in the cited comments that the grandparents objected or that the Kelces have an ongoing family rift.

How her approach to children's photos has changed

Kelce has separately discussed limiting identifiable photographs of her daughters online. She explained in an appearance on The Squeeze, hosted by Tay Lautner, that her family's higher public profile changed how she thinks about posting images. Rather than making a permanent public record of childhood moments, she wants her children to have more control over what becomes part of their online identities. That is a privacy choice, not evidence that the children are absent from family life or public events.

What parents can take from the conversation

The useful takeaway is the process: discuss expectations early, explain them clearly and allow relatives to help without assuming they must agree on every detail. In the Kelces' case, Kelce said the request was honored, making the situation less dramatic than some online headlines suggest. Decisions about photos, physical affection and travel may evolve as children grow. The podcast conversation is a personal account, not a claim that every family should use identical boundaries.
