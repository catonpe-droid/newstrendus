AO3 Down: Archive of Our Own Warns Restoration Could Take 2–4 Weeks

AO3's operator says its October 29 outage could last two to four weeks. What the nonprofit confirmed, what remains unknown and where to track service updates.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · October 29, 2026 (U.S. Eastern) · Independently written from credited sources · Representative editorial photograph

Representative licensed photograph, not a photo of the reported event or actual subject. Photo: panumas nikhomkhai / Pexels (Pexels free-use license).

At a glance: AO3's operator says its October 29 outage could last two to four weeks. What the nonprofit confirmed, what remains unknown and where to track service updates.

What happened to Archive of Our Own?

Archive of Our Own (AO3), the nonprofit-run fanfiction archive, was offline on Thursday, October 29, 2026. Its operator, the Organization for Transformative Works (OTW), first reported the interruption at 07:13 UTC and then identified maintenance at 09:03 UTC. The official status dashboard still showed a major outage in the latest incident information available for this report. The interruption affected readers seeking access to fan-written stories, personal bookmarks and author pages.

When will AO3 be back online?

The operator initially expected maintenance to take at least 12 hours. At 17:41 UTC on October 29, it changed its estimate substantially: restoration would likely take around two weeks and could stretch to four weeks. OTW said a limited or degraded version of the archive might return sooner, but warned that further interruptions could be necessary during recovery. There is no guaranteed restoration date. A partial return would not necessarily mean all features were fully functioning.

What caused the outage?

The public incident notices cited here did not identify a verified technical root cause. It would be inaccurate to describe the interruption as a confirmed cyberattack or to assert that fanworks were permanently lost. Readers should distinguish the known facts—maintenance, a major outage and a revised recovery estimate—from speculation about servers, software vulnerabilities or data integrity. An independent Engadget report on October 29 also documented the dramatic change in the expected duration.

How can users check the current AO3 status?

Check the OTW service-status page for the incident named “AO3 Down for Maintenance,” and read the newest timestamped update. Screenshots and search snippets may show earlier estimates that are no longer valid. The dashboard separately reports the status of other OTW projects, so a problem with AO3 does not automatically mean every related site is unavailable. Third-party outage reports can illustrate users' experiences but should not replace the operator's own restoration notices.

What should readers and authors do meanwhile?

Readers can consult copies of stories they previously downloaded legitimately. Authors can keep their drafts and backups in storage they control, rather than relying on any one publishing platform. Avoid unofficial recovery services asking for passwords, two-factor codes or other account credentials. A prolonged outage affects both recreational reading and the community's access to years of writing, collections and references. Future return dates should be treated as confirmed only when OTW announces them. This article reflects the October 29 updates and can become outdated as restoration progresses.
