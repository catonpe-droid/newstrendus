AO3 Is Back Online: What Works and What Is Still Limited

AO3 resumed limited service after November 19 maintenance. Check official status, disabled features, draft deadlines and account safety.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · October 29, 2026 (U.S. Eastern) · Independently written from credited sources · Representative editorial photograph

This is a source-linked desk explainer, not first-hand reporting. Research and drafting may use AI-assisted tools. Follow the cited source links and read our editorial and corrections policy.

At a glance: AO3 resumed limited service after November 19 maintenance. Check official status, disabled features, draft deadlines and account safety.

November 20 update: AO3 returned after another maintenance window

Latest operator update: The Organization for Transformative Works posted at 23:47 UTC on November 19 that AO3 was back up following preventive maintenance announced at 11:27 UTC that day. OTW said icons, importing works from external sites and non-HTML downloads remain disabled, and high traffic can still produce a waiting or downtime screen. Its live status dashboard may continue to display an active maintenance incident, so readers should check the timestamp of the latest operator message rather than assume every service is fully restored.

What to do if AO3 still looks down: Refresh after a short wait if you see OTW’s traffic-management page; avoid repeatedly changing passwords or using unofficial mirror sites solely because of that screen. If you have a saved draft, note OTW’s announced November 29 resumption of normal deletion for drafts at least 30 days old, and save or publish important work before that date. These are OTW-reported service conditions, not an independently measured uptime guarantee. AP independently reported the earlier partial restoration after the October security incident.

Is AO3 back online now?

Yes, but service is limited. The Organization for Transformative Works (OTW) announced on November 15, 2026 that Archive of Our Own (AO3) had returned after the October 29 shutdown. Its official service-status page has displayed maintenance or degraded-service indicators during the recovery. Visitors may encounter a waiting page or slow loading as traffic is managed. This is a partial restoration, not an assurance that every feature works or that further interruptions are impossible.

Which AO3 features are temporarily unavailable?

OTW says user and collection icons are temporarily hidden, external work importing is unavailable, and work downloads are currently limited to HTML. The organization says icons have not been deleted and that the disabled features will return, but it has not announced a verified date for restoring each one. Reading and other available archive functions may be affected by traffic controls. Readers should check the live official status before assuming a particular feature is available.

Are AO3 drafts safe? What happens November 29?

OTW says no works, bookmarks, kudos or other archive data were deleted during the shutdown. It also paused automatic draft deletion, so drafts were not deleted while AO3 was offline. The organization says this pause continues for two weeks after the November 15 return, with ordinary draft deletion scheduled to resume November 29, 2026. Once normal deletion resumes, drafts that are 30 days old can be deleted under the usual rule. Authors should log in when possible and save or publish drafts they want to keep; the date shown on an individual draft may still reflect its original 30-day expiry rather than the temporary pause.

What is known about the October security incident?

AO3 went offline October 29 after OTW found evidence of a security incident. The organization says it does not currently believe its user database was stolen, but it has not ruled out that possibility. The database may include usernames, email addresses, IP addresses and hashed passwords. As a precaution, users should change passwords on other services where they reused their AO3 password, and avoid suspicious recovery links or messages requesting credentials. OTW says donor data is held in a separate system. These cautions do not establish that any particular user's account was compromised. Read OTW's original security statement.

What should readers and writers do next?

First, visit AO3's official website rather than an unofficial mirror. If the site is slow, wait and retry; the operator says it is deliberately managing heavy demand. If you are an author, check saved drafts and keep a separate personal backup, especially before November 29. If you need a work download, HTML is the currently announced format. Watch the OTW status dashboard for feature restoration and changes to service availability. Engadget's November 15 report independently confirmed the limited reopening; AP also reported the return and its restrictions.

Timeline: shutdown to partial reopening

October 29: AO3 went offline and OTW reported a security incident. October 30: OTW estimated restoration could take two to four weeks. November 6: the operator said recovery work was proceeding and reiterated its estimate. November 15: AO3 returned with degraded service and temporarily disabled features. November 29: OTW plans to resume its ordinary draft-deletion schedule. Dates describe the operator's announced milestones; the live status page remains authoritative if plans change.
