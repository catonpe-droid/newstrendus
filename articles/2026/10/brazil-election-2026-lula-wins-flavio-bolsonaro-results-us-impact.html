Brazil Election 2026: Lula Wins 51.79% as Flávio Bolsonaro Challenges Result

Brazil's election authority confirmed a narrow runoff victory for President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, while his challenger disputed the result.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · October 26, 2026 · Independently written from credited sources · Representative editorial photograph

Quick answer: Brazil's Superior Electoral Court reported Lula winning 64,256,232 votes (51.79%) to Flávio Bolsonaro's 59,826,041 (48.21%) in the October 25 runoff.

BRASÍLIA, Oct. 26, 2026 — President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva won Brazil's October 25 presidential runoff with 51.79% of valid votes, according to the country's Superior Electoral Court (TSE). His opponent, Senator Flávio Bolsonaro, received 48.21% and has disputed the outcome.

Brazil election 2026: official Lula–Bolsonaro vote totals

The TSE's October 26 announcement lists 64,256,232 votes for Lula and 59,826,041 for Flávio Bolsonaro. Those totals correspond to 51.79% and 48.21% of valid ballots respectively, a margin of roughly 4.43 million votes.

The election gives Lula a fourth nonconsecutive presidential term. His comeback followed a first round in which Bolsonaro had led the vote count. The final tally is an official election result; it should not be confused with unofficial exit polls or social-media projections.

Why is Flávio Bolsonaro contesting the result?

Bolsonaro has refused to concede and has raised objections to a late judicial decision extending voting hours by two hours in response to transportation and weather problems. Reuters and the Associated Press reported the dispute, including Bolsonaro's demand that the result be annulled.

The objections are political and legal claims, not evidence that the announced count has been overturned. The TSE publicly declared Lula reelected. Any subsequent court action should be assessed on its own evidence and official outcome rather than treated as a reversal before a ruling.

What Lula's victory could mean for the United States

Brazil is Latin America's largest economy, and its relationship with Washington affects trade, agriculture, energy and access to critical minerals. The result is closely watched in the United States because Lula and President Donald Trump have differed over tariffs, regional diplomacy and Brazil's economic ties with China.

Both governments nevertheless have reasons to maintain working relations. U.S. businesses have interests in Brazilian markets and supply chains, while Brazil seeks investment and improved access for its exports. An election outcome alone does not determine the terms of future negotiations; trade decisions require separate policy actions.

What challenges face Lula after the election?

Lula enters another term facing questions about inflation, living costs, public finances and a conservative-leaning Congress. A close result also means a large share of the electorate backed a different political direction. Coalition building and legislative negotiations are likely to shape how much of his agenda can be implemented.

His victory also matters for regional diplomacy. Brazil's positions on climate, deforestation, trade and multilateral forums such as BRICS may continue to differ from some U.S. priorities. Those differences do not rule out cooperation in areas where interests overlap.

When will the next Brazilian presidential term begin?

Reporting from the Associated Press places the start of the new term in early January 2027. Until then, the immediate story is the certified vote tally, the opposition's response and the practical steps Brazil's institutions take during the transition.
