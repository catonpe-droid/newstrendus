Alabama Beats Texas A&M 31–20: Fourth-Quarter Rally, Score and Stats

The Crimson Tide overturned a three-point deficit entering the fourth quarter and finished with a 14–0 closing-period advantage.

TUSCALOOSA, Alabama — No. 13 Alabama beat Texas A&M 31–20 on Saturday, October 24, using a powerful fourth quarter to turn a 20–17 deficit into a comfortable final margin. It was an important response from the Crimson Tide after consecutive defeats. Official Alabama records show the hosts scoring 14 unanswered points in the fourth period while preventing the Aggies from adding to their total.

Alabama vs Texas A&M: The fourth quarter decided it

Texas A&M put together a competitive first three quarters. The Aggies entered the fourth ahead by three, a position that made the last 15 minutes especially important. Alabama's defense then cut off the visitors' offensive opportunities and supplied its own attack with better field position.

The official Alabama account says the Aggies gained just 15 yards during the closing quarter and finished all three fourth-quarter possessions with three-and-outs. Alabama scored two touchdowns in the period, making the final 31–20 result more comfortable than the late-game situation initially suggested.

Keelon Russell and Rico Scott powered the response

Quarterback Keelon Russell passed for 311 yards and three touchdowns, according to game coverage and the box score. Receiver Rico Scott was a decisive target, collecting 156 receiving yards on six catches. Those numbers reflect the biggest difference between the offenses when the Tide needed to take control.

Scott's production was particularly important because Texas A&M had been able to stay ahead into the final quarter. Alabama did not simply protect a lead; it had to create one. Russell's timing and Scott's ability to gain yards after the catch helped turn drives into points.

Turnovers and defensive pressure shaped the contest

The Aggies struggled to protect the ball, including multiple early interceptions by quarterback Marcel Reed. Reed finished with 163 passing yards, according to the official box score. Alabama's defense scored on an interception return during the first quarter, evidence that the hosts were creating chances even when their offense was not in control.

A&M's early resilience should not be ignored. It still found a way to lead entering the final period, and that made the collapse especially costly. The deciding contrast was between a team able to deliver late and one unable to sustain a fourth-quarter drive.

What the outcome changes

Alabama finished the game 6–2 overall and 4–2 in Southeastern Conference play, according to the university. Texas A&M stood at 4–4 overall and 1–4 in the SEC. Those records help explain why a late rally mattered to the Tide: it stemmed a two-game losing streak and kept the team competitive in its conference season.
