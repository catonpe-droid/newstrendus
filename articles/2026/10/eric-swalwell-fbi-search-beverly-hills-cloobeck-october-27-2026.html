FBI Searches Beverly Hills Home Tied to Eric Swalwell Inquiry: What Is Confirmed

Federal agents executed a warrant at a property associated with a former Swalwell supporter. The search is not a finding of guilt.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · October 27, 2026 · Independently written from credited sources · Representative editorial photograph

Representative licensed photograph, not the reported event or people. Photo: Zion Smith / Pexels (Pexels free-use license).

At a glance: The FBI searched a Beverly Hills property owned by Stephen Cloobeck on October 27, 2026. What authorities confirmed and what remains sealed in the Swalwell inquiry.

BEVERLY HILLS, Calif., Oct. 27, 2026 — Federal agents executed a search warrant at a Beverly Hills property owned by developer Stephen Cloobeck, according to local reporting and an Associated Press account connecting the operation to an investigation involving former U.S. Rep. Eric Swalwell. The FBI confirmed that a search occurred, while the underlying warrant documents remained sealed.

Where did the search take place?

ABC7 Los Angeles reported that law enforcement activity began around 7 a.m. Tuesday on Roxbury Drive near Benedict Canyon Drive. The station said the property belongs to Cloobeck, a wealthy developer and former political supporter of Swalwell. The Associated Press reported that Swalwell had spent time at the home earlier in 2026.

The address and private details of people associated with the property are not necessary to explain the public development. A search of a residence associated with a person does not mean every person who has visited or stayed there is suspected of wrongdoing.

What have federal authorities confirmed?

The FBI acknowledged serving a warrant, according to ABC7, but the documents were sealed and officials did not publicly provide a detailed inventory of seized items or the precise legal theory for the search. The Associated Press cited a law enforcement official and other people familiar with the investigation when describing its connection to Swalwell.

That distinction matters: a confirmed search is a fact; the full scope of the investigation, what agents expected to find and whether any evidence will lead to charges were not established in the publicly available material reviewed on October 27. Rumors about particular devices or records should not be presented as verified results of this search.

How is Eric Swalwell connected to the property?

Swalwell resigned from Congress in April amid sexual-misconduct allegations, which he has denied. The AP reported that Cloobeck had been a donor and that Swalwell had stayed at the property before the resignation. Cloobeck later publicly said he had severed ties with the former congressman.

The search comes after earlier investigative activity reported in August, including the seizure of electronic devices and a search of Swalwell's Washington residence. Those earlier reports provide context but do not establish what investigators sought at the Beverly Hills property on Tuesday.

Does a search warrant mean someone has been charged?

No. A search warrant permits investigators to search a specified place or property under judicial authorization. It is not a conviction, a finding that allegations are true, or proof that the owner of the property committed a crime. A charge, if one is ever filed, would be a separate public legal development.

This report does not infer guilt from law-enforcement activity. It also does not treat allegations as adjudicated facts. The status of any potential criminal proceeding must be checked against official filings and on-the-record statements rather than speculation.

What happens next?

Further clarity may come if the warrant or supporting materials are unsealed, prosecutors make a public statement, or court records disclose a later proceeding. Until then, the safest account is narrow: a search took place, the location and its ownership were reported, and the full legal basis remains unavailable to the public.

Readers should watch for official filings and corrections to initial reports. This article reflects information available on October 27 and will require updating if investigators release new facts.
