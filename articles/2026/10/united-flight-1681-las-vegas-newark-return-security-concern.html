United Flight 1681 Returns to Las Vegas: What Police, FAA and Airline Confirmed

A Las Vegas-to-Newark flight returned safely after its crew reported a potential security concern. Authorities say a passenger was arrested, but several circulating details remain unverified.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · October 26, 2026 · Independently written from credited sources · Representative editorial photograph

Representative editorial photograph, not the reported event, people or companies. Photo: Drinu Cutajar / Pexels (Pexels free-use license).

At a glance: United Flight 1681 returned to Harry Reid International Airport late October 24 with 150 passengers aboard. The FAA said it would investigate; no injuries were reported.

Why did United Flight 1681 return to Las Vegas?

LAS VEGAS, Oct. 26, 2026 — United Airlines Flight 1681, scheduled from Harry Reid International Airport to Newark Liberty International Airport, returned to Las Vegas on the night of Saturday, October 24, after its crew reported a passenger disturbance. United described the reason as a potential security concern, according to local reporting that cited the airline and FAA.

The aircraft landed safely around 11:05 p.m. local time. The flight had 150 passengers and six crew members, according to local reporting, and officials reported no injuries. The return was a precautionary response to a reported concern, not evidence that an attempted hijacking occurred.

What did Las Vegas police say about the arrest?

In Associated Press reporting, Las Vegas police said they detained a 25-year-old passenger following a report that he had made a terroristic threat. Police said he was arrested on an allegation of making a threat or providing false information concerning an act of terrorism. The reported allegation is not a conviction, and the individual is entitled to the presumption of innocence.

Some online accounts describe a message seen on a passenger's phone. The available official statements cited by AP did not fully establish those details, so NewsTrendus is not treating a purported text message as a proven fact. Investigators and court proceedings, not viral retellings, will determine what happened.

What did the FAA and United Airlines confirm?

The Federal Aviation Administration said the crew reported a passenger disturbance and that the agency would investigate. United confirmed that the flight returned safely and that the passengers were delayed overnight. The airline said the flight departed for Newark on Sunday morning after the interruption.

There was no reported injury among passengers or crew. The safe landing and subsequent departure should not be conflated with an FAA conclusion about the incident; the agency's investigation and any criminal case are separate processes.

How were passengers affected?

Flight-tracking data cited by AP indicated the aircraft turned back less than an hour after departure. A return to the origin airport can lead to new security checks, crew-duty limits and rebooking decisions. In this case, United said the overnight delay was followed by a Sunday departure. The airline, rather than an unverified social-media post, is the appropriate source for any individual passenger's reimbursement or itinerary.

Passengers seeking information about a specific reservation should use the airline's official customer-support channels and retain their ticket and disruption records. The public reporting reviewed does not establish a universal cash-compensation amount for everyone on the flight.

What is still unknown?

The publicly available accounts do not resolve the precise wording of the alleged threat, the ultimate outcome of the criminal case or the FAA's final findings. A reported initial court appearance was expected Tuesday, October 27, but that should not be described as a completed proceeding before it happens.

This report distinguishes the airline's safety response, the FAA's investigation and police allegations. It will be updated when authorities release confirmed findings.
