Blazers Beat Lakers 136-129: Dončić Scores 41, Portland Wins Late

Los Angeles got a 41-point night from Luka Dončić. Portland answered with several scorers and a stronger finish.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · October 28, 2026 · Independently written from credited sources · Representative editorial photograph

Representative licensed photograph, not the reported event or people. Photo: Reinis Brūzītis / Pexels (Pexels free-use license).

At a glance: Trail Blazers beat Lakers 136-129 on Oct. 27, 2026. Dončić scored 41, but Portland's balanced scoring and late baskets decided the game.

What was the final Lakers vs. Trail Blazers score?

The Portland Trail Blazers defeated the Los Angeles Lakers 136-129 on Tuesday, October 27, 2026, at Crypto.com Arena in Los Angeles. The seven-point margin obscures how much work Portland had to do to survive a 41-point performance from Luka Dončić. It was a high-scoring game decided by late execution rather than one team's inability to create offense.

Portland's win moved the team to 3-0, according to the postgame account from Blazer's Edge. The Lakers had enough offense to stay in contention but could not get the final stops they needed. The league's official game page provides the game identifier and box-score destination; the independent recap supplies the late-game sequence.

How did Portland overcome Dončić's 41 points?

Portland spread the offense across multiple contributors. Ja Morant and Deni Avdija scored 23 points apiece, Jrue Holiday added 22 and Damian Lillard scored 21, according to Blazer's Edge. The four-player balance meant Los Angeles could not focus its defense on a single Portland scorer without leaving another threat available.

Dončić set the tone early with 16 first-quarter points. His final total of 41 was the game's headline individual performance, but scoring volume alone did not determine the result. Austin Reaves supplied 26 for Los Angeles, giving the Lakers a second major offensive option. Portland's late shot-making proved more decisive than the largest individual total.

The closing possessions that mattered

Portland's advantage held in a frantic finish. Blazer's Edge reported that a Damian Lillard basket with 43 seconds remaining helped seal the victory after the Blazers generated timely perimeter offense. That sequence matters because the Lakers were close enough to make every possession significant; Portland needed points and defensive discipline, not simply a large earlier lead.

The contest also had interruptions from foul trouble and unusual late officiating calls. Those details should not be mistaken for a single-cause explanation. A more useful reading is that Portland received contributions from its backcourt and wings throughout the game and converted enough opportunities when the margin narrowed.

What the result means for both teams

For Portland, an unbeaten start provides evidence of early offensive versatility, but three games are far too small a sample to establish a season-long trajectory. A team that can get 20-plus points from four players is harder to defend on a single night; whether that distribution persists will depend on health, opponent adjustments and shooting efficiency.

For the Lakers, Dončić's scoring ability was not the question on Tuesday. The immediate review is how to limit late baskets and support the primary scorer with more consistent defensive possessions. The 136 points conceded are a better place to begin that assessment than the box-score-leading 41.

When do the Trail Blazers play next?

The reported next Portland game is against the Memphis Grizzlies on Thursday, October 29. Fans should consult the official Trail Blazers schedule for updated tip times and broadcast information. This report covers the completed October 27 game, not a live score feed.
