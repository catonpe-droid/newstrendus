Jeffery Simmons to Bears: Titans Confirm Trade Terms Including Two First-Round Picks

Tennessee's official announcement clarifies the exact draft-pick exchange and the remaining condition before the NFL trade is completed.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · October 29, 2026 · Independently written from credited sources · Representative editorial photograph

Representative licensed photograph, not a photo of the reported event or the exact product or person. Photo: SAULO LEITE / Pexels (Pexels free-use license).

At a glance: The Titans agreed October 29 to send Jeffery Simmons and a 2028 fifth-round pick to Chicago for two firsts and a 2027 fourth, pending a physical.

The Tennessee Titans agreed on October 29, 2026 to trade defensive tackle Jeffery Simmons to the Chicago Bears, with the transaction pending a physical, according to the Titans' official team announcement. The price is substantial: Chicago is giving up two future first-round draft picks and a fourth-round selection while receiving Simmons and a later fifth-round pick.

What are the exact Jeffery Simmons trade terms?

Tennessee says it will receive the Bears' 2027 first-round pick, 2027 fourth-round pick and 2028 first-round pick. Chicago will receive Simmons and Tennessee's 2028 fifth-round pick. The agreement was announced Thursday morning, and the team explicitly noted that the deal is pending a physical.

That qualifier is important: an announced agreement is not the same as an unconditional completed transaction. The reported compensation is not simply “two firsts”; the extra fourth-round and fifth-round picks are part of the exchange.

Why are the Bears investing so heavily in Simmons?

Simmons is a 29-year-old interior defensive lineman who can disrupt both running plays and passing pockets. According to independent reporting, he recorded four sacks and 38 tackles over seven games in 2026 after a career-best 11 sacks in 2025. He was selected by Tennessee with the No. 19 pick in the 2019 NFL Draft.

For Chicago, the trade signals an effort to strengthen the defensive front immediately rather than wait for future draft picks to develop. The cost is a reduction in first-round flexibility in two consecutive drafts. Whether that trade-off proves worthwhile will depend on health, performance, roster fit and Chicago's results over the next several seasons.

What did Tennessee say about the departure?

In the official announcement, Titans controlling owner Amy Adams Strunk emphasized Simmons' leadership and work in the community, including his foundation's impact in Tennessee and Mississippi. The team also noted that he was a four-time Pro Bowl selection and a five-time captain during his Titans tenure.

The move represents more than a personnel change: Tennessee is exchanging an established star for draft capital that can support a longer-term roster rebuild. Those selections are future opportunities, not guaranteed starting-caliber players.

How does his contract affect the deal?

Reuters reported that Simmons agreed to a three-year extension earlier in 2026 worth $105.8 million, including $100 million guaranteed. Contract figures help explain the size of the commitment Chicago is taking on, although the full salary-cap treatment and any restructuring should be checked against official team or league filings rather than assumed from headline contract totals.

What happens next for Bears and Titans fans?

The immediate milestone is confirmation that the physical and league transaction process are complete. After that, fans can watch for Chicago's defensive deployment plans and Tennessee's approach to the future picks. Until the transaction is finalized, the precise status should be described as an agreed trade pending a physical, not a completed on-field debut.
