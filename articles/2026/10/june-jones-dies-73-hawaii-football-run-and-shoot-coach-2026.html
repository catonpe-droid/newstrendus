June Jones Dies at 73: How Hawaii's Run-and-Shoot Coach Changed Football

University of Hawaii confirms June Jones died October 29 at 73. His 76–41 record, unbeaten 2007 regular season, NFL coaching career and passing-game legacy.

What has the University of Hawaii confirmed?

June Jones died October 29, 2026, in Oregon at age 73, according to the University of Hawaiʻi's official memorial. The university remembered its former head football coach as a transformative figure who influenced generations of players. Its statement did not specify a medical cause of death. The school said information about memorial arrangements would be announced later, so unverified dates circulating elsewhere should not be treated as confirmed.

How did Jones transform Hawaii football?

Jones took over a Hawaii team that had finished 0–12 the previous season. In 1999, his first team went 9–4 and won the Oʻahu Bowl, an extraordinary one-year turnaround. Over nine seasons at Hawaii he compiled a 76–41 record, reached six bowl games and won two Western Athletic Conference titles. The improvement made Hawaii a nationally recognized football program and helped bring sustained attention to games played late in the evening for many mainland U.S. viewers.

What happened during Hawaii's famous 2007 season?

The 2007 Rainbow Warriors went 12–0 during the regular season and reached the Sugar Bowl. Georgia then defeated Hawaii 41–10. That distinction matters: the team had an unbeaten regular season, not an unbeaten full season. Quarterback Colt Brennan became a Heisman Trophy finalist, and the team's appearance in a major Bowl Championship Series game was a landmark for the university. The success remains one of the clearest examples of Jones' offensive approach reaching a national stage.

Why did the run-and-shoot offense matter?

The run-and-shoot is a passing system that emphasizes receiver spacing and route adjustments based on defensive coverage. Its innovation was not simply calling more passes: quarterbacks and receivers needed to interpret defenses together as plays unfolded. Jones helped bring versions of the approach to professional and college football. Quarterbacks Timmy Chang and Colt Brennan became especially associated with his Hawaii teams. The Associated Press obituary traces the system's influence.

Where else did Jones coach, and how is he remembered?

Before his Hawaii tenure, Jones coached the Atlanta Falcons in the NFL and briefly served as interim head coach of the San Diego Chargers. He later coached Southern Methodist University, helping it return to bowl competition. Hawaii's current coach Timmy Chang, who played for Jones, remembered him as a mentor who continued supporting him when he entered coaching. The university's tributes from public officials reflected the coach's cultural impact beyond the field. Official Hawaii Athletics channels are the best source for any future memorial announcements.
