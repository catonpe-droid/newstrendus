Elke Sommer Dies at 85: The Prize, A Shot in the Dark and a Hollywood Career

A familiar face in 1960s Hollywood, Sommer moved between international cinema, American comedy and a second creative life as a painter.

At a glance: Elke Sommer, the German-born star of A Shot in the Dark and The Prize, died at 85 in Los Angeles. Her film career, Golden Globe and later painting work.

Elke Sommer, the German-born actor who became a prominent Hollywood presence in the 1960s, died at age 85 in Los Angeles. The Associated Press reported on October 29, 2026, that her stepson told German media she had died at home the previous day. Sommer was especially associated with The Prize and the Peter Sellers comedy A Shot in the Dark.

What has been reported about Elke Sommer's death?

Sommer's stepson Sebastian Walther told the German publication Bild that she died at her Bel Air home. The Los Angeles Police Department confirmed to the Associated Press that officers responded to a death in the neighborhood on Wednesday night. Reporting from The Guardian attributed heart failure as the cause to Bild; that detail is not independently established by the police response.

The available accounts support her death and age but do not provide a full medical history. Coverage of her career is therefore more informative than speculation about circumstances not publicly documented.

Which Elke Sommer movies made her famous in America?

Sommer appeared opposite Paul Newman in The Prize (1963), a suspense story set around a Nobel Prize ceremony. That performance brought her wider recognition in the United States and a Golden Globe for promising newcomer.

She played Maria Gambrelli in A Shot in the Dark (1964), the Peter Sellers-led film associated with the Pink Panther series. The movie became one of her best-known English-language performances, combining a murder mystery with the absurd humor of Inspector Clouseau.

Her other screen credits included The Art of Love, Boy, Did I Get a Wrong Number! and And Then There Were None. The range of roles illustrates how she moved between Hollywood comedies, thrillers and international productions rather than working in one narrow genre.

How did she reach Hollywood?

Born Elke Schletz in Berlin in 1940, Sommer spent part of her childhood in Bavaria during and after World War II. According to published biographies and obituaries, she initially considered work involving languages before a beauty-contest appearance in Italy led to film opportunities.

She built early credits in European productions, then moved into English-language cinema as international studios sought multilingual performers. Her career reflected a period when European actors frequently crossed between continental filmmaking and Hollywood.

What did she do beyond acting?

Later in life, Sommer devoted more attention to painting. Her visual art became an important part of her public identity after her peak period of film stardom. She continued to be remembered by classic-cinema audiences for her screen presence and comic timing.

For viewers discovering her work now, The Prize and A Shot in the Dark provide two different introductions: one showcases her place in a Hollywood thriller, while the other captures the comic persona that made her recognizable to generations of film fans.

Why does her career matter?

Sommer's career is a reminder of how international performers helped shape mid-century American entertainment. She was not defined solely by one role or one studio system; she moved across languages, genres and artistic mediums, leaving a filmography that remains accessible to classic-movie audiences.
