US Grand Prix 2026: Antonelli Takes Pole Ahead of Verstappen — Starting Grid and Title Stakes

Mercedes driver Kimi Antonelli has put himself in the best possible starting position at Austin. Here's what Saturday's qualifying established, who lines up behind him and what remains to be decided on Sunday.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · Published October 25, 2026 · Before Sunday's race · 5-minute read

Representative racing photograph, not from the reported 2026 Austin event. Photo: Dzmitry Charnou / Pexels (free-use licence).

At a glance: Antonelli qualified first in 1:34.035, with Max Verstappen second and Isack Hadjar third. The United States Grand Prix is scheduled for Sunday, October 25, at 3 p.m. local time in Austin (4 p.m. Eastern), according to Formula 1's official schedule.

AUSTIN, Texas — The 2026 United States Grand Prix has a significant championship storyline before the lights even go out. Mercedes' Kimi Antonelli took pole position at Circuit of The Americas on Saturday, October 24, with a 1-minute, 34.035-second lap. Red Bull's Max Verstappen qualified second, 0.179 seconds behind, while Verstappen's teammate Isack Hadjar finished third. The results are confirmed by Formula 1's official qualifying report and Reuters reporting.

Qualifying settles the starting order at the front, not the result of the race. Drivers still have to manage the start, tire choices, pit strategy and the demands of the Austin circuit. The weekend's biggest unresolved question is whether Antonelli can convert a pole into a win and, potentially, a championship.

The closeness of the front rows matters: just 0.232 seconds separated Antonelli from fourth-place Leclerc. Red Bull also has two cars among the leading three, potentially increasing strategic pressure on Mercedes. That suggests an intriguing opening phase, but qualifying gaps alone do not predict race pace.

Why Antonelli's pole could reshape the title fight

Antonelli arrived at the race with a substantial lead in the drivers' standings and a first mathematical opportunity to seal the 2026 championship. Winning at Austin would be necessary for that scenario, but not sufficient on its own: the finishing positions of other title contenders would also matter. Sky Sports explains the title permutations in detail.

For viewers, the distinction is important. A headline suggesting Antonelli has already won the title would be premature. Saturday delivered a pole position, while championship confirmation depends on Sunday's classified result and the points table after the race.

What could decide the race at Circuit of The Americas?

The early contest between Antonelli and Verstappen will attract attention, especially with Hadjar directly behind the leaders and Ferrari occupying fourth and fifth. On a circuit with elevation changes and different types of corners, positioning through the opening sequence can affect the race, but teams still have several strategic decisions to make.

Track limits also mattered in qualifying: the official report notes that Norris had a lap deleted, leaving him eighth. That is a reminder that small errors can change the order even when the underlying pace is competitive. Sunday's result may turn on a combination of clean driving, tire management, pit timing and how teams react to one another rather than the front-row order alone.

What time is the 2026 US Grand Prix?

Formula 1 lists the main race for Sunday, October 25, at 20:00 UTC. That converts to 3 p.m. Central Daylight Time in Austin, 4 p.m. Eastern and 1 p.m. Pacific. Check the official F1 event page for any late schedule or grid changes before the start.
