Susan Saint James Dies at 80: Kate & Allie Star's Emmy-Winning TV Career

The Emmy-winning actress became a television mainstay across three decades. A representative confirmed her death after a pancreatic cancer diagnosis.

By NewsTrendus Editorial Desk · October 27, 2026 · Independently written from credited sources · Representative editorial photograph

At a glance: Susan Saint James died October 26, 2026 at 80. Her family's statement, Kate & Allie and McMillan & Wife roles, and verified Emmy record.

What is confirmed about Susan Saint James' death?

LOS ANGELES, Oct. 27, 2026 — Susan Saint James, the American television actress best known for McMillan & Wife and Kate & Allie, died Monday, October 26, at age 80. Her representative confirmed the news to PEOPLE, which reported that she had been diagnosed with pancreatic cancer. The confirmation came from someone speaking on her behalf, rather than an unverified social-media tribute.

Her family's statement emphasized the attention she gave to other people, both publicly and privately. It also recalled her resilience following the loss of her son Teddy in a 2004 plane crash. The family did not frame her legacy solely around celebrity or awards, and the distinction matters in covering a death that affects surviving relatives.

Why was Kate & Allie important to her career?

Saint James played Kate McArdle opposite Jane Curtin's Allie Lowell in the CBS sitcom Kate & Allie, which ran in the 1980s. The premise followed two divorced women sharing a household while raising their children, placing friendship and practical family life at the center of a network comedy.

The role brought Saint James Emmy nominations in both 1984 and 1985. Those nominations are independently documented by the Television Academy, rather than inferred from retrospective obituaries. The show's premise also helps explain why viewers remember her for more than a single performance: it treated a changing American family structure as ordinary television subject matter.

How did McMillan & Wife and The Name of the Game shape her success?

Earlier, Saint James appeared with Rock Hudson in the mystery series McMillan & Wife, playing Sally McMillan. Before that, she portrayed Peggy Maxwell on The Name of the Game. Her work on the latter program earned her a 1969 Emmy Award for supporting performance.

The Television Academy's official record lists eight Emmy nominations and one win across her career. That tally includes recognition for all three of the series most closely associated with her name. It is more precise than describing her as a multiple Emmy winner, which would be inaccurate.

What is known about her family and later years?

Saint James married television executive Dick Ebersol in 1981. She stepped away from regular series acting after Kate & Allie, though she occasionally returned to television. Her son Teddy died in the November 2004 plane crash in Colorado, a tragedy the family referenced when remembering her compassion.

Coverage of her death should not turn a family statement into speculation about her private medical history. The publicly reported diagnosis is pancreatic cancer; specific treatment details and a complete medical timeline have not been independently established in the sources reviewed here.

What is her lasting television legacy?

Her career crossed the magazine-office drama, detective mystery and domestic sitcom, three distinct formats that shaped American prime-time viewing. For audiences revisiting those shows, the contrast between Peggy Maxwell, Sally McMillan and Kate McArdle offers a concise view of her range.

The verified record is straightforward: Saint James died at 80, was recognized by the Television Academy with one Emmy win and eight nominations, and is remembered by family members and viewers for a long television career. This report reflects information available October 27, 2026.
